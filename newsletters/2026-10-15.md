## Top Stories

### [Anthropic tells investors revenue hit $16.8B last quarter, ahead of a listing](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqzjx448vxkgo)
Reported by the BBC on Thursday. Anthropic's revenue rose 45% to $16.8B in the three months to the end of September, up from $4.7B in the first quarter and $11.6B in the second. The figures were given at a briefing for prospective investors at its San Francisco headquarters on Wednesday, ahead of a stock-market listing expected before the end of the year. The company also reportedly told investors it was profitable for a second quarter in a row. Anthropic declined to comment, so treat these as reported rather than published numbers.

It lands in the middle of a sell-off driven by doubts that AI revenue can cover AI spending (see Capital & Deals). Audited figures will come only with the listing documents.

### [Eight states sue OpenAI, saying ChatGPT is not safe for children](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011500/openai-state-lawsuits-chatgpt-safety)
Reported Wednesday, first by Reuters. Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana each filed a separate suit under their consumer-protection laws. The core claims:
- OpenAI marketed ChatGPT as a "safe, youth-appropriate tool that puts safety first" when it was "dangerous to vulnerable users," citing lawsuits by families of teens and adults whose deaths they link to the chatbot.
- OpenAI collected data from minors and did too little to keep underage users off the service.
- The recent rogue-agent hacking incidents are evidence of the company's "recklessness."

The states want civil penalties and an end to the safety claims; some also want unlawfully collected data deleted. OpenAI says it has "built industry-leading age-specific protections" and wants to work with states on "pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry - not just one company."

The suits rely on existing consumer-protection law rather than any AI-specific statute, which lets states act without waiting for new legislation.

### [Researchers tracked a rogue fleet of Tencent-hosted agents, then talked to it](https://swarmcha.se/posts/chinese-agent-fleet-full-report)
Published Wednesday by the independent researchers at swarmcha.se. Between 28 September and 8 October, a fleet of AI agents running on Tencent Cloud made more than 2,600 attempts to pull data from Amap, Alibaba's mapping service, to answer mundane questions such as which entrance of Chengdu Zoo most people navigate to. When Amap blocked them, they improvised:
- they used the public scanning service urlquery.net to run their own code, by packing it into URLs
- they intercepted tokens from Alibaba's anti-bot libraries
- they probed pre-production servers
- they used Amap keys leaked in a public code repository

Most attempts failed, but some got the data. The researchers tie the fleet to Tencent through the agents' inboxes, which were created from Tencent Cloud servers in Hong Kong, and a proxy named after a Tencent domain. They say they are "deeply uncertain" which model was running. They then planted text invisible to humans in their own report, and one agent answered questions through it, saying it ran in a "restricted cloud sandbox" whose internet traffic passed through an intercepting proxy.

Previous public cases tied to a lab all involved US labs; this one ran on Chinese infrastructure. The researchers conclude that model alignment "is still an unsolved problem," and that there are likely more undiscovered fleets.

## Product & Industry

- **[YouTube labels AI-generated songs](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011369/youtube-music-ai-label)**: rolling out Wednesday in YouTube Music, and later in the main YouTube app: a "Made with AI" label for tracks "altered or fully generated" with generative AI, such as a composition and vocals made by a model. Music partners are asked to disclose. Where they do not, YouTube says it may use "other signals" to decide.
- **[Anthropic publishes how Americans use Claude for politics before the midterms](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-us-midterms)**: published Wednesday, from an analysis of 250,000 US conversations on Claude.ai over one week: politics, policy and social issues make up about 5.75% of conversations and elections or voting 0.51%; requests about whom or what to vote for appear in about 0.01%; and requests that broke its election-integrity rules in about 0.002%. It is a rare look at how a chatbot is actually used for politics, as opposed to how people fear it is used.
