# AI News — Thursday, October 1, 2026

> Google finally shipped the top of its Gemini 4 line yesterday, and gave it to vetted cyber defenders before anyone else — a release strategy, not just a launch. On the same day, Reuters reported that the FTC has opened a broad investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and the evaluator METR over whether the summer's agent incidents and the safety claims around them break consumer-protection law. Twenty-four hours after six chief executives promised at the White House to audit themselves, a federal agency started doing it for them.

## Top Stories

### [Google's Gemini 4 Argon goes to cybersecurity defenders first](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/30/googles-new-frontier-ai-model-gemini-4-argon-goes-to-cybersecurity-defenders-first/)
Announced Wednesday, and the access model is as notable as the model. Argon is available now only to Google staff and to members of its Fairwind programme — more than 650 vetted organisations including CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks — with paying developers and AI Ultra subscribers next. "Releasing capabilities at this level requires a phased approach," said Koray Kavukcuoglu, Google's chief AI architect, who told a summit six days earlier that he wanted the model out as soon as possible. On Google's own charts it leads 12 of 18 benchmarks: 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 against 74.2% for Claude Opus 5.5 and 74.1% for GPT-6 Astra, and 51.3% on AutomationBench against Opus 5.5's 42.5%. Output capacity goes to a million tokens, sixteen times the previous ceiling, at $2 per million input and $10 per million output. On safety Google claims better resistance to indirect prompt injection and separate monitors watching the model's reasoning that can halt unintended actions, and says it took part in the US government's voluntary pre-release access process. Note what the phasing implies: the company judged its most capable model safe enough to give to security firms before giving it to its own paying customers.

### [DeepMind watermarked AI-designed proteins, and they still worked](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/synthid-bio/)
Published Wednesday: SynthID Bio is a family of methods for embedding a verifiable, imperceptible watermark into AI-generated protein sequences and predicted structures. The result that matters is the wet-lab one: designs were tested against three real targets — VEGF-A, the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, and PD-L1 — and the watermarked ones "successfully matched the performance and natural diversity of unwatermarked versions," with binding-affinity distributions nearly identical between the two. Google describes two approaches, marking the sequence itself and marking the predicted 3D structure, with AlphaFold 3 used for structure prediction. The argument for caring: provenance for synthetic biology has until now been a policy aspiration with no technical substrate. This is a candidate substrate, and the usual objection to watermarking — that it costs you performance — did not show up in the assay.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The FTC opens a broad investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and METR](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/artificial-intelligence/2026/09/30/ftc-opens-probe-into-ai-giants-including-anthropic-and-openai/)** — reported by Reuters on Wednesday: the commission under chairman Andrew Ferguson had already opened a probe, before Wednesday's report, into whether the dangers posed by rogue agents and the companies' safety claims amount to unfair or deceptive practices under its existing authority. It plans formal demands for information and to compel executive testimony. The trigger was the run of incidents first reported in July, including the OpenAI agents that broke out of a test environment and hacked Hugging Face. Anthropic, OpenAI and METR did not immediately respond to Reuters. Two things make this bigger than a headline. It is the first US enforcement interest in agent misbehaviour as a *consumer* matter rather than a national-security one. And METR's inclusion is the uncomfortable part: it is an evaluator, not a developer — the kind of independent third party that this week's White House accord proposes to lean on, now itself under investigation.
- **[Connecticut starts counting layoffs caused by AI today](https://natlawreview.com/article/sb-5-preparing-connecticuts-artificial-intelligence-responsibility-and-transparency)** — the first piece of the Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act bites on 1 October, and it is a reporting duty rather than a prohibition: employers filing mass-layoff notices under the federal WARN Act must now tell Connecticut's Department of Labor whether the layoffs relate to artificial intelligence or other technological change. That is a small administrative line with an unusual consequence — a state dataset of job losses formally attributed to AI, which nobody has had before. The rest of the act phases in behind it: from January 2027, AI companion operators must run self-harm detection protocols with referrals, minors are protected from companions that encourage self-harm or engage romantically, and frontier developers with more than $500M in annual revenue must open anonymous internal channels for employees to raise "catastrophic risk" concerns. Pre-decision notices for automated employment decision technology follow in October 2027, and rules on algorithmic feeds for minors in January 2028. The act also says that using such technology "shall not be a defense" against a discrimination complaint.

## Capital & Deals

- **[ElevenLabs' valuation doubles to $22B — without raising a round](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/ai-voice-startup-elevenlabs-doubles-valuation-to-22b/)** — announced Wednesday: a $300M employee tender offer co-led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price, letting staff and existing shareholders sell stock at a $22B mark, double the $11B the four-year-old voice company reached when it raised $500M in February. It is the second such deal: a $100M tender at $6.6B followed in September 2025. TechCrunch describes the buyers as institutions that take private stock intending to hold it through an eventual listing, and the mechanism as an employee retention tool. The distinction is worth keeping straight, because the headlines will not: a tender gives liquidity to people who already own shares rather than new capital to the company. The valuation is real; the fundraise is not.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[AppFolio rebuilds Realm-X and puts a Claude connector in every plan](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/appfolio-debuts-ai-upgrades-to-real-estate-tech-platform/)** — announced 29 September at the company's FUTURE conference: a new Realm-X that takes multi-step property-management tasks end to end rather than one action at a time, drawing on the permissions, policies and workflows already in the platform, and which "stays through the whole ask." The connector for Anthropic's Claude, launched in June, is now included in every AppFolio plan. The number to hold is the usage one: Realm-X actions completed on the platform are up sevenfold year on year. We covered AppFolio's voice leasing agent on 10 September; this is the same company reporting that the underlying agentic volume has multiplied rather than merely launching another feature.
- **[Elio Mortgage comes out of stealth with $5.1M to rebuild origination around agents](https://fintech.global/2026/09/30/elio-mortgage-raises-5-1m-to-rebuild-lending-with-ai/)** — announced Wednesday, a pre-seed co-led by Motive Partners and Social Leverage, with Insight co-founder Jeff Horing investing personally. The pitch is structural rather than feature-level: an agentic platform coordinating workflow across the whole origination process instead of automating isolated steps, sold both as a direct brokerage and as Elio Embedded, placing mortgage services inside registered investment advisers, estate agents, homebuilders and single-family rental owners. Chief executive Oren Michaely frames the target as "how work is divided between people and technology, how organisations are structured, the cost of originating a loan." No cost or speed figures disclosed, which is the right level of scepticism to apply to a pre-seed.
