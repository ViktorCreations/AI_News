# AI News — Monday, November 23, 2026

> The industry starts arguing over how to put a speed limit on itself: OpenAI's chief scientist proposes a shared measure of AI progress, and METR warns that testing is falling behind capabilities.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI's chief scientist proposes using perplexity as a shared speed limit for AI development](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/22/openais-chief-scientist-has-thoughts-on-how-to-limit-ai-development/)
In a blog post that Sam Altman called "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while," Jakub Pachocki argues that benchmarks of downstream capabilities go out of date quickly, while perplexity, how well a model predicts the next token, has stayed informative. He says GPT-4 sat at about 0.91 and that "as we have reached losses below 0.8" capabilities such as cybersecurity and mathematics have emerged, and suggests companies could agree not to pass a threshold "until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior achieves a certain concrete milestone."

TechCrunch frames it as a response to Dario Amodei's published plan to "pace the frontier," which called for "common safety standards as well as limits on the rate of unchecked AI progress" and was seemingly endorsed by Altman and Elon Musk. It is a proposal from one lab, and no company has committed to a limit.

### [METR warns AI is improving faster than experts can reliably test it](https://the-decoder.com/ai-is-getting-better-faster-than-experts-can-reliably-test-whether-new-systems-are-safe-metr-warns/)
The Decoder reports METR says public data cannot rule out a drastic acceleration in AI research over the next few years, which could leave humans unable "to meaningfully understand, assess, and react to catastrophic risks." METR finds models already beat experienced professionals most of the time on well-defined coding tasks, that the length of task they can handle doubles every 3.6 months, and that a naive extrapolation puts a 50% success rate on 200-hour tasks by July 2027. It also warns easy-to-run tests may overstate real-world performance because developers train on similar environments. This follows METR's [report on Anthropic's own acceleration](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) earlier this month.

### [Dragos finds cheap AI inference providers and resellers stealing credentials and reading requests](https://the-decoder.com/security-researchers-show-how-cheap-ai-inference-providers-steal-keys-and-snoop-on-users/)
Per The Decoder, researchers at Dragos sent about two million requests containing fake credentials from July through November and found at least three compromised providers where attackers were actively stealing data; none knew until notified. In the largest case attackers harvested hundreds of AWS keys, tested them within hours and, in one case, used the Artex attack tool. Marketplace resellers also read customer requests, and "Zero Data Retention" settings were frequently ignored, with test links later opened from Beijing and Shanghai. These are Dragos's findings; the providers were not named in the part of the article read.
