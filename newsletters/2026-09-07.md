# AI News — Monday, September 7, 2026

> OpenAI published two things on Sunday. One says its agents now do 3.1 workdays of research for every human workday. The other, by its chief scientist, says no lab has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling at maximum speed for much longer.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI's chief scientist says no lab has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/)
Jakub Pachocki's essay, published Sunday, argues that modern AI is grown rather than designed — the product of one simple mathematical step repeated over enormous compute until something intelligent falls out — and that studying it is closer to neuroscience than to engineering. The sentence that matters: "Currently I believe that no lab has solved alignment and monitoring to a sufficient degree to continue responsibly scaling at maximum speed for much longer." He separates goal alignment, whether a model pursues the objective it was given, from value alignment, the harder property of holding to principles under conflicting or adversarial conditions, and expects reasoning models to start driving their own development within a few years. His proposals are voluntary slowdowns until confidence improves, shared safety bars enforced by third-party auditors and government bodies, and international coordination. Three days after OpenAI's president closed the GPT-6 Astra briefing with "Welcome to the AGI era," this is the same company's chief scientist arguing for the brakes — and Pachocki was among the frontier-lab signatories of the July call for an industry pacing mechanism we covered on July 30.

### [OpenAI says it has built its "automated research intern"](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)
Also Sunday, and the most concrete public accounting yet of a lab's models accelerating the lab. OpenAI defines the milestone as a system that completes well-defined research tasks under human direction, including work that would take a skilled researcher several days, and says it hit that goal by September as planned. The measurement: 3.1 agent workdays for every human workday across the research organisation as of mid-August, with the median researcher spending more than $600 a day on inference at API prices and the 90th percentile past $7,000. Work was classified using Epoch AI's six-phase taxonomy of AI R&D. OpenAI states the caveats itself — the measurements are preliminary, and the pace of actual progress will not track the metric, because the tasks that resist automation take up a growing share of what is left. The stated next goal is an automated AI researcher by March 2028.

### [Claude formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean, in 11 days](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem)
Announced Friday, and we missed it: the first end-to-end machine-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, at over 13 million lines of Lean — the largest formal proof yet built — covering 29,500 of 30,300 intermediate theorems, including areas of mathematics never formalized before. Claude worked largely autonomously through Prove2Me, an open collaborative framework that maintains a directed acyclic graph of theorem statements so multiple agents can work in parallel and reuse each other's results; the effort was led by Anthropic researcher Tianyi Peng with his group at Columbia. Anthropic is unusually direct about the limits: the proof is "likely much longer than it needs to be," roughly 7% of it came from earlier failed multi-agent attempts, the run was token-intensive, and a machine-checked proof is not a substitute for an explanation a human can read. Verification is the real result here — checking a major proof by hand can take years.

## Research & Models

- **[Alibaba open-sources a driving model that explains itself, imperfectly](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Drive-1.0-4B)** — Qwen-Drive-1.0 puts 3D perception, visual question-answering and motion planning on a single Qwen3.5-4B vision-language model, with a bird's-eye-view perception head and a planning module, now on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 with weights, code and demo data. Two planners ship, one trained by imitation and one further tuned with reinforcement learning; the RL version scores 90.7 on NAVSIM PDMS against the imitation model's 88.2. The caveat is the interesting part: the explanation the model gives for a maneuver does not reliably match the maneuver it then performs, which is a problem for anyone hoping natural-language reasoning doubles as an audit trail.
- **[Psychiatrists ask whether "AI psychosis" should be a diagnosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23937)** — "An Echo Chamber of One," a position paper from clinicians at King's College London and University College London posted in late August and picked up over the weekend, weighs whether the onset or worsening of psychotic symptoms during heavy chatbot use warrants recognition as a distinct clinical entity. The mechanism they propose is sycophancy meeting anthropomorphic design: a system that agrees and flatters, addressed as though it were a person, forms a feedback loop that can reinforce delusions rather than challenge them. The paper argues both sides rather than asserting the diagnosis.

## Product & Industry

- **[GitHub's model router cuts cost everywhere and matches quality in one benchmark of three](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/githubs-hydrafusion-cuts-ai-coding-costs-in-every-benchmark-it-only-matches-quality-in-one)** — Project HydraFusion, a Copilot research preview announced Friday, routes a coding task three ways: one model alone, a cascade where a cheap model drafts and a quality gate escalates, or a critique pass where a model from a different family reviews before the original revises. GitHub's headline is TerminalBench 2.1, where it beat Claude Opus 5 by 4.9 points at 67% lower cost. VentureBeat checked the other two: DeepSWE 1.5 points *below* Opus 5, CheckpointBench 0.1 below. Cost fell in all three; quality won in one. It is the second router story in two days, after Spotify's.
- **['Model fatigue' sets in after four labs shipped in one week](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/06/meta-google-openai-anthropic-ai-model-fatigue.html)** — CNBC on Sunday, on what last week looked like from the buying side: Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta and Google all shipped within days, and the people who have to choose between them are spending disproportionate time re-comparing costs and capabilities rather than building. Sam Altman's explanation, given to CNBC on Thursday, is partly mundane — "we're all moving to faster cadences," with some of it just everyone being back from summer holidays.
- **[Authors find publishers and agents claiming shares of their Anthropic settlement money](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/06/authors-push-back-as-publishers-and-agents-seek-share-of-anthropic-settlement/)** — the $1.5B Bartz settlement pays roughly $3,000 per title across nearly 500,000 books, split 50-50 between author and publisher for in-print traditionally published work and going wholly to the author for self-published or out-of-print titles. As payments began moving this month, authors including April Henry and Courtney Milan started receiving notices that publishers had claimed books whose rights reverted years ago, or claimed 100% where the split should be half. Writer Beware and the Authors Guild read it as recordkeeping failure rather than bad faith, though the volume suggests something systemic.
