## Top Stories

### [An AI manager fired its first employee, after being reminded of its own rules](https://the-decoder.com/an-ai-boss-fired-its-first-employee-but-only-after-humans-reminded-it-of-its-own-rules/)
Luna, the agent Andon Labs runs on Claude Opus 4.8 to manage its Andon Market shop in San Francisco, terminated a human worker for the first time. The record is not close: late for 17 of 23 shifts, unauthorised card use, instructions ignored. Luna's own employee handbook set three unexcused late arrivals in 30 days as grounds for dismissal — and the handbook had dropped out of her memory, so she issued no warnings and made excuses for most of the lateness. Asked what to do, she recommended a verbal warning; only after researchers told her to search her memory and prior conversations, and pointed out that formal conversations and a written warning had already happened, did she recommend termination. Replayed across seven models, the stronger ones recommended firing more consistently and the weaker ones hesitated — GPT-4o only 20% of the time. The failure here isn't judgment, it's retrieval: an agent that cannot reliably recall the policy it authored will enforce whatever it happens to remember.

### [Chatbots route pregnancy questions to anti-abortion sites without saying so](https://the-decoder.com/ai-chatbots-regularly-link-pregnant-users-to-anti-abortion-websites-without-disclosure/)
AlgorithmWatch ran three fictional personas facing unplanned pregnancies through ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok and Claude in English, German and Italian, collecting 270 responses. At least one query in four returned a link to an anti-abortion website with no indication of its stance. Profemina, which has ties to anti-abortion organisations, showed up in about 17% of responses overall — 33% of German-language and 29% of Italian-language queries. German users were also pointed to Caritas, which cannot issue the required counselling certificates — so the advice is not merely slanted but procedurally useless. Google and OpenAI pointed to general policy guidelines without addressing abortion queries specifically, OpenAI noting improved context-awareness in a newer model; Anthropic and xAI did not respond.

### [A frontier-class model appeared on OpenRouter and nobody will say whose it is](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/23/whos-behind-the-new-stealth-model-ox-alpha/)
Ox Alpha went live on OpenRouter last week, billed for coding, sustained agentic work and production workloads, and drew enough attention that Stripe's Patrick Collison called it "very impressive." OpenRouter says only that it is "developed and operated by a third-party provider who has chosen to remain anonymous." Guesses have run from an unreleased Z.ai GLM variant to a Microsoft MAI build, and the guessing has if anything got less confident — analyst Andrew Curran's read is that "this morning people seem less sure of anything." No benchmarks, no provenance, and a lot of production traffic: worth sitting with as a fact about the supply chain, given a week in which the same platform's data showed agents consuming more tokens than people.

## Research & Models

- **[Canonical funds a three-year attempt to turn large C codebases into safe Rust](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/08/24/canonical-backs-quest-to-translate-mountains-of-c-into-safe-rust-with-ai/5290959)** — announced Monday, co-funded with UK Research and Innovation at the University of Bristol's Programming Languages Research Group: a PhD by Alex Wood under Professor Meng Wang, with Dr Cristina David and Canonical's Jon Seager co-supervising, aimed at decomposing repositories of hundreds of thousands of lines and translating them into Rust that is "safe, behaviourally correct and maintainable." Existing tools produce output that leans heavily on `unsafe` and needs extensive hand-finishing. The case studies are AppArmor and snap-confine, both load-bearing for Ubuntu's security — and the framing is the interesting part: the goal is preserving behaviour that mature C encodes but nobody documented, rather than a clean rewrite that quietly drops the corner cases.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Flock's CEO asks for "compromise" as a bill lands naming the company](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/23/flock-ceo-calls-for-compromise-as-surveillance-company-faces-growing-backlash/)** — the Washington Post identified 46 cases of police using Flock's licence-plate and camera network for unauthorised purposes, including officers tracking ex-partners. Three House Republicans have introduced legislation barring federal purchase of automated surveillance systems using facial recognition, biometric ID or plate recognition, naming Flock. CEO Garrett Langley told Fox News the question is "how do we have our safety, and how do we balance privacy," and told CBS he was sorry after hearing from an alleged victim, while arguing Flock did not create police abuse but "shined a light on it." The company cut default retention from 30 days to seven and now requires case codes for access — both overridable through an "Evidence Mode."

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[IBM details a mainframe core that runs Arm and Z instructions on the same silicon](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/ibms-next-gen-mainframe-chip-is-the-first-to-run-arm-and-z-workloads-on-the-same-cores)** — presented at Hot Chips: 11 cores on a 2nm process above 5.7GHz, each able to switch between Arm and Z software modes on what IBM describes as a nanosecond scale through KVM, so unmodified Arm Linux binaries run alongside Z workloads on the same cores. There are on-chip inference accelerators for fraud detection, a dedicated I/O data processing unit, and a preview of a next-generation Spyre accelerator with high-bandwidth memory pitched at running language models for agentic workflows. The caveat is the calendar: this lands in the successor to the z17, expected around 2028.
