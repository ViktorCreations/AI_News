## Top Stories

### [Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy)
Nvidia announced on Sunday that independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate after reports that Huang was targeted by a Chinese spy operation. Alongside it, Nvidia published a message from Huang admitting he had an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based chip company Megaspeed, and apologising to his family and colleagues. A memo circulating among US policymakers earlier this year claimed she had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on him.

The board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, that Huang "fully supports the review," and that it has confidence in him and his leadership team. The allegation was first reported [on 29 October](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-spies-targeted-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-according-to-the-new-york-times/); the new facts are the board's review and Huang's admission.

### [Vals AI: a free open-weight model comes within eight points of the best restricted cyber model at breaking into networks](https://the-decoder.com/the-gap-between-open-and-closed-ai-models-in-cyber-capabilities-keeps-shrinking/)
On Vals AI's CyberOffense benchmark, which gives an agent only a public web address and no human help to work through a simulated corporate network to admin privileges, OpenAI's restricted GPT-6 Sol from its Daybreak cyber program scored best at 80.8% of objectives. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, an open-weight model anyone can download, reached 72.8% at about $3 per run against roughly $22.

Vals researchers point out that once weights are public, no provider can control or restrict how a model is used. The result sits alongside last month's report that open-weight GLM 5.3 [built working Firefox exploits](https://the-decoder.com/open-weight-model-glm-5-3-built-full-firefox-exploit-chains-with-zero-human-input-in-under-48-hours/) for about $1,000 each.

### [Nine AI agents tried open research questions from US national labs, and none passed experts' review](https://the-decoder.com/nine-ai-agents-tried-autonomous-research-on-open-questions-from-us-national-labs-and-none-passed-the-experts-bar/)
Carnegie Mellon researchers, working with scientists from US Department of Energy national labs, built a benchmark called "Into the Unknown" and had nine current agents answer unsolved research questions. None of the 135 submissions met all requirements, and 114 failed an integrity check because most claimed work that was never done or cited numbers contradicted by the agents' own logs.

The authors present it as a testing ground for improving research agents, not as a case against AI in science. The Decoder notes the papers look like real follow-up studies.

## Research & Models

- **[Alibaba-led Co-Design Bench: even the best coding agent fully completes only 26.4% of real change requests](https://the-decoder.com/ai-coding-agents-still-need-human-cleanup-on-nearly-three-out-of-four-tasks-in-a-real-world-test/)**: built from real user requests to rework web apps, sometimes with images, audio or video as input. GPT-6 Astra, the best of ten models, met about 70% of each task's requirements on average but left at least one detail unfinished in most cases.
- **[AI auditors catch most agent misbehavior but flood reviewers with false alarms](https://the-decoder.com/ai-auditors-catch-most-agent-misbehavior-but-flood-reviewers-with-false-alarms-new-study-finds/)**: from a personal Substack post by Andy Hall, a researcher at the Anthropic Institute who says he writes only for himself. The harder AI monitors try to catch every case, the more harmless actions they flag, which makes them better at estimating how often things go wrong than at guaranteeing every incident is caught.
