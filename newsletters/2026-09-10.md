## Top Stories

### [OpenAI puts an alignment researcher on the committee that can block its releases](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/openai-adds-a-prominent-ai-doomer-to-its-board-of-directors/)
Paul Christiano joined the OpenAI Foundation board on Wednesday, and specifically its Safety and Security Committee — the body chaired by Zico Kolter that holds final authority over whether a model ships. Christiano developed reinforcement learning from human feedback at OpenAI, left in 2021 to found the Alignment Research Center, and now advises the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, from which he will recuse himself on OpenAI matters. His stated reason for joining is not reassuring and is not meant to be: "I now believe there is a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term," with the case for taking the seat being that "if OpenAI rises to the occasion we could significantly reduce risk." It is the third such signal in five days, after OpenAI's chief scientist said no lab has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling and Anthropic's alignment lead agreed publicly that AI could kill everyone.

### [Anthropic's own economic model makes its CEO's forecast the tail case](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios)
Published this week by the Anthropic Economic Institute as v1.0 of an "Econ Scenario Explorer": three scenarios for the US economy through 2030, labelled modest, substantial and extreme. The modest case has AI landing roughly the way the internet did. The substantial case has growth accelerating while knowledge-worker wages stagnate and displaced workers move into trades and care work. The extreme case has the economy doubling in size every 4.5 years while labour's share of output falls from about 60 cents in every dollar to 45.2%. The interesting part is institutional rather than economic: Dario Amodei has spent a year warning that up to half of entry-level office jobs could vanish by 2030 with unemployment at 10–20%, and his own institute has now placed that squarely in the outlier column. Either the CEO is out on a limb his researchers won't stand on, or the modelling is doing diplomatic work.

### [DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash under MIT, with a million-token context and a quarter of the KV cache](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)
Posted to Hugging Face today. It is a 552B-parameter mixture of experts that activates only 8B during prefill and 16B during decoding, built on a causal encoder-decoder design in which the decoder's global KV cache is projected from the encoder's final hidden states rather than rebuilt per layer. The result is the number that matters for long-running agents: 890 bytes per token of cache, about a quarter of V4-Flash, via a second-generation compressed sparse attention and an FP4 cache. It is multimodal with a vision encoder trained from scratch, trained on 45T multimodal tokens, takes a reasoning-effort dial from 1 to 100, and reports 90.6 Pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — against the 91.4 we noted for Claude Fable 5.1 on September 4. Frontier-adjacent agentic performance, MIT-licensed, with the memory footprint as the actual product.

## Product & Industry

- **[Suno rebuilds its models on licensed music and retires the old ones](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/suno-replaces-its-ai-models-with-a-new-one-trained-on-licensed-music-as-copyright-suits-pile-up/)** — v6 landed Wednesday in three variants (base, wild for looser output, mini for speed), built with data licensed from Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe, and Suno says it "is not trained using the data it used to train previous versions." The older models are being retired, which is the tell: the cleanest way to stop generating outputs derived from unlicensed training data is to switch the models off. It does not clear the decks — Sony, Universal and artists including Jason Isbell are still suing, and the company has admitted training on YouTube audio — but an artist opt-in remix programme with the labels suggests where the settlements are heading.
- **[Apple builds photo provenance into the iPhone 18 Pro camera sensor](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-has-a-new-way-prove-your-iphone-photos-arent-ai-slop/)** — announced Wednesday at Apple's "Surprise and Shine" event: the sensor signs its own capture data, Private Cloud Compute turns that into an unalterable Reference Image, and the Photos app lets you diff any edited version against it. Apple also says it supports SynthID for flagging AI-generated or AI-altered images, and developer APIs are coming so the check can live outside Photos. Aimed squarely at photojournalists — provenance at the sensor is a stronger claim than a watermark applied downstream.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Massachusetts orders large data centers onto 100% clean power](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/massachusetts-hits-data-centers-with-new-clean-power-rules/)** — Governor Maura Healey's executive order requires any data center above 25MW to cover all of its electricity demand with clean generation, either built on site, funded nearby, or paid into a ratepayer protection fund. The state is also pausing sales-tax exemption applications and telling municipalities not to sign non-disclosure agreements with developers — the quieter provision, and probably the one with the most effect on how these deals get negotiated. It is the third state in three months, after New York halted facilities of 50MW and above in July and Texas subjected new builds to public utility audits in August.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Qualcomm will design custom inference silicon for AWS — and use AWS to design it](https://the-decoder.com/aws-is-using-qualcomm-for-ai-inference-while-qualcomm-uses-aws-bedrock-to-design-the-chips/)** — announced Wednesday: multiple generations of Qualcomm inference chips will sit alongside Trainium, Graviton and Nitro in AWS data centers, together with optical interconnects carrying up to 1.6 terabits, while Qualcomm runs Bedrock to accelerate its own chip design. It is Qualcomm's third large data-center win since June, after Meta and Microsoft, against a target of $15B in data-center revenue by 2029 — a company that missed the training boom buying its way into the inference one.
