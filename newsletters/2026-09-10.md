## Top Stories

### [OpenAI puts an alignment researcher on the committee that can block its releases](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/openai-adds-a-prominent-ai-doomer-to-its-board-of-directors/)
Paul Christiano joined the OpenAI Foundation board on Wednesday, and specifically its Safety and Security Committee — the body chaired by Zico Kolter that holds final authority over whether a model ships. Christiano developed reinforcement learning from human feedback at OpenAI, left in 2021 to found the Alignment Research Center, and now advises the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, from which he will recuse himself on OpenAI matters. His stated reason for joining is not reassuring and is not meant to be: "I now believe there is a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term," with the case for taking the seat being that "if OpenAI rises to the occasion we could significantly reduce risk." It is the third such signal in five days, after OpenAI's chief scientist said no lab has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling and Anthropic's alignment lead agreed publicly that AI could kill everyone.

### [Anthropic's own economic model makes its CEO's forecast the tail case](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios)
Published this week by the Anthropic Economic Institute as v1.0 of an "Econ Scenario Explorer": three scenarios for the US economy through 2030, labelled modest, substantial and extreme. The modest case has AI landing roughly the way the internet did. The substantial case has growth accelerating while knowledge-worker wages stagnate and displaced workers move into trades and care work. The extreme case has the economy doubling in size every 4.5 years while labour's share of output falls from about 60 cents in every dollar to 45.2%. The interesting part is institutional rather than economic: Dario Amodei has spent a year warning that up to half of entry-level office jobs could vanish by 2030 with unemployment at 10–20%, and his own institute has now placed that squarely in the outlier column. Either the CEO is out on a limb his researchers won't stand on, or the modelling is doing diplomatic work.

### [DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash under MIT, with a million-token context and a quarter of the KV cache](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)
Posted to Hugging Face today. It is a 552B-parameter mixture of experts that activates only 8B during prefill and 16B during decoding, built on a causal encoder-decoder design in which the decoder's global KV cache is projected from the encoder's final hidden states rather than rebuilt per layer. The result is the number that matters for long-running agents: 890 bytes per token of cache, about a quarter of V4-Flash, via a second-generation compressed sparse attention and an FP4 cache. It is multimodal with a vision encoder trained from scratch, trained on 45T multimodal tokens, takes a reasoning-effort dial from 1 to 100, and reports 90.6 Pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — against the 91.4 we noted for Claude Fable 5.1 on September 4. Frontier-adjacent agentic performance, MIT-licensed, with the memory footprint as the actual product.

## Product & Industry

- **[Suno rebuilds its models on licensed music and retires the old ones](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/suno-replaces-its-ai-models-with-a-new-one-trained-on-licensed-music-as-copyright-suits-pile-up/)** — v6 landed Wednesday in three variants (base, wild for looser output, mini for speed), built with data licensed from Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe, and Suno says it "is not trained using the data it used to train previous versions." The older models are being retired, which is the tell: the cleanest way to stop generating outputs derived from unlicensed training data is to switch the models off. It does not clear the decks — Sony, Universal and artists including Jason Isbell are still suing, and the company has admitted training on YouTube audio — but an artist opt-in remix programme with the labels suggests where the settlements are heading.
- **[Apple builds photo provenance into the iPhone 18 Pro camera sensor](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-has-a-new-way-prove-your-iphone-photos-arent-ai-slop/)** — announced Wednesday at Apple's "Surprise and Shine" event: the sensor signs its own capture data, Private Cloud Compute turns that into an unalterable Reference Image, and the Photos app lets you diff any edited version against it. Apple also says it supports SynthID for flagging AI-generated or AI-altered images, and developer APIs are coming so the check can live outside Photos. Aimed squarely at photojournalists — provenance at the sensor is a stronger claim than a watermark applied downstream.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Massachusetts orders large data centers onto 100% clean power](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/massachusetts-hits-data-centers-with-new-clean-power-rules/)** — Governor Maura Healey's executive order requires any data center above 25MW to cover all of its electricity demand with clean generation, either built on site, funded nearby, or paid into a ratepayer protection fund. The state is also pausing sales-tax exemption applications and telling municipalities not to sign non-disclosure agreements with developers — the quieter provision, and probably the one with the most effect on how these deals get negotiated. It is the third state in three months, after New York halted facilities of 50MW and above in July and Texas subjected new builds to public utility audits in August.

## AI in Real Estate

*A new standing section on AI where it touches property — prices, rates, and the day-to-day of managing buildings and tenants. Because it starts today, this first edition catches up on the year so far rather than the past week; everything is dated. From tomorrow it runs two to four items drawn from the past month, with background pieces marked "Context:".*

**The year's shape in one line:** the software that sets rents is being outlawed city by city while the software that answers the phone, screens the applicant and files the tax appeal is being bought as fast as it ships.

- **[Cities are banning rent-setting algorithms outright, and the suits are following](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/algorithmic-rent-pricing-litigation-expands-under-new-state-and-local-laws)** — the clearest trend of the year, mapped in an August 21 review: six jurisdictions now restrict algorithmic rent pricing, with Berkeley and Spokane effective in January, Portland in February, Minneapolis in March, Santa Ana in April and Rockville following on January 1, 2027. The drafting differs — some ban coordinated pricing algorithms outright, others only the use of nonpublic competitor data — but the teeth are similar: $500 to $7,500 per violation, treble damages in some places, and both private tenant suits and public enforcement. Follow-on cases against property managers were filed through July and August in San Francisco, San Diego, Seattle and Philadelphia. For an owner, "our vendor sets the rents" has stopped being a defence.
- **[Context: mortgage AI now has a compliance regime, in force since August 6](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/news-events/lender-letter-ll-2026-04-governance-framework-use-artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning)** — Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-04, issued April 24 and effective last month, requires every approved seller and servicer using AI or machine learning anywhere in origination or servicing to run a documented governance programme: written policies covering the full lifecycle of each system, reviewed annually, with a named owner, and vendors held to the same standard. It deliberately does not distinguish between models a lender builds and ones it buys. Freddie Mac issued more prescriptive guidance alongside it. The practical effect is that a lender can no longer treat an AI underwriting or servicing tool as a vendor's problem — and separately, from **November 2** every appraisal delivered to either GSE must use the UAD 3.6 dynamic report, retiring the 1004 and its siblings in favour of structured fields, which is the precondition for valuation being automated at all.
- **[Context: algorithmic rent-setting has cost landlords about $360M so far](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-settlement-algorithmic-pricing/820745/)** — a second batch of settlements in the RealPage antitrust class action, filed May 14 in the Middle District of Tennessee, adds $218M across 14 defendants — Equity Residential at $56M, Camden and Mid-America at $53M each — on top of $141.8M agreed in October 2025. Settling landlords accept they will stop feeding RealPage nonpublic tenant data and stop using the revenue-management system that ingests competitors' confidential pricing. None admit liability, and a judge still has to approve. The software itself survives: the DOJ's November 2025 settlement lets it keep making rent recommendations, but only from a landlord's own data and public information.
- **[Colorado gutted the one US law that would have governed AI housing decisions](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/colorado-ai-act-amended-and-effective-date-delayed)** — the Colorado AI Act was due to take effect on June 30 as the first US statute regulating "high-risk" AI in consequential decisions, housing among them. On May 14 the governor signed SB 189, which pushed it to January 1, 2027 and stripped out the risk-based core: the duty of care against algorithmic discrimination, the obligation on deployers to run risk-management programmes and impact assessments, and parts of the reporting regime. What survives is largely disclosure and transparency. Anyone who screens tenants or prices credit with a model in Colorado just got fifteen months and a far lighter obligation than the one they were preparing for.
- **[The leasing agent that answers the phone shipped in July](https://www.appfolio.com/blog/product-update-2026-q2)** — AppFolio's Leasing Performer already handled email and text; the summer release, announced July 6, gave it voice. It now picks up prospect calls after hours, answers questions and books showings by phone in multiple languages, logging every interaction in the leasing CRM where staff can watch it work. AppFolio's own claim is 35% more showings booked on average — a vendor number, so treat it as a marketing figure rather than a measurement. The significance is not the chatbot but the channel: phone was the last part of leasing that still required a person to be awake.
- **[The AI-run letting agency is being tested at scale in the UK](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/02/dwelly-secures-69m-to-expand-ai-powered-rental-marketplace/)** — Dwelly raised £69M in February — £32M of equity led by General Catalyst with Begin Capital and S16VC, plus a £37M debt facility from Trinity Capital — to buy independent letting agencies and re-run them on its own software, with AI handling applicant screening, offer management and the triage of tenant maintenance requests. Founded in 2024 by former Uber and Gett operators, it is already among the UK's fifteen largest letting agencies with more than 10,000 properties under management. This is the "virtual property manager" thesis in its most testable form: not a chatbot bolted onto an agency, but a rollup whose margin depends on the software actually replacing the back office.
- **[AI is being pointed at the tax line of the operating statement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ownwell-raises-50m-launches-national-service-to-streamline-property-tax-appeals-and-make-home-ownership-more-affordable-302692103.html)** — Ownwell announced $50M on February 19 — $30M of equity led by Alpha Edison and Mercato Partners, with Intuit Ventures, Left Lane, First Round and others, plus $20M of debt from Western Alliance — alongside a national product for contesting property tax assessments in any state using its own data and models. Property tax is one of the few large operating costs an owner can contest rather than merely absorb, which is what makes it an obvious early target for automation.
