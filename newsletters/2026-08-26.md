## Top Stories

### [OpenAI says a Russian operation ran a fake think tank through ChatGPT for eight months](https://the-decoder.com/russia-used-chatgpt-to-run-a-covert-influence-campaign-pushing-pro-kremlin-narratives-across-the-west/)
From September 2025 to May 2026, operators reaching ChatGPT over VPNs from Russia asked it to strip any linguistic trace of their origin, then generated content promoting the "International Burke Institute," a fabricated think tank whose "sovereignty index" ranked Russia favourably against Western states. OpenAI found 34 of 36 expert-attributed articles were plagiarised with false bylines. Reach was modest — individual posts landed poorly, though Telegram channels ran 10,000–20,000 followers and the material spread to X, LinkedIn, Facebook and Substack — and OpenAI rates it three on Brookings' six-point Breakout Scale. It banned the accounts; it is the third Russian operation OpenAI has disrupted since June 2024. The shape is what matters: this is the second state-funded fake institute in nine days, after the Israeli-commissioned Hanover Institute we covered on August 18. Manufacturing an authoritative-sounding source is now a standard play, and chatbots are both the factory and the target.

### [Ukraine opens its battlefield dataset to British firms](https://the-decoder.com/ukraine-opens-its-massive-labeled-battlefield-dataset-to-british-firms-in-a-landmark-ai-weapons-partnership/)
Under the 100 Year Partnership, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and President Zelensky signed an agreement in Kyiv making Britain the first foreign country with access to Ukraine's Avengers Labs platform: roughly five million annotated combat images drawn from front-line drone footage, acoustic sensors and the DELTA combat system. The scale is the point — systems trained on it process over 100,000 drone video streams a month, and Ukraine's ministry cites 70% identification of enemy equipment at 2.2 seconds per object. Terms keep the data in place: UK firms train and test inside a secured dataroom built partly with Palantir, and finished systems stay with Ukraine. Sintela, Mind Foundry and Skyral are piloting. The dataset is real combat imagery rather than synthetic, at a volume no peacetime programme can assemble — and what it trains is the step from human-selected targets to machine-selected ones.

## Research & Models

- **[IBM ships Granite 4.2 as open weights under Apache 2.0](https://the-decoder.com/ibm-drops-open-weight-granite-4-2-family-with-built-in-agentic-capabilities-under-apache-2-0/)** — released Wednesday at 3B, 8B and 30B, with context windows to 512,000 tokens and a toggle between thinking and non-thinking modes. The 8B and 30B carry what IBM calls agentic RL training — tool use, writing and running code, and web search learned in real sandboxes rather than simulated ones — with OpenAI-format tool calling throughout, and IBM puts the 30B ahead on agentic and tool-use benchmarks. Alongside it, Granite Speech 5.0 Turbo CTC at 470M parameters, which IBM says transcribes three hours of audio in a second. On Hugging Face, Ollama and GitHub, running under vLLM or SGLang.

## Product & Industry

- **[Google launches Gemini Enterprise for Legal](https://the-decoder.com/google-launches-gemini-for-legal-work-to-automate-contracts-and-research/)** — in preview from Tuesday for contract review, legal research and regulatory monitoring, wired into iManage, NetDocuments, DocuSign, Everlaw, RelativityOne and Thomson Reuters HighQ while respecting existing permissions, with Deloitte supplying pre-built agents. Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian frames it as one of a line of industry packages, financial services already out and healthcare coming. Worth being clear about what these are: pre-built prompts — "skills" — plus data connectors over the same underlying models. It lands a day after Thomson Reuters explained why it spent $40M building its own legal model instead.
- **[OpenAI's head of data centers leaves, making 13-plus executive departures this year](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/openai-loses-a-top-data-center-exec-as-stream-of-high-profile-departures-continues/)** — Chris Malone, who joined in March 2025 after five years at Meta and over a decade at Google, left last week with no destination disclosed. He follows CRO Denise Dresser, COO Brad Lightcap two days before her, and Fidji Simo about a month ago — she left for reasons of chronic illness and stays on as an adviser. Losing the person responsible for data centres is a particular kind of gap for a company midway through the largest compute buildout ever attempted.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[OpenAI's Jalapeño posts better inference numbers than Nvidia's shipping hardware](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/openais-jalapeno-chip-is-built-for-fast-inference-at-scale-benchmarks-show/)** — benchmarked at Hot Chips on SemiAnalysis's InferenceX suite, the Broadcom-built chip delivered more tokens per user and more throughput per kilowatt than state-of-the-art Blackwell systems, at lower latency; no comparison against Rubin was shown. Hardware chief Richard Ho calls it "a very, very significant performance advance over state of the art," and supplies the caveat himself: deployment starts "at the end of 2026 in very small volumes," broadening in 2027, by which point Nvidia will have moved. OpenAI already rents Cerebras silicon for the Ultrafast tier we covered on August 14; Jalapeño is the in-house half of the same bet. The largest buyer of Nvidia's accelerators now has its own option for the inference half of the workload — in the same month Nvidia has been buying into power developers and underwriting GPU residual value.
