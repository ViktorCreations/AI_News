# AI News — Wednesday, September 9, 2026

> OpenAI says an unreleased model proved that Navier-Stokes solutions can blow up — and the two mathematicians who were days from the same result say OpenAI raced them to it after hearing rumours, then pressed one of them to drop his Anthropic-employed co-author. Separately, a pretraining researcher resigned from Anthropic saying both labs are gambling with our lives, and Anthropic's own alignment lead publicly agreed with him.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI claims a Millennium Prize problem, and a priority fight breaks out around it](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/)
Published Tuesday: OpenAI says a multi-agent system running an unreleased model more capable than Astra produced a proof that the 3D Navier-Stokes equations can blow up — fluids that start perfectly ordinary reaching infinite speed in finite time. That is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, and OpenAI says it will not claim the $1M. The work began September 1, cost about $22.5M in compute, and OpenAI concedes it started because of rumours that Anthropic's models had cracked a Millennium problem. Nothing here is verified yet: no independent mathematician outside the company has reviewed the proof, and the Clay Institute's own process requires publication and broad expert acceptance. Terence Tao called the *other* team's work — Tristan Buckmaster of NYU and Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic researcher acting personally, who announced three proofs the same day — a "remarkable achievement," while warning that "the indiscriminate strip-mining of open problems for solutions may destroy the ecosystem from which the next generation of mathematical techniques, problems, and practitioners would have developed."

### [The mathematician's account: "Why would you ruin your career?"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/)
Buckmaster published a statement alleging that news of his and Alpöge's progress reached OpenAI, which then aimed an entire team at the problem. He says OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck offered him two ways to keep credit: publish a partial result and let OpenAI publish the full proof a day later, or write a solo paper crediting an OpenAI model — without Alpöge, whose employer is a competitor. He refused both. When he said he would go public, he says Bubeck replied "Why would you ruin your career?" Buckmaster also says he asked whether the model had been trained on their Codex sessions and got no answer. OpenAI's response is partial: Bubeck denies asking for Alpöge's name to be removed and says "we did not use their prompts or proofs to prompt our models," while the company concedes it "cannot rule out that de-identified data" from their product use improved its models, and has since acknowledged the pair's priority. The threat allegations remain unaddressed. Alpöge says he would have collaborated had he been asked.

### [A pretraining researcher quits Anthropic, and its alignment lead says he's right](https://www.forbes.com/sites/siladityaray/2026/09/09/anthropic-alignment-lead-warns-ai-could-kill-all-humans-as-researcher-quits/)
Jacob Coxon resigned on Tuesday after three years of pretraining research split between OpenAI and Anthropic, and left the field entirely. His statement: "Neither company is acting responsibly. They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives." His distinction between the two is the sharp part — at OpenAI he says the civilisational stakes are not deeply internalised, while at Anthropic they are well understood and the race continues anyway, on the reasoning that someone will do it regardless so it had better be them. What makes this more than an exit note is the reply: Evan Hubinger, who runs alignment science at Anthropic, agreed publicly that Coxon is right that AI could kill all humans, and said that while Anthropic is trying its best, "we do not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence and are not clearly on track to." That lands three days after OpenAI's chief scientist wrote that no lab has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling at full speed.

## Research & Models

- **[DeepMind publishes a petabyte of predictions for every possible single-letter DNA change](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/)** — AlphaGenome Atlas, out Tuesday, precomputes the AlphaGenome model's output for all ~9 billion single-nucleotide variants in the human genome and serves them through a portal that needs no coding. It introduces a combined coding/non-coding impact score, and DeepMind reports one study surfacing 22% more non-coding genetic associations using it. The value is in the pre-computation: work that would have required a research group's compute budget becomes a lookup.

## Product & Industry

- **[Meta ships Muse, an agent that reads your email and pays with your card](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)** — launched Tuesday in the US for adults on iOS, Android and web, with AI glasses to follow. It books travel, fills forms, negotiates, and completes purchases, running on a dedicated cloud virtual machine with a browser the user can watch, and it keeps working after the app closes. Free up to 100 million tokens a week, then $20 a month for Power and $100 for Maximum. It runs on Muse Spark, the model line we covered on September 4. Meta's pitch is that conversations stay out of advertising — the claim to hold them to, given that an agent with your inbox and your card is the most commercially valuable object the company has ever built.
- **[Meta drops AI usage from engineer performance reviews after "tokenmaxxing"](https://the-decoder.com/meta-drops-ai-usage-from-engineer-performance-reviews-after-tokenmaxxing-backfires/)** — per The Information, an internal memo from Maher Saba and Santosh Janardhan removed AI-tool usage as an evaluation criterion effective immediately, because engineers were burning tokens to climb internal leaderboards rather than to get work done. What counts now is "the quality, speed, and complexity of the work." Internal AI spend is heading toward billions this year, and Meta is adding budgets and a central dashboard in 2027. A clean natural experiment in what happens when you make a proxy metric a target.
- **[ChatGPT Images 2.5 adds a sketch canvas and comment-based editing](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-images-2-5/)** — out Tuesday across ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex on every tier, generating up to 50% faster than Images 2.0, with better subject preservation across multi-turn edits. New: an @Sketch canvas for drawing what you mean, templates for posters and product shots, and comments attached to specific parts of an image. Two API models — Flare for speed, Sunburst for precision.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[ASML locks in the next lithography generation with all three leading foundries](https://the-decoder.com/asml-locks-in-tsmc-samsung-and-intel-while-huawei-races-to-break-its-grip/)** — reported Tuesday by Bloomberg and the FT: Samsung takes High-NA EUV into high-volume manufacturing from 2028, TSMC by 2030, and Intel already has machines running, at up to $400M per tool. The less visible shift is photomask size, moving from 6-inch to 12-inch — a test line at TSMC by 2031, production by 2033, and about 40% more throughput. Meanwhile Huawei is not chasing EUV at all: it is backing Shanghai's Yuliangsheng on advanced DUV, now being tested by SMIC, and funding Zeiss competitors.
- **[Argentine Patagonia is being lined up for gigawatt-scale AI data centers](https://the-decoder.com/patagonia-has-what-ai-data-centers-want-including-no-resistance-so-far/)** — Reuters on Monday: Pampa Energía is planning up to 500MW in Neuquén near its Loma de la Lata plant, with roughly $900M of grid work alone, while Poland's Green Capital targets 300MW in Chubut with 3,000MW long term. The draw is hydro, wind and Vaca Muerta shale gas, plus — as the headline concedes — no local opposition so far. First contracts are expected by the end of the year, with initial tenants at 20–40MW each; the 2027 presidential election is the obvious risk to all of it.
