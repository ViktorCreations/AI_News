## Top Stories

### [OpenAI is cutting Cursor off, and says the reason is Musk](https://the-decoder.com/openai-cuts-off-cursor-after-spacex-acquisition-citing-musks-history-of-breaking-contracts/)
Announced Saturday, effective 12 November: OpenAI is terminating its contract with Cursor, two weeks after SpaceX closed its acquisition of the company. OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux does not dress it up — "It boils down to trust" — and points to Musk's record on contracts, including cutting OpenAI off from Twitter data in 2022 and training on other labs' outputs. Users can still reach GPT models through Cursor with their own API keys, and OpenAI keeps supporting the IDE extension. Cursor co-founder Michael Truell says OpenAI models are only about 5% of Cursor's traffic, that he was surprised given years of close work, and that talks are ongoing. The number makes the commercial damage small and the precedent large: model access is now something a supplier will withdraw over who owns you.

### [Anthropic proposes an MCP for laboratory hardware](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-wants-to-do-for-physical-hardware-what-its-model-context-protocol-did-for-software/)
The Model Hardware Standard gives each device a driver that exposes its basic functions and makes it discoverable in a common format, so an agent can read from and control microscopes, robotic arms and instruments — and coordinate several at once, saving workflows as ordinary scripts that then run on their own. Anthropic says it cuts integration time for lab equipment "from the typical weeks or months down to hours or minutes." It was built with HHMI's Janelia Research Campus, with early testing at Genentech, Carnegie Mellon and QuEra, and integration work from AWS, Doosan Robotics, QIAGEN, Tecan, Universal Robots, Hugging Face and Raspberry Pi. It is a research preview for selected labs and manufacturers, open-source release promised later. MCP became an industry standard by being donated; whether this follows depends on that release actually happening.

## Research & Models

- **[DeepMind's Co-Scientist now runs the experiment and writes the paper](https://the-decoder.com/google-deepminds-ai-co-scientist-now-plans-experiments-runs-lab-equipment-and-writes-scientific-papers/)** — the hypothesis generator has become a closed loop: plan experiments, write code, control lab equipment, draft the manuscript, and cross-check numerical claims against execution logs. Tested across three fields — a 2D-material synthesis recipe that produced layered structures after 25 human-guided iterations; an E. coli colony-pattern pipeline matching three of four shape features against unpublished lab results; and, most autonomously, a medical AI architecture that beat six frontier models on benchmarks, though physicians found a statistical advantage in only one category. The number to carry away is the honesty check: it fabricated key results in 4% of autonomously written papers with reliability modules on, and 46% with them off. It also "writes highly plausible methods in the paper that did not match its actual code."
- **[Anthropic's automated alignment researcher beats humans on cost, if not on judgment](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/an-anthropic-researcher-just-gave-us-a-peek-at-self-improving-ai/)** — "Automated Researchers Can Reliably Mitigate Alignment Failures," published Friday from a project led by Chen Yueh-Han in Anthropic's fellows programme. The system searches the literature, proposes methods and trains models in 30-minute iterations, and improved all ten alignment benchmarks without degrading general performance; the paper claims the best automated method "beats what experienced humans propose, on average within six hours," at roughly $4 an hour of API inference against $150 an hour for a researcher. The caveat is load-bearing and stated: it only works when the benchmarks genuinely capture the alignment goal, and building and maintaining those benchmarks is the part that has not been automated.
- **[LAION releases 10 million hours of open video](https://the-decoder.com/laion-drops-massive-open-video-dataset-with-10-million-hours-of-footage-for-ai-research/)** — the Big Video Dataset: 80 million videos totalling 10 million hours, 55 million clips with auto-generated video and audio descriptions, and 300 million stills, filtered down from 1.3 billion video URLs found in CommonCrawl. Mostly English-language YouTube. It is research-only, free on GitHub, and LAION leans on a 2024 Hamburg ruling permitting non-commercial collection of copyrighted material for research, while asking users to respect creators' rights. Set against this month's arguments over training data — the FTC complaint over destructive book scanning, Google buying a bankrupt airline's inbox — this is the other model: collect openly, publish the provenance, restrict the use.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[A court finds the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic unlawful](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/anthropic-gets-its-first-court-win-over-the-pentagons-supply-chain-risk-label/)** — US District Judge Rita Lin ruled on Thursday that the supply-chain risk designation was unlawful on three counts: First Amendment retaliation, arbitrary and capricious decision-making, and denial of Fifth Amendment due process. "The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics," she wrote. The dispute began with Anthropic's guardrails barring use of Claude for autonomous weapons and mass surveillance of Americans; the Pentagon denied any such intent and argued Anthropic was overreaching in trying to govern models it had bought. Anthropic filed two complaints in March; this settles the California one, and the Washington, D.C. case continues. The timing matters for a company weeks away from an IPO, and the precedent matters more — a court has held that refusing a use case, and saying so publicly, is protected.

## Product & Industry

- **[Beatport bars fully AI-generated tracks](https://the-decoder.com/beatport-blocks-fully-ai-generated-music-from-its-dj-marketplace/)** — the DJ marketplace will reject music made entirely or mostly by AI while allowing predominantly human tracks that use AI, labelled as such, with screening by partner Beatdapp and notifications to rights holders when a track is rejected. CEO Matt Gralen's line is that electronic music has always absorbed new production tools and that wholesale replacement is a different threshold. The customer research behind it: 60% of Beatport users say they would not play AI-generated music in a set, and 77% prefer human-made tracks.
