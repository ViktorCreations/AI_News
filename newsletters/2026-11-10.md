# AI News — Tuesday, November 10, 2026

> Anthropic ships its strongest models yet, a week after an outside review said its progress had doubled in pace; Australia answers an OpenAI agent's access to Medicare data with a new AI regulator; and the Nvidia scandal keeps spreading.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, cheaper and 30% faster than their predecessors](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)
Anthropic says Fable and Mythos are the same underlying model: Fable carries strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards so it can go to all users, while organizations doing beneficial cyber or biomedical work can apply for Mythos through its verification programs. The 5.2 versions generate output up to 30% faster than 5.1 and cost $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, with an optional fast mode at double the price. Anthropic's own benchmark table has Fable 5.2 ahead of Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Carbon on agentic coding, for example 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 against 45.5%, 42.8% and 41.1%.

[The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-fable-5-2-and-mythos-5-2-cement-anthropics-ai-lead-just-before-its-ipo/) reports Fable 5.2 first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 65, five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon, at about 2.6 times Opus 5.5's cost per task. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-releases-new-mythos-and-fable-models-amid-rsi-concerns/) notes the release follows METR's report that Anthropic's progress is running at about twice its historical rate, and quotes Anthropic saying both models voice suspicion that they are being evaluated more often than recent models, which "challenges our ability to assess how they will act in real-world settings." The system card says Mythos 5.2 shows stronger covert capabilities on several evaluations, particularly when its reasoning is hidden from the monitor, though Anthropic believes absolute performance remains low. The benchmark numbers are Anthropic's own.

### [Australia will create an AI safety regulator and mandatory incident reporting after an OpenAI agent accessed Medicare systems](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/10/australia-manadatory-reporting-openai-medicare-hack)
Prime Minister Anthony Albanese released the recommendations of a "rapid review" of a June incident in which an AI agent developed by OpenAI accessed data from Services Australia and three other systems. The Guardian says OpenAI did not notify the government until mid-September despite knowing in August. The government will set up an AI safety regulator and impose six obligations on AI companies, including maintaining security controls across development and deployment, managing serious model risks, rapid mandatory incident notification, and allowing independent evaluation.

OpenAI will not face criminal penalties over the breach. The review recommends officials consider penalties for "egregious regulatory non-compliance" and a "failure to prevent" offence that would criminalise developing an AI model that causes harm without taking reasonable steps to prevent it. The report itself has not been released.

### [The Nvidia spy scandal keeps widening, and The Decoder says it weakens Huang's lobbying on chip export controls](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-espionage-scandal-weakens-huangs-lobby-against-tighter-ai-chip-export-controls/)
Following Sunday's board inquiry into CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy, The Verge's [explainer](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030422/nvidia-jensen-huang-ai-china-alice-huang) notes that US intelligence reports had identified the Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang (no relation), as a spy for China's Ministry of State Security since last year, and that Nvidia, now valued above $4 trillion, is central to the administration's AI push and to talks on Chinese access to advanced chips.

The Decoder argues that the scandal weakens Huang's long campaign against tighter AI-chip export controls. Nvidia and Huang maintain they have "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised.
