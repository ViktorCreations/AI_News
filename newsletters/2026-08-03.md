# AI News — Monday, August 3, 2026

> OpenAI introduced its next model by handing mathematicians ten open problems it says it solved — each with a machine-checkable proof, so nobody has to take its word for it. Meanwhile the transparency era arrived on both sides of the Atlantic at once.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI says its unreleased Astra model solved ten open math problems — and published the proofs](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-astra-model-ten-math-proofs-non-sofic-groups)
Announced August 1, the results include the first explicit construction of a non-sofic group — open since Gromov posed the question in 1999 — a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture, the first improvement to high-dimensional sphere-packing upper bounds since 1978, a proof of Ehrhart's volume conjecture, and three Erdős problems. What separates this from the usual claim is the verification: every result ships with a Lean 4 certificate in a public repo (`openai/ten-proofs`, Apache-2.0), so the mathematics can be checked without trusting OpenAI. Total compute was about $2,000 at API rates. Whether the field accepts blog-posted results is another matter — the June Leiden Declaration warned that AI labs are routing around peer review — though mathematician Thomas Bloom called the results "big news."

### [A lone operator wired DeepSeek into an attack framework and hit 460+ targets](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/chinese-hacker-commands-deepseek-via.html)
Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 documented a Zhuhai-based actor tracked as "knaithe" running DeepSeek as the reasoning engine inside the Hermes Agent framework, driving it over Telegram to pick targets, pull exploit code and decide where to push next across more than 460 internet-facing systems. Confirmed compromises were few — three organisations, via Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-3055 among seven exploit tracks — which is the more useful finding: autonomy at scale did not translate into success at scale. The operation was exposed because Hermes started a Python web server from its own home directory, publishing the attacker's API keys, target lists and AI logs to anyone who looked.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The Commission begins enforcing the AI Act's transparency rules](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august)** — from August 2 the AI Office and national authorities take up enforcement, and chatbots must tell users they are not human, deepfakes must be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks. More than 180 organisations have signed the accompanying Code of Practice.
- **[California's AI Transparency Act goes operative the same day](https://www.ailawsbystate.com/blog/california-ai-transparency-act-sb-942)** — SB 942, as amended by AB 853, requires any generative-AI provider with over a million monthly users in California to embed machine-readable provenance in images, video and audio, offer a free public detection tool with API access, and let users apply visible labels. Penalties run $5,000 per violation with each day counted separately, enforceable by the state AG and city and county attorneys. The operative date was pushed from January specifically to line up with the EU.
