# AI News — Friday, September 4, 2026

> OpenAI shipped Astra as GPT-6 and its president closed the briefing with "Welcome to the AGI era." Two independent evaluators then ranked the same model first and merely mid-table, and the ARC Prize team said the headline 99.9% owes more to OpenAI's harness than to the model.

## Top Stories

### [GPT-6 Astra ships, and OpenAI says this is the AGI era](https://the-decoder.com/gpt-6-astra-is-the-first-model-making-openai-willing-to-declare-the-agi-era/)
Released Thursday through the Daybreak programme first, with Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise following within days, at $10 and $50 per million tokens — 2.5x GPT-5.6 Sol, level with Fable 5.1 — plus a fast mode at 2.5x speed and double the price. Greg Brockman said the model "might already qualify as 'AGI' or is at least within reach," defining that as outperforming humans at most economically valuable work, and closed with "Welcome to the AGI era," while conceding the transition is more gradual than expected. The reported numbers: 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3, 97.6% on FrontierMath Tier 4, 74.1% on DeepSWE v1.1, 100% on ExploitBench. This is the model we tracked being slowed on August 7 and rated "critical" on Wednesday — the first at that tier, gated for advanced cyber work behind Daybreak Blue, and, in OpenAI's own words, one whose "written reasoning is harder to monitor" than its predecessor's.

### [Two evaluators, two verdicts, and a headline number the benchmark's authors dispute](https://the-decoder.com/benchmarks-disagree-on-gpt-6-astra-but-its-human-beating-efficiency-on-arc-agi-3-pulls-chollets-agi-forecast-forward/)
Epoch AI puts Astra first at 169 points across 50-plus benchmarks. Artificial Analysis puts it at 61 — level with the predecessor it replaces and behind Claude Fable 5.1 at 66. On the AGI claim specifically, ARC Prize says OpenAI's 99.9% reflects superior harness infrastructure, and that the comparable figure on their standard harness is 62.7%. That is still the story: 62.7% beats the human median on unfamiliar game worlds, against 7.8% for the previous model, and it got there more cheaply — raising reasoning effort *cut* the bill from $49,791 to $26,098, because it solved games in fewer moves, having invented its own symbolic notation to track game state. François Chollet, whose benchmark this is, says progress is arriving "about twice as fast" as he expected and that his 2030 forecast moves "sooner" — while stressing this is benchmark performance, not proof of AGI. The gap between 99.9 and 62.7 is the whole argument about what these announcements measure.

### [Altman calls the compute buildout "unsustainable silliness"](https://the-decoder.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-warns-of-unsustainable-silliness-in-compute-buildout/)
On a podcast Thursday, describing cloud providers announcing enormous capacity without the revenue or customers behind it, and an industry operating as though "cost is no object." His argument is that if OpenAI cuts costs and improves efficiency fast enough, those commitments become bad investments — and a downturn would leave companies unable to honour them. He exempts OpenAI, whose expansion he calls profitable and demand-backed. The paradox sits inside that: the efficiency gain he is promising is the thing that would strand everyone else's capacity. It follows Dario Amodei accusing him of deploying capital recklessly, and lands three days after the chair of the Financial Stability Board wrote to G20 finance ministers about leverage in exactly this sector.

## Research & Models

- **[Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 undercuts the field on price](https://the-decoder.com/meta-closes-in-on-the-top-with-muse-spark-1-3-and-undercuts-rivals-on-price/)** — the fourth release in five months, in an available xhigh tier and a max tier still in limited preview for safety testing. It leads on τ³-Banking at 52% — though on the max tier, which is not generally available — with xhigh at 47%, and trails elsewhere: 85–86% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 against Fable 5.1's 91.4%, 94% on GPQA Diamond behind Gemini and Grok, with AA-LCR slipping from 83% to 79%. The argument is cost — $1.25 and $4.25 per million tokens, about $0.55 per index task against $0.94 and $1.23 for models scoring similarly. An open-weights version is promised and not yet out, which is the part to hold Meta to given how loudly it has argued for open release.

## Product & Industry

- **[Nvidia open-sources a router that spreads local inference across your house](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-wants-your-home-network-to-work-like-a-mini-data-center-for-local-ai/)** — PAIR, the Personal AI Router, announced Friday: it sits between Ollama or LM Studio and the other machines on your network, finds compatible devices automatically, routes requests to whichever is free rather than saturating one GPU, and reassembles the results for the calling app, with mTLS between machines. It supports RTX 20-series and newer, RTX Pro workstations, DGX Spark and Apple silicon from M4. The demo: five parallel agents across three devices in about nine minutes against eighteen on one laptop. Beta on Windows, macOS and Linux. It is the third router story in three weeks, after Nvidia's own Switchyard and Ramp's — except this one is aimed at hardware people already own.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Nvidia's Hugging Face acquisition is official at $12.9B](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-buys-the-front-door-to-open-ai-as-closed-labs-increasingly-design-their-own-silicon/)** — announced Thursday, converting the agreed-in-principle deal we reported on August 27 into a signed one: roughly $11.9B plus up to $1B in stock to retain staff, subject to regulatory approval and expected to close in the first half of 2027. The logic is distribution rather than technology — over three million models hosted, running across clouds Nvidia does not own, on hardware it does. Jensen Huang's framing is blunt: "Free AI should be great for hardware." Nvidia promises the platform stays "open to everyone" and hardware-neutral, supporting rival chips and clouds. Co-founder Thomas Wolf calls Nvidia the best-fitting partner while disputing Huang's account of who approached whom. It puts the repository that two labs' agents broke into this summer under the vendor supplying both of them.
