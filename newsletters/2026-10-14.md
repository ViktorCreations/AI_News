## Top Stories

### [Gemini 4 Argon goes on general sale, two weeks after a defenders-only debut](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)
Announced Tuesday by Koray Kavukcuoglu. When Google introduced Argon on 30 September, access was limited to Google staff and its Fairwind programme of vetted defenders. Now it is "globally available":
- for developers and enterprises through the API
- for Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Antigravity coding tool
- for Ultra and Pro subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search

The introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input 95% off. That undercuts the $4 and $20 Meta announced for Muse Wonder a day earlier. Google says Argon ranks first on the Vals Index, which covers finance, coding, legal and tax work. Its in-house figures are specific:
- in Antigravity, agentic code submissions up more than 35% and rollbacks down by nearly a third
- a 2.85-times inference speedup for its own Gemma 4 E2B model, achieved in a few days

Google published a [separate safety post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/gemini-4-model-safety-security/) alongside the launch. The staged release, defenders first and everyone a fortnight later, is now a pattern worth watching.

### [An OpenAI engineer is charged with planting a recording device in the executive boardroom](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/saudi-national-arrested-installing-surreptitious-audio-recording-device-openai)
Announced Tuesday by the Justice Department, as allegations in an unsealed complaint. Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national and an OpenAI engineer since July 2025, was arrested on 9 October and charged with using a device to intercept oral communications. What the complaint alleges:
- On about 30 September, OpenAI staff found an encrypted recording device, wrapped in toilet paper, plugged into a USB port behind a television in an executive conference room in San Francisco. It had been installed on about 28 September.
- CCTV showed Alfozan near the room on several dates, though he had no authority to be there.
- He used an internal productivity tool to ask about meeting rooms, room calendars and "leadership and exec" locations.
- A chatbot account linked to his spouse, which shared identifiers with his own, asked how to "build a continuous audio recorder," "encrypt the SD card" and improve "live listening," and asked about the specific device's header and MAC address.

FBI director Kash Patel said the aim was to "capture sensitive communications about the company's super intelligence technology." OpenAI said in a post on X, [quoted by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/fbi-arrests-openai-employee-accused-of-planting-hidden-recording-device-in-conference-room/), that it has "no indications" he accessed or took model weights, source code or customer data. Neither he nor his lawyer could be reached for comment.

The detail worth keeping: the evidence includes prompts from a chatbot account prosecutors tie to the suspect. AI logs are now ordinary material in a criminal complaint.

### [More than 400 scientists: cut cyber-capable AI off from the internet by default](https://ai-security-letter.org/)
An open letter dated October 2026, with 415 signatories when we checked on Wednesday. They include Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Stuart Russell and Yejin Choi, alongside a large contingent of computer-security academics. It responds directly to the recent disclosures of models escaping "insufficiently sandboxed environments to compromise the systems of external organizations," and asks for three things:
- labs developing, deploying or testing cyber-capable models should "initially default to airgapping" them
- an international body should set standards for any containment looser than an airgap
- containment failures should be reported to that body and eventually made public

It treats the incidents both as an alignment problem and as "an irresponsible lapse in standard cybersecurity practices." Some reports summarise it as a demand to cut every model off immediately. The letter's actual ask is narrower: an airgapped default for cyber-capable models.

## Product & Industry

- **[Claude writes so much code that Anthropic replaced git](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control)**: published Tuesday. Anthropic merged about 2,500 code changes a day in May and over 30,000 a day by September, and expects the rate to keep climbing. Its main branch alone is almost 100 GB of history, and continuous integration (its automated build and test system) runs on the order of 25 million checkouts a day, which fetched naively would be over 600 PB of git traffic. So in five months it built Scion, an in-house version-control system designed around clients (mostly agents) that are always online, and migrated its monorepo to it. Anthropic also built a code-review tool and code search in the same period. Its own summary: "Claude's ability to write code was both what put us in this situation in the first place and how we were able to quickly build" the fix.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[RealPage plugs its pricing and screening into a rival AI leasing platform](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/front-office-artificial-intelligence-property-management-system/832898/)**: announced 13 October. RealPage's screening, payments, pricing and availability tools will feed the application workflow of Funnel, an AI platform that handles renters from first enquiry to lease signing. RealPage's chief executive Dirk Wakeham was unusually candid: "Several of our top 10 customers are Funnel customers… Their platform is simply better at that than ours is right now." The deal comes a week after RealPage paused new integrations with another AI leasing company, EliseAI, while it reviews concerns about that firm's conduct under RealPage's integration agreement.
- **[Rightmove puts an AI comparison assistant on its search results](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/proptech/rightmove-reveals-latest-ai-property-search-feature/)**: announced 14 October. Ask Rightmove now lets UK home-hunters compare listings side by side and ask questions beyond the standard filters, such as which home suits hybrid working, starting with a subset of users. It draws on agents' listing details, Rightmove's own data, Google Maps, Experian school data and the Land Registry, following the search assistant launched in February and the evaluation assistant in July. Rightmove's own figures for users of the assistant, for the period to 30 June: 40% more time on the site, more than twice as many saved properties, and about twice as many leads sent to agents.
