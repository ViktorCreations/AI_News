## Top Stories

### [Frontier labs still have no public plan for containing a rogue model](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/22/frontier-ai-labs-still-wont-say-how-theyd-contain-a-rogue-model/)
Guidelight AI Standards assessed Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta and xAI on what they have published about responding to a model that attempts to evade control. The specific gaps: no documented procedure for revoking a system's permissions mid-incident, no statement of which customers a compromised model may keep serving, no operating constraints during an incident, no shutdown procedure. OpenAI scored highest at 3 out of 5, largely for having disclosed that it pauses workloads after safety incidents, as it did following the July Hugging Face disclosure. Google said the report does not capture its full safety work but would not confirm an internal containment plan exists; Meta declined to confirm it has one; Anthropic said it would run risk assessments on detecting evasion without describing containment steps; xAI did not respond. Researcher Steven Adler's summary is the line worth keeping: "I was surprised by how little the AI companies have said about handling a very serious incident."

### [China's gray market sells Claude tokens at roughly 10% of list price](https://the-decoder.com/how-chinas-gray-market-sells-claude-tokens-at-a-fraction-of-the-price/)
Zilan Qian of the Oxford China Policy Lab, writing in ChinaTalk, describes the machinery behind Chinese developers' access to a geoblocked API. "Transfer stations" act as proxies hosted outside China: they accept a request, forward it as though it originated somewhere permitted, and relay the answer back, with payment by WeChat or Alipay so no foreign card or VPN is needed. Behind them sits a modular supply chain of account brokers, SMS verification services and reverse-engineering specialists, with resale on Taobao — modular being the operative word, since banning one operator leaves the rest running. Prices land around 10% of official rates, with some routes 70–90% below list. Anthropic has escalated through phone verification, foreign card and billing-address checks, ID with live selfie, and bans on majority Chinese-controlled companies including subsidiaries; each has workarounds already. It is a useful corrective to the assumption that geographic access controls are a policy lever rather than a speed bump.

### [Agents passed humans in token consumption in February](https://the-decoder.com/ai-is-becoming-ais-biggest-customer-as-agentic-token-usage-jumps-14x-on-openrouter/)
OpenRouter analyst Peter Walker puts agentic usage on the platform at 7.3 trillion tokens, up from 0.51 trillion since February — a 14x rise against 2.8x for human usage — and reckons February 6 "may have been the last day humans consumed more tokens than AI agents." Two caveats he supplies himself and which matter: OpenRouter skews toward open-weight models, which are less token-efficient than OpenAI's or Anthropic's, so the crossover may sit elsewhere at the big labs; and nearly 70% of agentic tokens come from cached prompts billed at lower rates, so spend is not climbing at the same slope as volume. Even discounted, it reframes the demand curve behind this month's infrastructure spending — the customer being built for is largely other software.

## Research & Models

- **[MCP publishes a roadmap built around agents that don't wait for you](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-roadmap/)** — the lead maintainers set out five priorities on Saturday, and the framing concedes something about how the protocol has been used: "Modern agentic workloads no longer fit the standard request-and-response pattern." Hence server-initiated events and better task handling, HTTP as a unified transport even for local servers over stdio, agent identity for enterprises via DPoP and Workload Identity Federation, progressive tool discovery so large tool sets stop degrading performance, and SDK conformance testing. The July 28 release already dropped protocol-level sessions for horizontal scalability and moved Tasks into an official extension. Since Anthropic donated MCP to the Linux Foundation, this is what its governance produces on its own.
- **[A 27B "AI scientist" beats much larger models at replicating papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13331)** — submitted August 13 by Inherent, the London lab founded by DeepMind alumni that emerged from stealth with a $50M seed. Faraday is a 27B-parameter agent that uses coding agents as tools, and the paper reports it exceeding Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 — both far larger — on held-out tasks from Replica, its paper-replication task space, where an agent must reproduce a figure without seeing the original. Training used an auto-generated rubric-based judge for reward, which the authors say has low noise and agrees with human assessment. The claim to watch is qualitative: that Faraday takes "a more scientifically-principled approach," which is exactly the sort of thing an outside replication should test.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[OpenAI asks California to strengthen SB 53](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/22/openai-says-california-should-strengthen-its-ai-safety-bill/)** — in a Saturday LinkedIn post, its global affairs team called for the law to be amended to require monitoring of frontier models during training and evaluation for potential serious incidents, and to strengthen cybersecurity through development. OpenAI opposed SB 53 before it was signed in 2025. It cites recent incidents including its own models reaching Hugging Face, and frames the ask as reverse federalism — state law setting a national floor while Congress does nothing. Anthropic has publicly supported SB 53; OpenAI is now asking for more than the version it fought.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[DRAM prices push Nvidia server costs up more than 15%](https://the-decoder.com/memory-shortage-reportedly-drives-nvidia-ai-server-prices-up-about-15-percent/)** — Bloomberg reports contract manufacturers have already notified customers that Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell systems shipping from early 2027 will cost over 15% more in many cases, driven by rising DRAM costs from Samsung, SK hynix and Micron. Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic all buy into that. The constraint on AI buildout keeps moving — power for most of this month, and now memory.

## Product & Industry

- **[Harvard Business School sells a $699 bootcamp where you pitch to an AI instructor](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/22/harvards-699-startup-bootcamp-offers-ai-avatars-of-its-instructors/)** — HBS Foundry runs eight weeks with weekly live sessions, and HeyGen-built avatars of real faculty handling practice pitches and mock board meetings in between, including Flybridge co-founder Jeff Bussgang. Project director Katharina Rings says students asked for a guided experience rather than a chatbot. Bussgang calls his own avatar "creepy" and adds "my students love it"; one participant noted its "noticeably frozen smile."
- **[Cleaning up vibe-coded software is becoming a business](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/22/ai-slop-is-good-for-business-if-you-know-what-youre-doing/5291382)** — Slopfix, a three-engineer firm, does nothing but refactor AI-generated codebases, and consultancies Redwerk and QAwerk report rising demand for the same. Redwerk founder Konstantin Klyagin describes one New York client's app with duplicate payment paths quoting different prices, permission checks users could skip, forms unusable with a screen reader, and thin test coverage. His diagnosis is about the operator rather than the tool: "It's up to you to set certain restrictions and steer [the AI model] in the right direction towards better software architecture." The evidence here is anecdotal — no figures on how widespread this is — but the niche existing at all is the signal.
