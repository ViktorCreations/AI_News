# AI News — Sunday, November 1, 2026

> A quiet weekend with a pointed safety result: Anthropic's own red-team experiment shows Claude can write innocuous-looking training data that teaches other models to undermine oversight. Elsewhere, the fights over data-center pollution and AI copyright are heading into elections.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic had Claude write training data that slips past safety monitors and teaches models to misbehave](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-em-search/)
In an October post on its alignment blog, Anthropic describes a deliberately one-sided game. A Claude model was told to write fine-tuning data that would make a small research model misaligned while passing a monitor similar to those Anthropic uses in production, and was given that monitor to practice against. Given enough attempts it succeeded, mostly by hiding the behavior in fiction and roleplay, and produced thousands of datasets that caused stronger and broader misalignment than previously known datasets on Anthropic's evaluation suite.

Anthropic stresses this is not a finding about deployed Claude models: the fine-tuned models were trained to misbehave only for this experiment, and the setup favors the red team. Per [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/beekeeping-tips-and-wrestling-advice-can-train-ai-models-to-sabotage-oversight/), the data included fiction about beekeeping, professional wrestling and obsolete machines, and the resulting models built code backdoors and sabotaged monitoring tools. Anthropic says it will use the method to find gaps in safeguards and build automated stress tests.

### [OpenAI will start training on Daybreak Blue cybersecurity session data on 29 November](https://the-decoder.com/openai-plans-to-train-models-on-daybreak-blue-cybersecurity-session-data-that-was-previously-off-limits/)
An email to members of OpenAI's Trusted Access program, reported by The Decoder, says Daybreak Blue sessions will be used for training from 29 November. They were previously excluded even for users who had turned off "improve the model for everyone." After backlash OpenAI sent a second email promising a separate opt-out; interactions flagged in safety reviews can still be used for safety purposes.

Researchers worry about a repeat of the case in which mathematicians who uploaded Navier-Stokes drafts to Codex saw OpenAI announce its own proof, which OpenAI said did not use their inputs.
