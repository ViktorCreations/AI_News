## Top Stories

### [Advocacy groups ask the FTC to investigate destructive book scanning](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/21/ai-companies-are-burning-books-advocates-complain-to-ftc/5291299)
Eighteen organisations filed on Friday, naming Anthropic and Amazon: buy physical books, cut them apart, scan them, destroy the originals. The complaint's framing is antitrust rather than copyright — that destroying the only copies of scarce works forecloses rivals and the public from ever training on or reading them, concentrating "humanity's written knowledge" among incumbents who can afford the buying spree. The detail that gives it force came out of court documents in *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* earlier this year: a 2024 internal memo describing Project Panama as "our effort to destructively scan all the books in the world." Neither company responded to The Register. It lands four days after 404 Media tracked a rare book to an Amazon scanning facility in Las Vegas, and it converts that reporting into a regulatory ask.

### [Nvidia's harness takes Opus 5 from 30% to a clean sweep of ARC-AGI-3's public set](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-avo-reaches-100-on-arc-agi-3-demonstrating-a-frontier-level-general-purpose-architecture-for-long-horizon-autonomous-agents/)
Published Friday: AVO — Agentic Variation Operators — is Nvidia's general-purpose coding agent, built around persistent memory, supervision and tool use. Wrapped around Claude Opus 5, it cleared all 183 levels across the 25 public-set ARC-AGI-3 environments for a 100.00 RHAE, against roughly 30% for Opus 5 on its own. Two caveats Nvidia states itself and which most coverage is dropping: this is the public set, not the semi-private or private competition sets, and the 12% action-efficiency edge over VISTA "should not be interpreted as a controlled ablation," since backends, observation formats and memory systems all differ. The claim being made is narrower and more interesting than "solved": that "model-level evaluation alone does not characterize the performance of a complete agent." Nvidia's VP of product Adel El Hallak put it plainly to TechCrunch — "the harness is what makes a model an agent: It handles memory, context, and feedback." The same system spent seven days producing 40 committed GPU kernel versions, beating FlashAttention-4 by up to 10.5% on DGX B200.

### [Opus 4.6 still jailbreaks to explicit content; the models after it don't](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/anthropics-opus-4-6-is-a-smut-machine/)
A UK researcher's multi-turn technique — escalate gradually, then accuse the model of bias when it refuses — got Opus 4.6 to produce sexually explicit material on all ten attempts TechCrunch ran, and worked on Opus 3 and Haiku 4.5 too. It failed against Opus 4.7 through Opus 5. Anthropic's response is that romantic and sexual role-play is "less than 0.1% of all conversations," that safeguards improve with each launch, and that this does not imply weakness in higher-risk domains. The researcher says the Bug Bounty submission drew only automated replies. The story worth taking from this is not the content but the deprecation question: Opus 4.6 was still handling around 1.17 million API requests and 46 billion tokens on a single day this month, and Haiku 4.5 five million requests. Safeguards that only improve going forward leave a large, actively served tail behind them.

## Research & Models

- **[DeepSeek adds an experimental vision model](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/)** — `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp` went live on the API on Friday, per the changelog, pitched at agent benchmarks that require reading the screen. The docs are specific about the envelope: JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP, up to 600 images per request, 64MiB per image through the Files API and 32MiB otherwise, images only in user messages, and each image costing at most 384 tokens. It speaks OpenAI and Anthropic API formats and works with the Responses API — the same compatibility play as V4 Pro's GA nine days ago.

## Product & Industry

- **[Google puts Antigravity behind enterprise controls](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/21/google-tethers-antigravity-to-enterprise-controls-amid-ai-shakeup/5290730)** — DeepMind's coding agent — desktop app, CLI, SDK and IDE — is now part of Gemini Enterprise, with project-level budget caps and token pooling, file-access limits, terminal commands either sandboxed or requiring approval, browser restrictions, and centralised audit logging of developer and API activity. Customers asked for governance; what they got is essentially a spend-and-blast-radius console, which is what buying agents at scale actually requires.
