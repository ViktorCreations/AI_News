## Top Stories

### [A Microsoft executive called AI training "the largest theft of labor in human history"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-new-unredacted-filings-reveal/)
Newly unsealed material in the three-year-old New York Times copyright suit against OpenAI and Microsoft is the most damaging set of internal quotes yet, and it lands against a fair-use defence that turns on whether the output substitutes for the original. Brent Hecht, Microsoft's director of Applied Science, wrote in a January 2023 memo that this was "an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions" and "the largest theft of labor in human history"; a year later he described Copilot's effect as a "doom loop" that would "hurt the performance of our models and the entire web at the same time," citing Microsoft's own data showing click-through to nytimes.com fell as much as 93% versus traditional Bing search. OpenAI's head of ChatGPT, Nick Turley, wrote that publishers face an "existential threat" from products that are "largely substitutive" and "will get more and more substitutive as they get better." Satya Nadella testified that "anything that is paywalled should be licensed," and that had he known OpenAI trained on paywalled material he would have required it to retrain. On scale: OpenAI's mid-training datasets alone are said to hold more than 91,692 copies of works from the Times, Daily News and Center for Investigative Reporting, a Common Crawl-derived set more than 2 million documents from nytimes.com, and a Microsoft-supplied dataset built under "Project Mango" at least 160,903 unique works. **The necessary caveat, which TechCrunch makes itself: most of this comes from the Times' own brief rather than the underlying exhibits, which stay sealed, so the quotes arrive without their original context.** The Trump administration filed a brief defending OpenAI's unlicensed training earlier this month.

### [Anthropic publishes the numbers on how much AI is building Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)
The most concrete thing to come out of the pacing debate so far: three measurements, with methodology, of what is actually happening inside a frontier lab. On AI building AI, Anthropic scores work on a scale from AL0 (no AI involvement) to AL5 (fully autonomous, no human in the loop) and reports that Claude "leads" 26% of its AI R&D work, that more than 90% sits at or above "AI collaborates," and — the number that matters for recursive self-improvement — that Claude is not operating fully autonomously on any measured subset. On oversight, it analysed over a billion agent decisions during August 2026 and found 0.002% blocked by its monitor, roughly one in 47,000, with one to two transcripts per thousand flagged for human review. On compute, a snapshot of the week of July 13–20 shows about 6% of the compute going to AI R&D was allocated to safety, rising to about 12% of the compute going to *AI-driven* AI R&D. Anthropic also states plainly that it plans to embed independent third-party evaluators from multiple organisations, with access to internal processes, systems and data "comparable to what internal risk assessment teams have" — which is Amodei's September proposal becoming a commitment, a day after the evaluators themselves said they would want it legislated. Publishing 6% is a choice; whether that number is good or alarming is now an argument everyone can have with a figure in front of them.

### [US and Chinese experts draft the nuclear red lines before Trump meets Xi](https://the-decoder.com/us-and-china-experts-push-for-shared-rules-banning-ai-control-over-nuclear-weapons/)
Melanie Sisson of Brookings and Tianjiao Jiang of Fudan University published joint proposals ahead of the September 24 summit, and their specificity is the point: no AI system should be able to launch nuclear weapons on its own or attack nuclear command systems, humans must retain sole control over AI-driven cyberattacks on strategic infrastructure, and the two countries should agree a shared definition of "human control" — the phrase that otherwise means whatever each side wants. It builds on the Biden–Xi understanding of November 2024. Jiang additionally proposes a hotline for AI incidents, on the reasoning that a defensive system reacting automatically to suspicious activity could read as an attack, and someone needs to be able to say *that was an accident* before it escalates. The sceptical note comes from Carla Freeman of Johns Hopkins: during the 2023 spy balloon crisis, China did not pick up the phone. The National Security Commission on AI asked for human control over nuclear release in 2021, and five years on there is still no binding mechanism.

## Product & Industry

- **[Claude Code rebuilds Projects around parallel agents](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-keeps-pushing-claude-code-toward-autonomous-coding-with-new-parallel-agent-workflows/)** — announced Thursday: describe a goal, and a coordinator splits it across parallel "threads," each its own cloud session, each able to open pull requests and run tests, with shared memory accumulating across them and progress trackable per thread or from mobile. Beta for selected Pro and Max subscribers on cloud sessions; Team and Enterprise later, local execution promised. Worth reading against the OpenAI Codex developer we quoted yesterday arguing that more than two parallel sub-agents burn tokens for no quality gain — the two leading labs are shipping in opposite directions on the same question, and the one selling tokens is shipping more parallelism.
- **[OpenAI launches Astra for Law](https://openai.com/index/astra-for-law)** — announced Thursday on OpenAI's newsroom: a legal vertical offering custom firm workflows, connections to legal data sources, and what it calls legal-grade controls for confidential client work. A companion case study describes Cooley building an IPO review tool on ChatGPT Work. The vertical push into regulated professions is the commercial story of this quarter, and it puts model reliability inside a setting where being confidently wrong is a malpractice question.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The FAA's answer to the controller shortage is $875M of AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/the-faas-plan-to-fix-air-traffic-875-million-worth-of-ai/)** — per the Wall Street Journal, the agency will launch SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes, and Trajectories), automated software intended to help air traffic controllers manage workflows and route flights more safely, against a persistent national shortage of controllers. Whatever the merits, it is a large public deployment of automation into a safety-critical system where the failure mode is not an embarrassing chatbot answer.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Crusoe raises $3.9B at a $30.9B valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/crusoe-raises-3-9b-to-build-massive-data-centers-and-small-modular-ai-factories/)** — the Series F, announced Thursday, was co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, the Qatar Investment Authority, Radical Ventures and TPG also in. It funds existing sites including the large Abilene, Texas campus used by OpenAI, plus "Spark" — modular AI factories built in Crusoe's own facilities, moved by truck and plugged into large power sources. The modular bet is partly an answer to the community opposition now dogging large complexes: smaller units, less construction labour, fewer hearings. New board members include Cloudflare CFO Thomas Seifert and Redwood Materials CEO JB Straubel.
- **[Mazama Energy raises $135M to drill for supercritical geothermal](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/khosla-backed-mazama-energy-just-raised-135m-to-drill-deeper-into-super-hot-rock-geothermal/)** — an oversubscribed Series B for horizontal wells in super-hot rock, each intended to generate 15MW. Mazama has drilled past 10,000 feet in 15 days en route to roughly 15,000 feet, where 750˚F (400˚C) heat turns water supercritical, and now puts its first Oregon site's potential at 10GW — double the 5GW that investor Vinod Khosla cited last year. Generation is meant to start next year. Set against Wednesday's BloombergNEF forecast of 18 billion cubic feet of gas a day for data centers by 2035, enhanced geothermal is the quiet alternative that would make that forecast wrong.
