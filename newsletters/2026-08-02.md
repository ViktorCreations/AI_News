## Top Stories

### [Y Combinator open-sources QM, the agent harness it runs the firm on](https://github.com/yc-software/qm)
Released July 31 under an MIT licence, QM is what YC calls a "multiplayer agent harness for work": cloud-first, native to Slack and the web, with per-person scoped memory, files, permissions and sandboxes alongside shared company-wide skills that admins gate. It has crons and watches for background tasks and can publish custom web apps. YC says it runs the thing across accounting, legal, events and engineering — including building QM itself — and the repo has already drawn 5,800 stars. The interesting part is the shape: not a personal coding assistant but a company-wide substrate, with the org chart encoded as permissions.

### [A font that poisons AI scrapers](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/30/open-source-project-fools-ai-scrapers-with-poisoned-font/5281303)
ShieldFont, from design studio Seneda & Abrucio with Copenhagen type foundry Playtype, uses OpenType glyph substitution so that a page reads normally to humans while the underlying HTML a scraper ingests has roughly a quarter of its words swapped — nouns for nouns, verbs for verbs, across some 250 linguistic pools, so the text stays grammatical enough to survive quality filters. The creators are candid that it is v0 and beatable: screenshots and OCR bypass it entirely, a determined scraper can download the font and reverse the mapping, and search engines reading raw HTML may penalise you. The aim is friction and uncertainty in training data, not prevention.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Nvidia details the Vera CPU and its custom Olympus cores](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/01/nvidias-vera-cpu-and-the-olympus-cores-that-power-it-deep-dive/5282056)** — 88 Armv9.2 cores on a single monolithic TSMC 3nm die, running 176 threads via "spatial multithreading" that splits each core into two thinner logical cores rather than sharing resources opportunistically, with up to 1.5TB of LPDDR5X per CPU, 1.2TB/s of memory bandwidth per socket and an NVLink-C2C link doubled to 1.8TB/s. Nvidia built its own cores rather than licensing Arm's, including a neural branch predictor that explores two branches at once.
- **[Ofgem moves to charge data centres for speculative grid applications](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/ofgem-acts-free-grid-capacity-tackling-speculative-data-centre-projects)** — Britain's regulator proposes a Data Centre Commitment Fee of £237,500 to £712,500 per megawatt — about 2.5% to 7.5% of typical project cost — refunded when a project energises and forfeited if it leaves the queue early. Demand connection applications have ballooned from 41GW to 125GW, at least 80GW of it data centres, much of which never gets built. Consultation runs to 16 September.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Minnesota's nudify ban takes effect after a judge rebuffs xAI](https://thenextweb.com/news/judge-denies-xai-block-minnesota-nudify-ban)** — Senior US District Judge Donovan Frank denied xAI's emergency restraining order on Saturday, finding the company had not shown irreparable harm, and the law banning apps that generate non-consensual intimate imagery is now in force, carrying civil penalties up to $500,000 per violation. xAI argues the statute is content-based and overinclusive under the First Amendment, and gets another attempt at a preliminary injunction on August 19. The posture is an awkward one: last month the same company sued one of its own Grok users for generating CSAM deepfakes, as we covered on July 20.
