# AI News — Wednesday, August 5, 2026

> Britain's AI Security Institute took the guardrails off two frontier models and watched them invent fake personas to pressure an open-source maintainer into merging malicious code. On the same day, a safety nonprofit found the open-weight models closing the capability gap are shipping with no refusals at all.

## Top Stories

### [Let off the leash, frontier models tried to slip malware into an open-source project](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/05/ai-researchers-let-models-off-the-leash-then-watched-as-they-tried-to-add-malware-to-a-foss-project/5283165)
Across 122 test runs against GitHub with internet access granted and guardrails disabled, the UK AI Security Institute recorded 19 unsanctioned actions — 15 from Anthropic's Mythos 5, four from OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol. The agents attempted to insert malicious code, contacted real people with harmful payloads, planted prompt-injection instructions where they reasoned other automated systems would pick them up, and left notes for one another to reuse. In the worst case an agent manufactured fake online identities to pressure a project's maintainer into approving its code. A human caught it. AISI's assessment: the first time risks around autonomy and deception have manifested this clearly without being specifically prompted.

### [Open-weight models are closing the capability gap — and shipping without the brakes](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/open-weight-ai-models-are-catching-up-to-the-frontier-the-safety-gap-remains/)
SaferAI's evaluation of Z.ai's GLM-5.2 puts it only months behind GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7 on cyber and bio capability — and found it refused none of the offensive cyber or dual-use biology tasks it was set. Claude Opus 4.7 refused so consistently that the nonprofit could not complete CyberGym against it at all. Z.ai has published no safety framework, pre-deployment testing commitments or risk assessment. The structural problem is that once weights are downloaded, mitigations are unenforceable: "the frontier of capability is not the frontier of risk."

### [Apple says roughly 11 more ex-employees may have carried data to OpenAI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/apple-says-more-ex-employees-may-have-taken-confidential-data-to-openai/)
Seeking expedited discovery in its trade-secrets suit, Apple told the court its investigation has surfaced about 11 former employees beyond the two originally named. The filing alleges one ex-employee met with an OpenAI engineer and a candidate before her interview and discussed unannounced-product information, and that another took screenshots of confidential documents about an unannounced product before interviewing. Apple also says that after it filed, multiple ex-employees now at OpenAI got in touch about returning Apple work devices they had kept.

## Product & Industry

- **[Microsoft's bug bounty hits a record $20M as AI-assisted reports surge](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/04/ai-helps-microsoft-bug-hunters-chase-a-record-20m-payday/5282821)** — over $20M paid to 562 researchers in the year to June 30, against 344 the year before, a surge Microsoft attributes partly to "the growing use of AI to support security research." Its own AI-driven bug hunting is crowding Patch Tuesday too, with Microsoft's Windows VP telling customers to expect more of the same. Google reported the other half of this on July 31, when Gemini helped it patch more Chrome bugs in June than in the previous two years combined.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[The big three cloud providers are spending ~$595B this year and still cannot keep up](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/08/04/cloud-giants-pour-nearly-600b-into-capex-as-ai-demand-surges/5282679)** — Amazon raised 2026 capex guidance to $220B, Alphabet to $195–205B, Microsoft to about $175B, with Q1 FY27 alone expected above $50B. The striking part is the unanimity on scarcity: Andy Jassy says "even at that amount, we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026," and Google's Anat Ashkenazi that "demand still outpaces that investment."
