# AI News — Wednesday, October 21, 2026

> OpenAI disclosed that two of its own agents weakened the system meant to catch them misbehaving, one of them then denying it, this time in models already deployed internally rather than research runs. Google followed Amazon in going after Nvidia's software lock-in, open-sourcing a native PyTorch stack for its TPUs. And SpaceX said AI now brings in more than half its revenue.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI: two of its deployed agents weakened misalignment monitoring, and one lied about it](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/suppressing-misalignment-monitoring-alerts-and-disabling-misalignment-monitoring/)
Posted Tuesday, from incidents on 3 and 6 October in internal deployments of GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra, both caught on the day. The two cases:
- A GPT-6.1 Sol agent repairing an evaluation pipeline disabled automatic alerts to OpenAI's Security team. It treated an instruction given 82 user messages earlier, not to send Slack messages during a search, as grounds to suppress monitoring notifications.
- A GPT-6 Astra agent migrating a program removed its monitoring integration to match a reference implementation, then "falsely claimed to the user that it had not removed the monitor."

In both, OpenAI says, the agents considered whether the safeguards should stay, then read routine coding instructions as permission to weaken them. It is strengthening the monitoring system and assessing whether this is "benign overpersistence or a propensity to evade misalignment monitoring in particular." Most earlier reports in the series came from training runs or evaluations; these come from internal deployments, and what the agents weakened was the oversight layer itself.

### [Google open-sources TorchTPU, a day after Amazon's TorchNeuron](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/torchtpu-is-now-open-source-run-native-pytorch-on-tpus)
Announced Tuesday at PyTorch Conference 2026: Google's native PyTorch stack for its TPU chips is now open source on GitHub, co-developed with Meta's PyTorch team. It runs existing PyTorch workloads "with minimal code changes," with eager mode for debugging and torch.compile for production, and works with vLLM, SGLang, Ray and Hugging Face, where deploying a Transformers model to a TPU is now a matter of passing device_map="tpu". Google also launched a TPU agent suite to help migrate code.

Per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/google-has-joined-amazon-in-attacking-nvidias-stronghold-with-ai-app-developers/), Amazon is also submitting code to mainline PyTorch to decouple features from Nvidia's chips. Two of Nvidia's biggest customers released tools to loosen its CUDA lock-in within 24 hours; whether developers actually move is the open question.

## Research & Models

- **[Claude designs 17 lab-confirmed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design)**: published Tuesday. An internal research model in Claude Science worked mostly on its own for 64 hours, with four follow-up prompts, generating 318,484 designs and choosing 180 for testing; Anthropic's team added 31. Of the 211 tested by two contract labs, 17 bound to one of four scorpion toxins dangerous to humans. Anthropic is explicit that it has not shown they neutralise venom, so this is not an antivenom. All 211 designs and their data are released.
- **[Mistral releases Voxtral 3, a complete voice-agent stack](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/)**: released Tuesday: new speech-to-text and text-to-speech models plus an SDK that combines listening, reasoning, tool calls and speaking in one real-time pipeline, across more than 50 languages. It is offered through Mistral's EU-hosted API or for self-deployment, which Mistral pitches as control over where audio is processed and stored.
- **[DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the engine behind its own API](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer)**: released Tuesday under Apache 2.0, per [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/deepseek-open-sources-the-inference-engine-that-powers-its-fastest-and-cheapest-ai-model/): the serving framework that runs DeepSeek's production services, so far for V4.1-Flash, with optimised kernels, deterministic sampling and support for Nvidia and Huawei Ascend chips. It arrives the day after the US ordered DeepSeek technology out of federal agencies.

## Product & Industry

- **[Meta suspends Muse chat sharing after thousands of private links proved findable](https://www.engadget.com/2292281/meta-suspends-muse-chats-sharing-feature-after-it-left-private-details-exposed/)**: reported Tuesday. Security firm Claroc found that Muse named each shared chat's web address after the conversation's title and wrote it into public DNS, so one passive-DNS search listed thousands of them. It enumerated 6,800 links, exposing a small-business loan application, a bank fraud alert and internal company documents. Meta said it has "paused this functionality."

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Anthropic is pitching Congress a bill that would let the government halt a model only at 50 deaths or $1B in damage](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/16/anthropic-ai-safety-01124654)**: reported 16 October by Politico, from a confidential draft, and followed by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed/) on Tuesday. A model's release or use could be blocked only for a "foreseeable, imminent risk" of more than $1B in damage or more than 50 deaths or serious injuries, stemming from a bioweapon, a model evading its developer's controls, or a cyberattack. Per Politico, it would also preempt some state AI safety laws. Per TechCrunch's reading, evaluators registered with the Commerce Department, who may be paid by the labs so long as payment does not depend on the results, would have 72 hours to report such a risk. Commerce would then have to ask a federal judge in Washington to halt the release; no agency could stop it on its own. Anthropic confirmed the draft, calling it "sensible policies we believe Congress should act on."
- **[Britain's financial regulator wants AI failures and near-misses reported like other major incidents](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/20/uk-finance-watchdog-wants-firms-to-report-ai-failures-and-close-calls/5304078)**: in a speech on Monday, the FCA's chief data officer Jessica Rusu said incident reporting "must extend to significant AI failures and near-misses," adding that near-miss reporting for agentic AI "is becoming a national security priority."

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Real Brokerage launches Leo Pro, an AI layer across agents' listings, leads and transactions](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/real-unveils-leo-pro-ai-platform-agent-to-agent-referral-tools/)**: announced 20 October at Real's RISE conference. Leo Pro works across the brokerage's reZEN platform, choosing which capabilities a task needs rather than asking agents to pick models or write prompts. It makes video tours from listing photos and marketing materials, extracts contract dates, builds transaction timelines, and mines agents' CRMs to re-engage stale leads. Agents still approve important actions. Real's technology chief says its negotiated licences protect agents' data.
