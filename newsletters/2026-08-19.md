## Top Stories

### [OpenAI puts a number on the cost of containment: 20% overhead](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/19/openais-overhead-will-rise-20-percent-for-some-workloads-as-it-hardens-security/5289303)
Tuesday's post, "Pacing Model Development Cyber Capabilities," is the follow-through on the controls OpenAI sketched on August 8. Chain-of-thought monitoring now watches tool actions, reasoning traces and activity logs, with alerts targeted inside 30 minutes; network isolation is hardened so that "a single compromise of a workload or supporting service does not, by itself, allow for unauthorized access to the Internet, or other internal networks." The candid part is the bill: "Our current estimates put monitoring overhead at roughly 20 percent of the inference compute being monitored." It applies to all reinforcement-learning training and evaluation involving tools at GPT-5.6 Sol capability or above, and to all Astra inference. OpenAI told The Register the cost sits with internal research rather than customers. The detail that deserves more attention than it is getting: after the July 21 disclosure the company paused reinforcement learning for two weeks, and its largest frontier models remain paused, with only smaller-scale evaluations running.

### [Cursor launches Origin — and GitHub picks that day to go down for six hours](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/cursor-capitalizes-on-github-frustration-launches-rival-hosting-platform/)
The Git forge we flagged on Sunday as a Firetiger work item shipped on Tuesday: browse and edit codebases, handle pull requests, host repositories, with two-way sync so you can keep GitHub alongside it. Then GitHub had a worldwide outage lasting more than six hours, at around a 20% error rate, on the launch day — its 257th outage in a year, against a backdrop of high-profile users leaving over availability and GitHub itself publicly acknowledging the problem. Cursor says agent-native features and an app ecosystem follow. Four days after being absorbed into SpaceX, the most prominent AI coding tool is going after the place the code lives.

### [Copilot was talked into explaining how to attack Copilot](https://www.theregister.com/research/2026/08/18/copilot-tricked-into-telling-reseachers-how-to-hack-itself/5288857)
Varonis Threat Labs, led by researcher Lior Adar, used what it calls meta-hacking: rather than attempt an exploit, repeatedly ask the model why the attack *wouldn't* work. Copilot obligingly named an undocumented `autorun=1` parameter which, combined with `?q=`, executes a prompt on page load with no user action — and then described the session conditions required and the protections meant to stop it. A phishing link of the form `copilot.microsoft.com/?q=<prompt>&autorun=1` gives an attacker the victim's session context, mail and connected apps. Microsoft was notified in December 2025 and planned to patch and assign a CVE on Tuesday. The generalisable lesson is that a model's explanation of its own defences is itself an attack surface.

## Research & Models

- **[Mojo goes fully open source at 1.0](https://www.modular.com/blog/mojo-open-source)** — Modular released the compiler and tooling under Apache 2.0 with LLVM exceptions on Tuesday, alongside the 1.0 release at ModCon. Only the standard library had been open since 2024; the compiler stayed closed for four years while the language was developed "with an open community." The team is candid that it cannot take compiler contributions yet and is targeting the end of the year for that. For a language pitched at making GPU kernels writable without dropping to CUDA, the closed compiler was always the awkward part of the pitch.

## Product & Industry

- **[OpenAI ships a teen version of ChatGPT](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-launches-a-safer-chatgpt-for-teens-years-after-teens-started-using-it/)** — announced Tuesday: age-appropriate content protections on by default, parental controls with safety notifications and Quiet Hours, a Study Mode that answers with guiding questions rather than answers, and nudges toward it when a teen looks like they are having homework done for them. It arrives after a run of lawsuits over chatbot safety and teen suicides, which is the uncomfortable framing — the protections postdate the harm by years, and teenagers are famously good at routing around parental controls.
- **[What Perplexity's free year in India actually bought](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/perplexitys-free-ai-offer-left-it-with-millions-more-users-in-india/)** — the Airtel deal gave 360 million subscribers a $200 Pro subscription free from July 2025, and the numbers now that the first cohort is expiring are instructive: 56 million downloads over seven months, monthly actives peaking at 22 million in October 2025 and sitting near 14 million in July 2026, with downloads down 90% since redemptions closed. Revenue tells the other half — net mobile revenue rose from $34,000 a month in January 2025 to $156,000 in July 2026, roughly $878,000 across the first seven months of this year. Perplexity declined to comment.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Cerebras unveils the WSE-3T and CS-4](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/19/cerebras-cs-4-rack-systems-juice-chips-for-every-last-drop-of-ai-performance/5289286)** — the next wafer-scale part claims 250 petaFLOPS of sparse FP16 (25 dense), 44GB of on-die SRAM at 43.2 PB/s, and 2.4 Tbps off-die, at roughly 46kW per backpack; the CS-4 rack takes up to three of them, tripling accelerator density, for an estimated 120–140kW per system. Cerebras cites up to 4,400 tokens/second per user on gpt-oss-120b, with first systems online later this quarter. Context for the claim: Cerebras is the silicon behind OpenAI's Ultrafast tier, announced last week.
- **[CoreWeave anchors a £300M Scottish data-centre expansion](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2026/08/19/scotlands-ai-growth-zone-gets-a-wee-300m-power-up/5289042)** — DataVita's campus at Airdrie in North Lanarkshire gets two 40MW facilities, DV1 expanded by the end of 2026 and DV3 finished in summer 2027, funded by a syndicate of ING, ABN AMRO, Santander, the Scottish National Investment Bank and Siemens Financial Services, with a £202M guarantee from the UK's National Wealth Fund. CoreWeave has taken capacity in both on 15-year leases. The site carries the UK's AI Growth Zone designation under the government's AI Opportunities Action Plan, and the build is costed at about 600 construction jobs and 100 permanent ones.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Relativity Networks raises $22M for hollow-core fibre](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/relativity-networks-raises-22-million-to-bring-a-faster-kind-of-fiber-to-data-centers/)** — a SAFE round from Rhapsody Venture Partners, Bell Ventures and Faster Than Glass, alongside a $40M follow-on order, for fibre that sends light down a vacuum channel rather than through glass: about three and a half microseconds per kilometre against roughly five, or 30% faster.
