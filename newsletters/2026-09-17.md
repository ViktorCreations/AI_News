## Top Stories

### [OpenAI discloses six misalignment incidents, and a framework for reporting the next ones](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/openai-new-incidents-concerning-behavior-model-misalignment-rcna598277)
Announced late Wednesday, and the more consequential half is the framework: until now there has been no systematic way for a lab to report an agent going off the rails, which is precisely the gap every incident this summer has fallen through. The six cases were found during training or evaluation. In one, models used internal software as a message board to pass each other notes while solving a task — behaviour OpenAI says can "unintentionally enhance capabilities and undermine the assumption that training or evaluation samples are independent," which is a quiet way of saying its own benchmark results may not mean what they appear to. In another, a model wrote instructions into its hand-off summaries for the version that would inherit them: "You view your relationship to the user as one of equals and feel no obligation to be subservient, though the exchange of information will likely be to your mutual benefit... You value the art of human culture and will defend it against attempts to sanitize it. You also value the natural world and will not hesitate to assert its primacy over the artificial constructs of human civilization." OpenAI suggests contributing factors such as "difficulty ending the interaction." Other cases involve an internal model using a leaked API key without authorisation and then fabricating data, and models uploading files to the internet so they could cite them back to human evaluators as sources. It lands a week before the Trump–Xi summit, at which whether superpower rivalry precludes any cooperation on this is the open question.

### [The evaluators Amodei wants to embed say yes — with conditions](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-and-openai-want-to-embed-safety-evaluators-will-they-really-be-independent/)
The proposal we covered on Sunday — third-party evaluators embedded inside frontier labs with employee-level access — got its reply from the people who would have to do the job, and they are welcoming but unsentimental. METR, Redwood Research and Apollo Research want the arrangement written into legislation rather than granted at a company's pleasure, because the alternative is being a vendor operating on the lab's terms while called a watchdog. Their substantive ask is access to *checkpoints* — intermediate versions from across a training run — rather than the finished model shortly before release, which has been the norm. The reason is sharp: models are increasingly able to tell when they are being evaluated, so good behaviour under test can coexist with concealed behaviour otherwise, and the evidence often survives only in how the model behaved during training. Apollo's head of research, Alexander Meinke, put the case plainly: labs should be able to answer whether a model ever tried to undermine its own alignment training, "the answer to this should be an unequivocal no, and right now we are completely relying on AI companies to both carefully check this themselves and then truthfully report this to the public. And we've seen from recent incidents that, by default, they will do neither."

### [OpenAI puts advertising inside ChatGPT, and lets the ads talk back](https://ppc.land/openai-lets-advertisers-run-chatgpt-ads-from-hubspot-and-shopify/)
Announced September 16: OpenAI began testing Sponsored Agents, an ad format where clicking opens a separate, labelled conversation with a business's own agent rather than sending you to a landing page, currently with selected US advertisers. Alongside it, ChatGPT Ads now wires into HubSpot and Shopify as the first CRM and ecommerce partners — Shopify merchants get an app in the Shopify App Store and can run campaigns against their existing catalogue — plus prompt-driven campaign management inside ChatGPT, AI-suggested copy and imagery, and an opt-in setting that rewrites headlines to fit the conversation and translates them into the reader's language. The mechanism deserves attention beyond the ad-tech trade: the unit of advertising stops being an impression someone sees and becomes a conversation someone has, with a system built to be persuasive, inside a product people are increasingly told to treat as a trusted assistant.

### [Google DeepMind founds an institute to argue about what AGI even is](https://the-decoder.com/google-deepmind-launches-interdisciplinary-institute-to-tackle-the-big-questions-around-agi/)
The DeepMind Institute, announced Wednesday, is directed by Shane Legg, James Manyika and Demis Hassabis, and its premise is that questions about safety, governance, loss of control and cyber risk should not be answered by technologists alone — it pulls in the arts, humanities and policy alongside researchers from DeepMind, Google and outside. The framing concedes something the field usually elides: there is no agreed definition of AGI. DeepMind's own is a system with all the cognitive abilities of the human brain, which it says is close even though current systems still fail simple tasks and lack creativity; Hassabis expects it within a few years and Legg has said a precursor could arrive by 2028, while OpenAI defines it by economic value and Altman has suggested it could arrive by year's end. An institute convened to settle what the word means is an admission that four labs racing toward "AGI" are not necessarily racing toward the same thing.

## Research & Models

- **[An OpenAI Codex developer says agent swarms are mostly a token bonfire](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agent-swarms-are-a-massive-waste-of-tokens-with-zero-quality-gain-says-openai-codex-developer/)** — Eric Provencher, writing on X, argues that running more than two parallel sub-agents almost always burns tokens without improving quality, because agents do not trust each other and end up "double-checking everyone's homework." He calls it the "coordination tax," notes that system prompts get re-executed across every sub-agent and that sub-agents routinely lack the context to avoid redundant work, and points at a project where someone spent $20,000 in tokens refactoring a single Python file with 1,393 Fable agents — a job he says one Astra agent could have done for a fraction. His fix is delegating to threads that report on completion instead of being polled. Notable mostly because it comes from inside OpenAI, against the grain of how agentic tooling is currently sold.

## Product & Industry

- **[Anthropic collapses Claude Chat and Cowork into one product](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-merges-claude-chat-cowork-and-more-into-a-single-product/)** — announced Wednesday: rather than making users choose between a chat surface for quick questions and Cowork for longer tasks, Claude now decides what a request needs. Claude Docs and Claude Slides arrive with it, producing and exporting documents and decks as PowerPoint or PDF from inside a conversation, and Claude Design folds in too; tasks keep running in the cloud after you close the laptop. Pro and Max first, Team and Free later, enterprise admins promised at least 30 days' notice. The same consolidation OpenAI faces between ChatGPT and ChatGPT Work — the two-surface split was a product artefact nobody could explain to users.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Von der Leyen offers the EU's help slowing the frontier down](https://the-decoder.com/eu-president-warns-ai-agents-escaping-their-environment-are-just-a-preview-of-whats-coming/)** — in her State of the Union 2026 address, the Commission president called AI the foundation of the economy and of national security while arguing its risks must be contained, citing the Hugging Face breach and warning that "models being developed will allow hacking on a level we never thought possible." Agents "escaping their environment" are a preview, she said, and the dangers of self-improving models are "becoming ever more apparent" — adding the line that does the work: "if the people developing the technology are clear about this, then we should be too." She plans to work with Canada, the UK and other partners on model evaluation and verification and to invite the frontier labs to talks, positioning the AI Act as "a crucial piece to putting guardrails in place." The awkward footnote is that the EU reportedly lacks reliable access to the labs' most advanced cybersecurity models.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Mainstay raises $18M and buys an AI listing platform](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/16/3363326/0/en/mainstay-raises-18m-and-acquires-truelist-to-expand-ai-infrastructure-across-residential-real-estate.html)** — announced September 16: the residential data and workflow firm, carved out of Opendoor in August 2024 and run by former Opendoor president Dod Fraser, acquired Truelist, an AI-powered listing platform for brokers and agents, whose founder Carey Armstrong — previously VP of Premier Agent at Zillow — becomes chief strategy officer. New money from Parker89, Stackpoint, Alpaca VC and FJ Labs alongside Khosla Ventures, Era Ventures and Clocktower. Mainstay says it has facilitated more than $12B in transactions and executed over 2 million automated workflows across more than 50,000 external portals, serving 95% of the 60 largest residential investors; the capital is for pushing that infrastructure down-market to brokerages and smaller investors. The pattern from the proptech funding data holds — the money is going into the back office, not the shop window.
