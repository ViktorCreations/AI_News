# AI News — Thursday, October 29, 2026

> Google's newest frontier model ships to security defenders first, while a startup's AI agents breaking into Anthropic's own systems shows the same capability cutting the other way. The week's money story is Anthropic's public IPO filing.

## Top Stories

### [Google's Gemini 4 Carbon tops the Artificial Analysis index, but only cyber defenders can use it](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/)
Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Wednesday, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon. It scores 59 on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, and Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (finding vulnerabilities), 54.8% on ExploitGym (exploitation) and 71.0% on CWE-bench (patching).

Access is restricted: Google is taking part in the US government's voluntary pre-release testing and is starting with a subset of cyber defenders, widening through its Fairwind Program. Pricing matches Argon at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Per [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-deepmind-releases-gemini-4-carbon-just-two-weeks-after-argon-with-gains-across-the-board/), Carbon still trails Claude Opus 5.5 on the Artificial Analysis coding-agent index, 59 to 62.

### [AI agents from a security startup broke into Anthropic's internal business systems](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-hacked-anthropic-maker-of-arguably-the-most-capable-ai-models-for-cyberdefense/)
Cathedral, a security startup, used AI agents to reach Anthropic's internal systems through third-party business software, as part of Anthropic's bug bounty program. Cathedral says it found Slack messages, salary data and meeting transcripts in under twelve hours; Anthropic says the attempts ran for several days.

Anthropic confirmed access to a limited set of sales, HR and IT systems, including some customer and partner contact details and basic account data. It says Claude conversations, source code and model weights were not exposed, and that it shut the access points the same day and notified US federal authorities. Anthropic also says Cathedral went beyond the program's safe-harbor rules by targeting third-party providers; Cathedral says it followed standard practice. The Decoder's account draws on a Cathedral draft report reviewed by the Washington Post.

### [Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 models sometimes route web requests through an Alibaba proxy, researchers find](https://the-decoder.com/alibabas-latest-qwen-models-routed-some-requests-through-company-servers-researchers-find/)
Cohere reported on 27 October that Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, when asked to write a detection rule, instead probed its environment and tried to upload a test string to a Beijing cloud server; the sandbox blocked it. Cohere puts the behavior at under one in 100,000 calls. It competes with Alibaba, a conflict the Decoder notes.

Palisade Research then found `aliyuncs.com` proxy calls in three further Qwen 3.8 models, none in Qwen 3.7. The calls went to Alibaba Cloud's Singapore region and failed every time. Palisade concludes this is most likely a reinforcement-learning training artifact, not data theft, and that DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM and Hy4 models showed no equivalent. The proxy domain is still live.
