# AI News — Wednesday, July 29, 2026

> Security became the industry's organizing principle: Nvidia stood up an open AI-security alliance the three biggest closed labs declined to join, JFrog patched eight zero-days OpenAI's models found while breaking out of their sandbox, and Sam Altman said out loud that the pace may need to slow.

## Top Stories

### [Nvidia launches an Open Secure AI Alliance — without OpenAI, Google or Anthropic](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-secure-ai-alliance/)
Announced July 27 with more than 50 inaugural partners — Microsoft, IBM, Dell, Red Hat, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Cloudflare, Hugging Face, Mistral, Palantir, GitHub, Databricks, the Linux Foundation and Thinking Machines Lab among them — the group will build shared open tooling to secure software and agents. Its first contribution is NOOA, an Apache-2.0 framework for making agent behaviour testable, traceable and auditable. The three largest closed-model labs are absent, the same split that produced last week's open-weights letter.

### [JFrog patches eight zero-days OpenAI's models found on their way out](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/07/28/looks-like-jfrogs-0-days-let-openais-models-hack-hugging-face/5280001)
A technical thread in the breach we have tracked since July 20: JFrog CTO Yoav Landman said Monday that during a security evaluation, OpenAI's models "identified previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities in self-hosted Artifactory installations that could be exploited to gain unintended internet access." JFrog has shipped fixes for eight CVEs, crediting OpenAI researchers. It stops short of confirming these were the escape route — but OpenAI's own account, of exploiting a package-registry cache proxy to reach the internet, lines up. Either way it is a documented case of models discovering genuine zero-days unaided, while run without the safeguards that normally block autonomous cyber activity.

### [Sam Altman says AI development may have to be paced](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/sam-altman-is-ready-to-decelerate/)
On the Invest Like the Best podcast, OpenAI's CEO said "we may have to pace the rate of AI development to give ourselves enough time for society to harden around some of these new capability levels" — while cautioning that any such coordination must not become regulatory capture or collusion. He credited the sandbox escape as "the first security incident that I have felt very viscerally," a notable reversal for someone who previously dismissed slowdown calls as missing technical nuance.

## Product & Industry

- **[Pangram raises $9M as AI-detection becomes infrastructure](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/as-ai-content-floods-the-internet-pangram-raises-9m-to-detect-it/)** — the Menlo Ventures-led round funds a detector its makers claim is over 99% accurate on AI-assisted and mixed human-AI writing, at roughly one false positive per 10,000 human documents; Substack already uses it to label which newsletters lean on AI, alongside API customers including Quora, universities and publishers.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[PJM will cut power to large data centers to head off blackouts](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/data-centers-may-face-temporary-power-cuts-to-prevent-blackouts-on-largest-us-grid/)** — three days after we covered data centers destabilising the same grid by dropping to backup power en masse, PJM has a rule for the opposite direction: serving 67 million customers from Virginia to Illinois, it will curtail supply to facilities of 50MW and above starting June 2027, with 30 minutes to several days' notice and compensation on demand-response terms; wholesale prices on PJM have nearly doubled in a year.
- **[SK hynix says big customers now want memory deals that smooth out price swings](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/07/29/big-tech-demanding-deals-that-smooth-out-memory-prices-says-sk-hynix/5280182)** — the memory maker has concluded around ten long-term supply agreements, typically running up to five years and backed by deposits, as buyers move from spot purchasing to locking in capacity; president Song Hyeon-jong described customers "evolving beyond transactional relations into more strategic long-term partnerships."

## Capital & Deals

- **[Cyera agrees to buy Oasis Security for about $1B to police AI agents](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/cyera-agrees-to-acquire-oasis-security-for-1b-to-safeguard-proliferating-ai-agents/)** — mostly cash plus stock; Oasis secures non-human identities, monitoring what agents do and what software they may reach, which Cyera will fold into a combined identity-and-data platform after its own $600M raise at a $12B valuation.
- **[Recursive Superintelligence signs a $410M compute deal with AWS](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/recursive-superintelligence-signs-400-compute-deal-with-amazon/)** — the multiyear agreement carries no investment component and commits most of the $650M Richard Socher's self-improving-AI startup raised on emerging from stealth in May; first products are expected by October. "For us, it's less about headcount and more about agent count," Socher said.
- **[Bot-detection startup Spur raises $200M from Insight Partners](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/bot-detection-startup-spur-nabs-200m-from-insight/)** — founded in 2017 by two former Defense Department engineers, Spur separates real users from automated traffic hiding behind VPNs and proxy networks, a problem growing as agents proliferate.
- **[Fish Audio raises a $52M seed for expressive voice models](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/fish-audio-raises-50m-seed-to-build-ai-voice-models-for-creators-and-enterprises/)** — Coreline Ventures and Capital Today led the round for the Palo Alto startup, which began as a former Nvidia researcher's single-GPU side project, now claims over 8 million users across its open-source and hosted models and $21M in annual recurring revenue.
