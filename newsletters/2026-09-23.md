# AI News — Wednesday, September 23, 2026

> The two companies that spent this month arguing for a coordinated slowdown shipped new frontier models ninety minutes apart yesterday, both at roughly half the price of what they replace. Whatever pacing the frontier turns out to mean, it does not yet mean fewer models, later models, or dearer ones.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic ships Opus 5.5, its first model since Amodei called for slowing down](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-releases-opus-5-5-with-lower-prices-and-fable-level-performance/)
Released Tuesday, two months after Opus 5 arrived on July 24, and Anthropic claims a new state of the art in coding and knowledge work — beating the larger Fable model on many benchmarks and completing informal tasks Fable could not. Output tokens drop to $20 per million from $25, with similar cuts elsewhere and faster serving, reflecting a genuine fall in the compute needed to run it. It also talks differently: less jargon, important information first. Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 follow "in the coming weeks." On safety, Opus 5.5 is judged comparable to Mythos on biology and cybersecurity, so it ships under the same restrictions as Fable — limits on finding exploits in compiled binaries and on work toward recognisable biological weapons — with pre-release evaluation by METR and Frontier Design, and training broadly similar to its predecessors. That last detail is the one worth holding onto. Eleven days ago Amodei wrote that "fully addressing the risks requires even more prudence... not just investing in risk prevention, but pacing the rate of capabilities advancement so that risk prevention has time to keep up." The first model released under that doctrine arrived on schedule, cheaper and more capable, with safety training its maker describes as broadly unchanged.

### [OpenAI answers ninety minutes later, at half the price](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/)
GPT-6 Sol and Luna extend the Astra generation downward — Sol for complex work like coding, Luna for "high-volume tasks with a clear goal, like summarizing documents, extracting information, or answering quick questions." The headline is cost: both land at half the price of the 5.6 versions, which OpenAI attributes to caching and inference improvements passed through to users. Sol is now $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output; Luna $0.10 and $0.50, which puts a frontier-lab model into the price band of cheap open-weight models. Terra, previously the cheapest tier, is simply gone. OpenAI's substantive claim is about reliability rather than capability: "On our internal factuality evaluation, which is based on de-identified real-world conversations where users flagged mistakes by our models, GPT-6 Sol makes about half as many mistakes as its predecessor, reaching Astra-level reliability at much lower cost." The Decoder's read is blunter — prices halved, performance barely moved — and both readings can be true, because this release is about unit economics, not the frontier. The ninety-minute gap after Anthropic's launch is not coincidence, and it is the clearest evidence yet of what the pacing debate has and has not changed.

### [OpenAI says the math model did it in a month](https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-its-internal-model-solved-over-100-long-standing-math-problems-after-just-a-month-of-training/)
A detail that reframes yesterday's advisory-group announcement: the internal model credited with more than 100 long-standing open problems reached that point after roughly a month of training. The advisory group at the Institute for Advanced Study does include a Fields Medallist, Timothy Gowers, and will advise on how results are shared with researchers and the public — but, as we noted yesterday, OpenAI has explicitly excluded the pace of its mathematical research from that remit. The mathematicians' objection was that results are arriving faster than the field can absorb, attribute or verify them. "A month of training" is the number that makes the objection concrete, and it sits alongside a governance structure designed not to address it.

## Research & Models

- **[Xiaomi tops the open-model rankings, and Anthropic says Claude helped](https://the-decoder.com/xiaomis-affordable-flagship-ai-leads-the-open-models-and-anthropic-says-claude-helped-get-it-there/)** — MiMo-V2.6-Pro leads current open-model rankings at far lower cost than its rivals, a jump Xiaomi credits to expanded reinforcement learning, and it has released its tooling and training tasks openly, which is more than most claimants to "open" manage. The complication arrived with it: Anthropic accuses Xiaomi of improperly siphoning data through Claude to train the lineup. That is the same allegation Anthropic made against Chinese labs in the 200-million-exchange distillation report we led with on September 11, now attached to a named company shipping a model that tops the leaderboard — which is either the strongest evidence yet for the claim or the clearest demonstration that it does not much matter.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[AI safety reaches the Trump–Xi table tomorrow, with neither side wanting to slow](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/23/trump-xi-meeting-ai-safety-chips-us-china-dialogue.html)** — analysts tell CNBC that AI safety will be a key focus of the September 24 summit, after a month in which the subject went from lab blog posts to a presidential agenda item. The structural problem is the one the Brookings and Fudan researchers set out last Friday when they proposed nuclear red lines: cooperation on frontier safety is being asked for by the same government that is restricting the other side's access to frontier compute, and neither leader has any intention of conceding pace. Expect declaratory language rather than mechanism.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Snorkel AI raises $350M at a $3.5B valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/snorkel-ai-triples-valuation-to-3-5b-as-demand-for-ai-training-data-booms/)** — a Series E led by Insight Partners and S32, nearly tripling the $1.3B mark set 17 months ago, with Addition, Lightspeed, Greylock, GV and Wells Fargo following. Snorkel now sells finished datasets and reinforcement-learning environments rather than labelling software, and reports an annualised run rate of $375M, eighteen times higher than a year ago. TechCrunch adds the caveat that belongs on every number in this sector: peers like Mercor at $2B gross and Handshake at $1B pay roughly 60–70% of top-line revenue straight to the domain experts doing the work, so headline figures overstate the businesses considerably.
