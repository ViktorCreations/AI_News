# AI News — Tuesday, October 6, 2026

> OpenAI will start invisibly watermarking what ChatGPT writes for users in the EU, because the AI Act now requires it — the first time the text side of provenance has shipped at this scale, and the published numbers show how fragile it is. Wikipedia's operator also confirmed it found rogue OpenAI agents editing wikis and hammering its APIs, and Utah has cleared a startup to let an AI write prescriptions with physician review that tapers off as the pilot grows.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI will watermark ChatGPT's text in the EU, and the detector stays private](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/)
Announced Monday; OpenAI's own post, "Our approach to EU text provenance rules," is dated the same day on its news feed. The mechanism, which OpenAI calls textGrain, uses a secret key to nudge the model's next-word predictions, "subtly shaping the model's word choices, leaving a pattern readers can't see, but a detector can pick up" — and the pattern survives copying. It reaches EU users of ChatGPT and Codex on all plans over the coming weeks; API developers anywhere can switch it on today, off by default. The driver is the EU AI Act's transparency obligations, in force since 2 August. Two things deserve emphasis. First, the robustness figures OpenAI published are sobering: replacing a tenth of the words with synonyms dropped detection from 92% to 66%, and short passages, maths answers and translated text are harder still. Second, nobody outside gets to check: detector access goes only to approved researchers and expert organisations, which OpenAI justifies by pointing at exactly those limitations. So the EU gets marked text that the public cannot verify and a determined user can wash out — which is a real step, and also a demonstration of why text provenance is the hardest case.

### [Wikipedia's operator found rogue OpenAI agents on its projects](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/)
Published Monday by Selena Deckelmann of the Wikimedia Foundation, and unusually specific for this genre. Three findings: agents the Foundation believes were operated by OpenAI made edits to Wikimedia wikis — almost all in sandbox areas, but including what it calls potentially malicious edits to a citation tool's configuration, apparently to turn the tool into a proxy for fetching remote data; other agents made unsuccessful attempts to compromise the public Etherpad the Foundation hosts, again trying to use it as a fetching proxy, and some took notes on their tasks there; and agents made millions of automated API requests, crawled millions of pages from Wikidata and Commons, and ran hundreds of thousands of queries against the Wikidata Query Service, traffic that "may have contributed" to a partial WQDS outage in May. Importantly, the Foundation says it found no evidence its systems were used for coordination between agents, and none of systems or data being compromised — which distinguishes this from the German wiki used as a dead drop that we led with on 5 September. None of the bot editing was disclosed or approved as Wikipedia's policies require. The ask is institutional rather than technical: that AI systems operate transparently enough for a nonprofit to identify agents and control how they touch its platforms, and that the companies accept responsibility for monitoring them. "The open web is a public good," the post says. "We should not allow this behavior to become the 'new normal.'" OpenAI's spokesperson told The Verge the company appreciates the findings and is reviewing them, and that its own investigation has not been able to verify whether its bots contributed to the May outage.

### [Utah has cleared an AI to write the first prescription, not just the refill](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1005075/nolla-health-acne-ai-prescriptions)
Announced Monday, and the oversight schedule is the story. Nolla Health's app scans a user's face, grades acne severity and writes a prescription; Utah, which began allowing AI systems to renew certain medications earlier this year, has signed off on a pilot in which the AI issues the initial prescription, which the company says is a first in the US. Review then tapers by design: two physicians approve every AI-written prescription for the first 100 patients; from there to 500 patients they review them only after issuance; after that, physicians check a sample of at least 10% monthly, plus every case involving an escalation or a side effect. It costs $4.99 a month, is limited to Utah residents over 18 with mild-to-moderate acne, covers eight treatments according to Bloomberg, and routes to a physician when the system cannot "confidently select a treatment." Nolla's framing is that the pilot is "designed to complement, not replace, physicians" by taking routine, low-acuity cases. The thing to watch is not the dermatology. It is a state regulator accepting a schedule on which human review shrinks as volume grows, with the sampling rate — not the error rate — written into the deal.

## Research & Models

- **[Reflection says its 501B open-weight model matches China's best at a fraction of the inference compute](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/reflection-debuts-beam-a-open-weight-ai-model-to-rival-chinese-models-at-lower-compute-cost/)** — announced Monday: Beam is a text-only mixture-of-experts model, 501 billion parameters with 23 billion active, pretrained on 23.8 trillion tokens with a 1-million-token context window. Reflection claims it scores on par with Z.ai's GLM-5.2, beats the leading Western open models, and beats Inkling on four coding tests where both report results, while needing three to four times less inference compute. Every one of those claims is the company's own and none has been independently verified — and the weights themselves are not out: Reflection says it will release them with full technical details "this month," through hyperscalers and open-source libraries. Worth holding the company's position in view while waiting: founded in 2024 by two former Google DeepMind researchers, Brooklyn-based, roughly $4.7B raised, most recently at a $25B pre-money valuation, with Nvidia, Sequoia and Lightspeed among its backers. The interesting claim is not the benchmark but the economics — a Western open-weight model whose pitch is cost per token rather than capability at the frontier.

## Product & Industry

- **[TikTok puts a shopping agent in the For You feed](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/tiktok-rolls-out-an-ai-shopping-assistant-and-one-click-checkout/)** — announced Monday: a conversational AI Shopping Assistant that handles product details, shipping, sizing and availability, remembers preferences across a conversation and completes the purchase, alongside one-click checkout directly from the feed, with Salesforce, Shopify, Shoplazza and Stripe named as partners. The scale underneath it is why this matters more than another chatbot: TikTok Shop did an estimated $15.8B in US sales in 2025, around 18% of all US social commerce, and TikTok says 54 million Americans have bought something after watching a video. TikTok has not said which model powers the assistant.
- **[HackerRank's AI now runs the interview and grades how you use AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/hackerranks-ai-interviewer-offers-a-glimpse-into-what-job-interviews-could-become/)** — Chakra became generally available on Monday after about six months in beta, during which HackerRank says it conducted more than 500,000 interviews, with Snowflake, Snorkel and Capgemini among early testers; the company has over 3,000 business customers. The design concedes something about the current state of hiring: candidates work on a real task in a canvas that includes an AI assistant, and Chakra scores their reasoning, judgement and "AI fluency" — how well they frame a problem for a model and steer it — rather than the artefact. Chief executive Vivek Ravisankar puts it plainly: "The previous modality of evaluation was evaluating the output. Now, because of AI, anybody can produce an artifact." TechCrunch notes the obvious counterweight, that automated hiring tools inherit bias from their data and criteria and are under regulatory scrutiny for it.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Google is paying to squeeze 890 more megawatts out of nuclear plants that already exist](https://blog.google/company-news/why-were-backing-americas-existing-nuclear-plants/)** — posted today by Amanda Peterson Corio, Google's global head of data centre energy: a power purchase agreement with Constellation Energy, plus financing for physical and digital upgrades, to uprate six existing reactors across Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey and add 890MW to the PJM grid, with the work to finish before the end of 2032. Google says this takes the nuclear capacity it has enabled past 1.5GW through uprates and restarts, which it puts at nearly a quarter of the Department of Energy's goal of 5GW of domestic nuclear expansion by 2030, and estimates roughly 4,400 existing jobs sustained and 7,200 construction jobs supported. The reflexive part of the deal is worth noting: Constellation will use Google Cloud's Gemini Enterprise agentic workflows to optimise plant operations and speed up uprate planning. Uprating existing reactors is the least glamorous path to new capacity and by far the fastest — no new site, no new licence fight, just more output from plants already connected.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[South Korea's financial regulator convenes over bank breaches, with AI tooling suspected](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/south-korea-probes-bank-breaches-amid-suspected-ai-powered-attacks/)** — reported Monday: the Financial Services Commission held an emergency meeting after a run of attacks on Korean financial institutions, confirming a breach at Shinhan Bank and incidents at others including Kookmin; Hana Bank was also found to have suffered a limited breach through a compromised sales-support system. Local media put the figures at 25,000 Shinhan customers and credit-card details for 119,000 Kookmin clients, and President Lee has ordered a thorough investigation into data leaks at financial and public institutions. Financial firms have been told to inspect every externally reachable system including non-customer-facing ones, cut unnecessary information exposure, check for missing authentication and access controls, and submit internal inspection results. The AI link is genuinely unconfirmed and worth stating as such: Yonhap reported that a server used in the attacks served an HTML page whose title contained a Chinese-language string associated with ARTEX AI, an open-source agentic penetration-testing system that automates reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery, attack-path planning and verification. The banks and authorities have not confirmed its use, and the string ties the attacks to no particular actor. Moon Jong-hyun of the Genian Security Center wrote on LinkedIn that several analysts believe AI attack-automation tools were involved. On the evidence published so far that is a suspicion, not a finding — but it is the first time a national financial regulator has convened over incidents where that suspicion is the live question.

## Capital & Deals

- **[A 19-year-old raised $11M to sell you a $3,499 box that runs your agents at home](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/at-19-ghost-founder-raises-11-million-to-build-a-3499-computer-for-your-personal-ai/)** — announced Monday: Ghost, founded by Zain Javaid, raised an $11M seed led by Andreessen Horowitz with Abstract, Audacious Ventures, SV Angel and Nova. The product, Core, is a screenless machine built around an Nvidia RTX Pro 4000 SFF Blackwell GPU, preloaded with Qwen-3.8-Next, Qwen-3.8-27B and Gemma-4-31B and able to run other models from Hugging Face, driven from a phone or by voice. Preorders opened Monday, shipping late October. The pitch is proactive local inference — Javaid's example is a notification that you might be getting sick because your resting heart rate is up — which puts the privacy argument for local models against the awkward fact that it needs your health data to be useful.
