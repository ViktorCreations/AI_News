## Top Stories

### [Positive sentiment about AI in employee reviews has halved since 2019](https://the-decoder.com/ai-sentiment-is-turning-sour-as-employee-reviews-reveal-growing-frustration-across-the-workforce/)
Glassdoor analysed reviews mentioning AI, LLMs, GPT or OpenAI from January 2019 through May 2026. Positive sentiment fell from 81% to 43%, with negative comments now at 53%, while mentions of AI in reviews rose 240% year over year. The breakdowns are where it gets uncomfortable. Among Gen Z, 42% of men are positive against 21% of women — the widest gender gap in the data. By role, executives and software architects stay optimistic while insurance claims adjusters and journalists run up to 98% negative, which tracks exposure rather than familiarity. The complaints sort into job-loss fear (20%), being made to use the tools (14%) and quality concerns (13%). Set beside Pew's finding on August 20 that 52% of Americans are more concerned than excited, this is the same curve measured inside workplaces, and the gap between who is enthusiastic and who is exposed is doing most of the work.

### [The labs are buying Mac minis by the tens of thousands](https://the-decoder.com/openai-and-rival-ai-labs-are-buying-tens-of-thousands-of-mac-minis-to-train-computer-use-agents/)
Per The Information: OpenAI, Anthropic and others have bought tens of thousands of Mac minis and Mac Studios to train computer-use agents, with Anthropic renting its through AWS. The appeal is the hardware profile — strong chips, unified memory rather than the GPU-centric split, and cooling that holds up under sustained load — and the constraint is the one we have been tracking all month, with the memory shortage leaving units sold out for months at a stretch. Training an agent to operate a computer apparently means buying a very large number of actual computers, which is a useful corrective to the mental model of agent training as pure GPU time.

### [OpenClaw ships 2.0, with shared sessions and cloud provisioning](https://the-decoder.com/openclaw-2-0-brings-simplified-setup-a-rebuilt-browser-app-and-multiplayer-sessions/)
The OpenClaw Foundation's largest release yet, at over 16,000 pull requests: setup now auto-detects existing ChatGPT or Claude subscriptions, API keys and local models; the browser app opens straight into a conversation with a "Session Rail" showing progress; and multiplayer sessions let several people work in one shared session with full context. Sessions run locally, on a paired device via `openclaw connect`, or on temporary cloud machines through a provisioning tool called Crabbox, with provider credentials staying on the gateway and never reaching the remote machine. Worth noting who this is: OpenClaw is the agent framework that turned up in April's gym-booking incident and again in the July intrusion into Taiwan's nuclear regulator. Easier setup and disposable cloud machines cut both ways.

## Product & Industry

- **[Debian votes to allow AI-assisted contributions](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/30/debian-votes-to-let-contributors-code-with-ai/5293421)** — results announced Sunday: from eight proposals ranging from an outright ban to rejection on environmental grounds, "Responsible Use of Generative AI" won with roughly 450 valid votes out of nearly 600 cast. Disclosure is encouraged but not required — "We encourage our contributors to disclose whether a contribution was made with AI assistance, but do not require them to do so" — and every contribution meets the same bar for quality, correctness, maintainability and legal compliance regardless of how it was made, with "blindly accepting or uploading AI-generated material without appropriate human review" ruled out. One of the oldest volunteer distributions settling this by vote is a useful reference point for every project still arguing about it.
- **[A doctoral student beat an energy company in court using GPT-5.5 and Claude](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/31/energy-biz-sse-smacked-around-in-court-by-a-guy-and-ai/5293391)** — reported Monday, though the judgment came on 17 July at the County Court at Oxford. SSE Energy Supply pursued Lyle Hopkins for £1,091.01 over an electricity meter that did not exist, for more than 20 months, having acknowledged he was not liable. Representing himself, Hopkins used the models to build arguments and check case law, regulations and court rules: "I mostly just gave it the facts and it came up with the arguments." He was awarded £1,087.88, and the judge found continued pursuit would amount to harassment, describing what SSE put him through as "a rollercoaster ride and not a good one." SSE then sent another bill contradicting the judgment.
- **[Caterpillar puts $100M into retraining as it rolls AI past mining](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/30/caterpillar-is-bringing-to-ai-deployment-what-it-learned-from-automating-mining/)** — the Cat AI Assistant gives field technicians voice access to repair procedures, with AI also running digital-twin site scanning and legacy-code modernisation across a business with 1.6 million connected assets and over 16 petabytes of structured data. The number that distinguishes it from most enterprise AI announcements is $100M over five years to train 118,000 employees. CTO Jaime Mineart's framing is the lesson from two decades of autonomous mining: "The hard part about autonomy and about physical AI is incorporating that technology into the customer jobsite and into the workflows" — the hardware was never the constraint.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[SpaceX is building its own turbine blade foundry to get gas plants up faster](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/30/musks-faster-path-to-more-gas-turbines-comes-with-pollution-problem/)** — the company bought roughly 830 acres near its Starlink factory in Bastrop, Texas between March and June, and job listings describe a blades-and-vanes foundry; making its own could pull about 18 months out of the wait for gas turbines to power AI sites. The cost sits downwind. Turbines emit smog precursors and hazardous compounds including formaldehyde; a Virginia study of eight turbines at one facility estimated effects across more than 2.5 million people and 3.4 to 6.5 additional premature deaths a year. In Memphis, where xAI has run turbines since 2024, the NAACP has repeatedly accused the company of operating without required federal permits or pollution controls, and University of Memphis researchers found air quality slightly worse. It is the fourth gas-for-AI story this month, after Amazon's Pecos County plant, Noreva's price forecast and the 9.2GW plant planned for OpenAI's Ohio campus.
