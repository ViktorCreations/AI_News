# AI News — Tuesday, November 3, 2026

> Anthropic's updated IPO filing is the day's centre of gravity: a revenue jump, a swing back to loss, and a warning that it may soon meet its own definition of AI-accelerated AI development. Beyond it, the UK's cyber agency and a Wall Street bank both pulled in the other direction on AI's reach.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic's revised IPO filing says it may "imminently" meet its own threshold for AI-accelerated AI progress](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-warns-that-its-very-close-to-rsi-capability-aka-self-building-ai/)
In its S-1/A filed Monday, per TechCrunch's reading, Anthropic added risk-factor language saying that increasing use of AI to speed up its own research "may cause the capabilities of our models to advance more rapidly than our ability to develop, validate, and implement corresponding safety, alignment, and security measures." It says it has in the past failed to notice a significant acceleration until after it happened, and that internal estimates and expectations for future models, rather than current ones, indicate it "may imminently meet" its Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI."

The filing also says no current model has been designated as exceeding that threshold, that Anthropic does not expect to safely use Claude to build successive frontier models without meaningful human supervision, and that it is considering Responsible Scaling Policy updates to keep humans in control. TechCrunch notes an S-1 is both disclosure and marketing. The filing itself was not readable from here; the quotations are TechCrunch's.

### [The UK's NCSC says AI-powered malware will "highly likely" hit national infrastructure by 2028](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/03/ai-powered-malware-artificial-intelligence-hacking-critical-uk-infrastructure)
The National Cyber Security Centre, part of GCHQ, said in its annual review that it is highly likely that between now and 2028 AI-powered malware will be used in a ransomware attack on UK critical national infrastructure such as power stations, airports and hospitals. It also warned that Russia and China will begin using AI "routinely" in cyber-attacks.

NCSC chief executive Richard Horne is due to say in a speech on Tuesday that "in the coming months, we face a real prospect of an unprecedented rise in the number of cyber incidents."

### [Goldman Sachs pulls advanced AI coding tools from everyone but its engineers](https://the-decoder.com/goldman-sachs-pulls-ai-coding-tools-from-traders-and-bankers/)
Bloomberg, as relayed by The Decoder, reports that internal October guidelines cut most employees off from tools such as Claude Code and GitHub's AI coding features, leaving them to engineers; traders and bankers had used them for months to monitor trading flows and create content. The move reportedly caused frustration.

A Goldman spokesperson cited "a variety of factors including productivity and risk management," and said the experimentation phase is over. In July the bank's CIO had praised non-technical staff "vibe coding" prototypes.
