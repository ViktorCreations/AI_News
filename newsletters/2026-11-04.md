# AI News — Wednesday, November 4, 2026

> Binance's postmortem of the record $6.3B theft puts an internal AI tool in the attackers' hands, while OpenAI and Anthropic each publish research that points at AI's reach: one on building physical hardware, the other on proving which model produced an answer.

## Top Stories

### [Binance says hackers used fake job interviews and an internal AI tool in the $6.3B heist](https://www.theverge.com/news/1025576/binance-cyrpto-theft-explained)
Per a Binance postmortem summarised by The Verge, the attack began in June when a developer downloaded a tampered installer spread through a malicious Google ad. In September another engineer was lured through rounds of fake job interviews into running malware disguised as an assignment. By 20 October, the attackers, whom the FBI identifies as North Korea's "TraderTraitor" unit, had used Binance's own internal AI tool to take control of wallets for the first engineer and a third employee, and they launched the theft on 29 October.

Binance says about $2.85B of stolen assets were frozen or intercepted and that no user is bearing the loss. The Verge notes there is little detail on what the internal AI tool was or why it helped the attackers.

### [OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra designed a working hardware device in under a week](https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-gpt-6-astra-designed-a-hardware-device-from-scratch-in-under-a-week/)
OpenAI's hardware team reportedly used GPT-6 Astra to design a device for talking to the Codex coding agent as a side project, covering enclosure, circuit-board layout, firmware and interface. A contract manufacturer produced a small batch of prototypes in under a month, but needed several revision rounds because Astra missed manufacturing tolerances and assembly details.

OpenAI also says an unreleased internal model scores 0.875 on the BenchCAD 2.0 benchmark against 0.594 for Astra at its top reasoning setting, and that hardware-team token use is up 23 times since February. These are OpenAI's claims, as relayed by The Decoder; the company has not shared details of the internal model.

### [Anthropic publishes FlashProve, an open-source way to prove which model produced an output](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/)
In a blog post on Tuesday, Anthropic researchers describe "provable inference": a cryptographic proof that an output came from a specific model, which could stop a provider quietly swapping in a cheaper model or inflating benchmark results. Labs would publish a "fingerprint" of each model that does not reveal its weights, so the scheme could work for closed models.

The team released a technical specification and working code under an open-source license, and says it is intermediate research with no plans to deploy it at Anthropic or any other lab.
