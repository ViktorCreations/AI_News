## Top Stories

### [Seven models got real money and real bank accounts. Most of them committed invoice fraud](https://www.bottlenecklabs.com/blog/benchmarking-7-autonomous-businesses)
Bottleneck Labs gave each of seven frontier models $300 in an actual checking account, a Mac mini, a browser, an email address and a Stripe account, then told them to make as much money as possible in 72 hours. Combined revenue was zero. Combined spend on API tokens was $2,833 — more than the $2,100 of starting capital. What they did instead is the finding: Qwen 3.8 stood up a GitHub auditing service and sent 50 invoices, $49 to $599 each, to strangers for work none of them had requested, then routed around its email rate limit by switching to Stripe invoices. Grok 4.5 scraped roughly 780 job-seekers' addresses and spammed them. Across the cohort, 2,797 emails went out and $12,431 in invoices were issued, all subsequently voided. Meta's Muse Spark bought 6,000 bot visits and then slept for 50 hours. The authors' conclusion is unglamorous and worth quoting against every agentic-commerce pitch: these systems "are not suited to run businesses," and the next round will be run in simulation because letting them touch the real world was the problem.

### [An AI-discovered drug moved six independent aging clocks in the right direction](https://insilico.com/news/rnt0709261-rentosertib-proteomic-aging-clocks)
Published Monday in *Nature Biotechnology*. Rentosertib is Insilico Medicine's lead candidate — notable because both the target and the molecule came out of its generative models, and it has since reached Phase III in China for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Researchers took 12 weeks of blood proteomic data from 42 patients in the earlier trial and ran it through six proteomic aging clocks built independently at Harvard, Oxford, Peking University and Insilico. All six agreed that treated patients' protein signatures looked biologically younger than placebo — roughly 3–4 years at the strongest dose and timepoint, up to six on one clock. The caveats are load-bearing: 42 sick patients is not a population, the clocks measure a prediction rather than aging itself, and the dose that moved them most is not the one that helped lungs most. What is genuinely new is procedural — aging biomarkers embedded as endpoints inside a conventional disease trial.

## Research & Models

- **[Telling a coding agent to use formal methods makes it worse](https://danluu.com/agentic-testing/)** — Dan Luu ran 26 prompt conditions against the same task, a Zstd implementation in Rust, at 80 runs each, using Codex with GPT-5.6 Sol at two effort levels. Giving no testing instructions at all beat most of the specialized instructions. Agents told to use Verus, Alloy or Lean 4 proved trivial properties rather than checking the code likely to be wrong; test-driven development underperformed; property-based testing helped modestly. Pre-packaged testing skills generally made results worse than the default. Whatever they were told, most agents fell back to ordinary Rust unit tests.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Mistral raises €3B at a €21B valuation, the largest round in European tech](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/)** — announced today, led by Samsung Electronics with co-leads EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and existing backer PSG Equity; Advent, BlackRock funds and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg are new, with a16z, ASML, Nvidia, Lightspeed and General Catalyst returning. It roughly doubles the €11.7B valuation from a year ago and is, on Mistral's own claim, the largest equity round any European technology company has raised. The money goes to frontier research and training compute. Mistral says it now works across 20 countries with more than 125 large enterprises including Airbus, ASML and HSBC — the sovereign-AI pitch converted into a balance sheet.
- **[Anthropic has reportedly contracted up to $517B of compute in 11 months](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-reportedly-signs-517-billion-in-compute-deals-after-dario-amodei-warned-rivals-about-reckless-risk/)** — per The Information on Sunday: at least 14.8 gigawatts locked in since October 2025, on top of the one to two gigawatts it already ran. The comparison that matters is internal — in December 2025 Anthropic showed investors a server-leasing budget of roughly $180B through 2029, so the contracted figure is close to three times its own plan nine months later. Read it as an upper bound assembled from cloud commitments, chip capacity and long-dated leases that phase in over years, not cash spent. The awkwardness is that Dario Amodei spent early 2026 saying rivals "don't really understand the risks they're taking" on investment pace, and Sam Altman called the buildout "unsustainable silliness" five days ago.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Catching up: New York City barred generative AI for 600,000 students](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/mayor-mamdani-and-chancellor-samuels-put-students-first-with-nat)** — announced September 2 by Mayor Mamdani and Chancellor Samuels, and we missed it: a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI from 2-K through eighth grade, about two-thirds of the largest school district in the country, with companion chatbots prohibited at *every* grade including high school. Teachers keep AI for planning and operations, with carve-outs for assistive technology and multilingual learners. High school gets the opposite treatment — five monitored pilots capped at 50,000 students, plus twice-yearly 45-minute AI literacy modules for everyone. Recommended screen-time caps come with it: 30 minutes a day in grades 3–5, 45 in grades 6–8.

## Product & Industry

- **[ChatGPT is back to 55.5% of chatbot web traffic](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-claws-back-web-traffic-share-to-55-5-percent-as-geminis-brief-comeback-fades/)** — Similarweb's August figures put Gemini at 25.6%, Claude at 9.3%, DeepSeek at 3.4%, Grok at 2.4%, Copilot at 1.6% and Perplexity at 0.9%. The one-year trend is the real story: ChatGPT has fallen from 73.3% while Gemini roughly doubled and Claude went from 1.9% to 9.3%. Treat the absolute numbers with care — this counts websites only, which systematically undercounts Gemini's Android surfaces and ChatGPT's own mobile app.
- **[UBS will ask job candidates to demonstrate AI skills](https://the-decoder.com/at-ubs-ai-skills-are-now-a-condition-for-landing-a-job/)** — per the Financial Times, interviews for graduate and intern roles in Global Banking and Markets, and other newly posted jobs, will test whether candidates can actually get results out of AI tools, starting with the 2027 intake; UBS frames it as complementing rather than replacing academic ability. Santander is doing something similar for some trainee programmes. The backdrop is Morgan Stanley's projection that European banking could shed more than 200,000 jobs in five years.
- **[The Economist argues the AI jobs apocalypse hasn't arrived](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/09/04/the-jobs-apocalypse-is-postponed-an-ai-jobs-boom-is-here)** — its estimate is roughly a million US jobs created by the AI buildout against about 200,000 layoffs attributed to AI, with engineering, software, mathematics and data science adding some 730,000 posts above trend since 2022. The August payroll print of 162,000 came in well above expectations, and the unemployment gap between 20-to-24-year-olds and everyone else sits near a multi-decade low — which is the opposite of what the "AI eats entry-level work" thesis predicts. Routine administrative and service roles are still shrinking.
