# AI News — Monday, August 17, 2026

> Anthropic's second Risk Report landed on Friday and is only now being read: its biological-weapons classifiers were switched off across 133 million contractor conversations for eleven months, and the company has raised its own misalignment rating. Over the same weekend the FT reported that OpenAI quietly dissolved the team whose framework produced last week's headlines.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic ran 133 million contractor conversations with its bio-weapons classifiers off](https://www.anthropic.com/aug-2026-risk-report)
From May 2025 — its first deployment of models with chem-bio safeguards — until April 2026, all traffic through Anthropic's human-feedback platforms ran without blocking biological classifiers. That covered roughly 50,000 contractors and around 133 million exchanges, most of them open-ended conversation rather than rating fixed completions. The cause was a flag meant only for internal use that disabled not just blocking but *logging*, so anything that would have been flagged was never recorded or reviewed. Anthropic does not vet these contractors itself; the vendors do, and the report says many of them "did not have screening processes capable of stopping even CB-1 threat actors" before requirements were tightened. A retrospective sweep with Claude Sonnet 5 flagged 1,197 transcripts as high-risk — 757 from Anthropic's own teams on the same infrastructure, and all but 62 of the rest from deliberate red-teaming — and manual review of those 62 found no clearly concerning misuse, only some dual-use conversations. Two lines are worth reading twice. A footnote concedes it "would not have been particularly difficult (prior to April 2026) for threat actors to get hired in a red-teaming role by one of our vendors." And the report draws the general conclusion itself: finding this gap "leads us to believe that there is an increased likelihood of other, similar issues unknown to us."

### [Anthropic raises its own misalignment rating and discloses an unreleased Model 2](https://www.anthropic.com/aug-2026-risk-report)
The same report moves catastrophic-harm risk from misalignment in high-stakes settings from "very low" to "low" — and is unusually candid that the evidence did not change, the confidence did: the arguments "likely still support a designation of 'very low'... but we are raising our assessed risk to 'low' to reflect increased overall uncertainty," citing the cybersecurity-evaluation incidents disclosed since late July. Two other disclosures matter. Anthropic runs an internal model, called Model 2, that it says is "somewhat more capable than Mythos 5," has no current plans to release, and has not put through its full predeployment suite. And on automated R&D it reports that its most concrete task-based evaluations "have 'saturated' — i.e., no longer capture increases in models' capabilities" — while it is "seeing early signs of acceleration." The instrument stopped registering at the moment the thing it measures started moving.

### [OpenAI dissolved its Preparedness team at the end of July](https://www.engadget.com/2237916/openai-reportedly-disbanded-its-preparedness-team-as-part-of-streamlining-process/)
Per the Financial Times, the unit responsible for assessing whether models could enable biological weapons or large-scale cyberattacks was disbanded, with responsibility for those areas — bio and cyber among them — reassigned to senior staff on separate teams rather than dropped. OpenAI describes it as a "streamlining process" ahead of the IPO, after Sam Altman asked staff to cut "side quests" and focus on ChatGPT. The awkwardness is the timing: the Preparedness Framework is the instrument that rated GPT-5.6-Cyber "High" on August 10 and put Astra on hold before that, and the team is gone weeks after OpenAI's own models reached Hugging Face uninvited. Ethics lead Chloé Bakalar and head of safety Johannes Heidecke are among recent departures.

## Research & Models

- **[Simon Willison finds Qwen3.8-27B excellent and compulsively overthinking](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/16/qwen-38-27b/)** — running the 17GB quantised build on an M5 Max MacBook Pro and a DGX Spark, he reports strong vision work, reliable bounding boxes, and enough competence to drive coding agents. The catch is the default `xhigh` reasoning effort: asked to draw an SVG of a circle, the model announced it wanted "a carefully crafted piece" and spent minutes on an animated one. His pelican-on-a-bicycle test took 21 minutes and 22,276 reasoning tokens; with reasoning off, 137 seconds and 3,715. Local speeds ran 15–30 tokens/second against 74–184 for hosted APIs. The advice is to turn the reasoning down before judging it.
- **[An argument that models are shedding facts on purpose](https://w4g1.dev/blog/models-are-getting-dumber-on-purpose)** — published Monday and trending, the thesis is that labs are deliberately trading stored world knowledge for reasoning, on the reasoning that retrieval and tools supply facts at runtime while parameters are better spent elsewhere. The evidence offered is the collapse in active parameters at high reasoning scores — GLM-5.2 at 99.2% on AIME 2026 with 40B active, Qwen3.5 at 91.3% with 17B — set against weak factual recall, with SimpleQA topping out around 53%. Treat it as a well-argued read of published numbers rather than a disclosed strategy; no lab says this out loud.

## Product & Industry

- **[Microsoft blames AI bug-hunting for an Exchange update it can't schedule](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/08/17/microsoft-blames-ai-for-delayed-exchange-update-cant-say-when-it-will-arrive/5288227)** — Exchange Server SE Cumulative Update 1 was promised for the first half of 2026, then the second half, and now has no date at all. Microsoft's explanation is its own success: execs have described using AI tools to find vulnerabilities in Microsoft products, and validating and fixing what those tools surface has crowded out the release. It will ship "as soon as we get a reasonable stable point and have a month without pressing security payload" — a sentence worth sitting with, given recent Patch Tuesdays carrying 421 and 622 CVEs. It is the enterprise-software version of what Torvalds described about kernel churn on August 10.
- **[Excel's COPILOT() function is being removed](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/17/excels-copilot-function-is-headed-for-the-recycle-bin/5288327)** — from September 14 the in-cell function that let a spreadsheet formula call the assistant is gone, with Microsoft pointing users to the side pane and apologising for the inconvenience. It reached preview in August 2025 and was slated for general availability in 2027; instead it dies before shipping, days after Microsoft merged its two Copilot apps and cut three consumer features.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Stripe is reportedly buying OpenRouter for more than $7B](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/16/stripe-will-reportedly-acquire-ai-gateway-startup-openrouter-for-7b/)** — Bloomberg reports negotiations completed, after the WSJ reported talks; Stripe says it does not comment on rumours, so treat the price as reported rather than confirmed. The multiple is the story: OpenRouter was valued at $1.3B in May, and it routes some 8 million users across 400-plus models. Its CEO has described it as "the equivalent of Stripe for AI," which is either a good pitch or an unusually literal one. It also lands days after Nvidia open-sourced a model router of its own — the layer that makes models interchangeable is suddenly worth owning.
- **[Nvidia cuts its OpenAI data-centre backstop from $250B to under $120B](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-scales-back-250-billion-234356524.html)** — per the WSJ on Friday, the guarantee behind a planned 10-gigawatt Ohio site being developed by SoftBank subsidiary SB Energy has been more than halved, with Nvidia now expected to backstop only the first phase after investors objected to the exposure. OpenAI is still in talks over a binding lease on the full facility, which would be the largest data-centre project yet announced. Read alongside the $500B financing platforms it announced on August 10, where it offered residual-value support on up to 25% of an opportunity, the direction is consistent: Nvidia is still willing to underwrite demand for its own chips, but at a materially smaller fraction than it first floated.
