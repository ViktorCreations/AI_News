# AI News — Sunday, October 25, 2026

> OpenAI is reportedly buying business from Anthropic with deep discounts, and some big customers say it is working. Block showed a voice model that learned to talk only by listening to people, and Pennsylvania became one of the first states to require labels on AI-generated ads.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI is reportedly winning Anthropic customers with discounts of up to 90%](https://the-decoder.com/openai-offers-discounts-up-to-90-percent-to-poach-anthropics-biggest-customers/)
Reported by The Information, per The Decoder on Sunday. Customers named in the report say they have moved spending to OpenAI:
- Rippling's chief executive, Parker Conrad, said OpenAI's share of its coding workload rose from 16% to 52%, while Anthropic's fell from 57% to 18%.
- Replit switched after OpenAI offered more than 30% off a contract worth over $100M a year; Replit made OpenAI its default model, as we reported on 18 October.
- DataCamp's chief executive said OpenAI's pricing cut his costs by 80–90%.

Per The Decoder, some of that is OpenAI giving up margin to gain share, and some reflects genuinely cheaper Sol and Luna models; it also cites an investor memo saying Anthropic's growth slowed from the second quarter to the third.

### [Block shows Dessa, a voice model trained only on recorded human conversation](https://block.xyz/dessa)
Announced Friday by Jack Dorsey's company. Block says it trained Dessa "from scratch on nothing but human conversation… No text, no pretrained models," making it, as far as Block knows, the first AI you can hold a realistic live conversation with that learned only from speech. It is full-duplex, listening and talking at the same time, so it can interrupt, laugh mid-sentence and make listening noises the way people do, and everything it knows came from audio. Block gives safety as one reason for building it: today's voice assistants turn speech into text and lose the emotion, so they can't hear when someone is in distress. Per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/blocks-new-voice-model-sounds-eerily-human/), it was trained on a single Nvidia GPU rack. For now there is only a waitlist.

## Research & Models

- **[AI lifted patent lawyers' work, but only senior lawyers kept the gain without it](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35720)**: an NBER working paper by David Autor and colleagues, from a study Google ran, dated September 2026 and covered Saturday by [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-makes-junior-patent-attorneys-instantly-better-but-may-quietly-stunt-their-professional-growth/). In a pre-registered three-month trial with 133 lawyers at 11 US firms, an AI drafting assistant raised the quality of their work, most of all for juniors. When everyone then reviewed a patent application without AI, treated senior lawyers beat the controls, but juniors showed no average gain: their scores split into more poor and more good results.
- **[Nvidia open-sources Flora, the data engine that labels video for its robot models](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/flora/)**: three models posted on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/flora-6ad6a8986320a55467825cf5), last updated 22 October. Flora splits robot and human videos into timestamped actions and scores how well each step and episode went, labelling 200,000 hours of video a day across 512 GB300 GPUs for Nvidia's GR00T training. Nvidia says it is competitive with GPT-6 Astra on its internal tests at roughly 1/125th of the cost.
- **[Forced to pick, Grok backed Republicans and GPT backed Democrats in nearly 500 midterm races](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/)**: a study by Convovo, a research studio led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, reported Saturday. Asked through their APIs to name one candidate, Grok 4.7 chose the Republican in 92% of answers and GPT-6.1 Sol the Democrat in 97%; the two agreed less than 10% of the time. Both consumer apps refused the question, which OpenAI says is how most people use ChatGPT.

## Product & Industry

- **[Anthropic now tells companies to fold most subagents back into one agent](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop)**: published Thursday by Anthropic's Applied AI team, with a [migration kit](https://github.com/anthropics/single-agent-loop-kit). Across several enterprise deployments, a single agent that loads skills on demand often beat both one giant prompt and a fleet of subagents on quality, usually at lower cost and latency. Too many companies, it says, build an agent architecture that copies their org chart. Subagents still make sense for parallel work, for context that has grown too long, or when one agent can't pick the right tools.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Pennsylvania now requires labels on AI-generated ads](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb806)**: Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act on Thursday, as Act 99 of 2026. Per [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018187/pennsylvania-ai-deceptive-advertising-act), ads "significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence" in a way that creates a false impression of a product must carry a label in the same format as the ad, wherever the ad was made. Parody, satire, commentary and "evidently fictional" content are exempt, and it is not yet clear how "significantly modified" will be judged.
