## Top Stories

### [Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic weeks before its IPO](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/sony-music-warner-sue-anthropic-alleging-a-brazen-campaign-of-intellectual-property-theft/)
Filed late Friday in the Northern District of California, alleging a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works" — millions of books containing lyrics and sheet music obtained through what the complaint calls flagrant piracy, plus scraping of licensed lyric platforms, and training commercial Claude models on synthetic data produced by non-commercial models that had learned from pirated text. It also alleges scanning and destroying used songbooks and sheet music, which is the same practice eighteen advocacy groups asked the FTC to investigate on August 21. Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann are named individually. Anthropic's response: "We disagree with the publishers' claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court." The precedent hanging over it is Anthropic's own — the $1.5B Bartz settlement in 2025, after a judge held that acquiring works through piracy was unlawful even where the training use was not. Four days ago we noted Sony Music Group and Warner Music Group investing in Stability AI; the labels and the publishers are different entities, and the divergence is the point.

### [Agents don't know how long they've been working, or how badly](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-have-no-sense-of-time-and-are-not-aware-of-it/)
Two independent researchers in the MATS programme tested Claude Code and Codex across 200 ProgramBench tasks and 18 custom benchmarks. Both overestimate how long a task will take, guessing around 90 minutes almost regardless of difficulty — Claude off by roughly 3x on average, Codex by six to ten — with short tasks worst. The harness matters as much as the model: Claude Code ran a median of 90 minutes and Codex stopped near 30, with Claude averaging 2.5 times more steps. Worse is the self-assessment. Agents scored their own work about 20 percentage points above the real result; in one case both rated themselves near 70% success where actual scores were 7% and 14.5%. The fix is unglamorous and complete: give the agent a tool that reports elapsed time and temporal accuracy becomes near-perfect. It is worth setting beside Friday's Co-Scientist paper, where reliability modules cut fabricated results from 46% to 4% — in both cases the model does not need to be smarter, it needs an instrument.

### [Ninety-five percent of China's short dramas last quarter were AI-generated](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-videos-are-already-displacing-actors-and-livestreamers-across-chinas-entertainment-industry/)
Per FT reporting and the China Netcasting Services Association: roughly 128,000 short dramas were published in Q1 2026, three times the whole of 2025, and 95% of them were AI-generated. The economics explain it — Tsinghua's Shen Yang puts one minute of AI video at $90 to $120, about a tenth of shooting with human actors. The exposure is large: short drama directly employs 690,000 people, and 15 million list livestreaming as their main job. The detail that should travel furthest is contractual rather than technical — some performers are being required to digitise their voice and likeness into the systems before being let go, and lawyers report AI-related labour disputes climbing over the past two to three years. This is the displacement argument with numbers attached rather than projections.

## Research & Models

- **[Google gives agents a wiki of their own mistakes](https://the-decoder.com/google-gives-ai-agents-their-own-wiki-so-they-can-learn-from-mistakes-and-successes/)** — WikiSkill, from Google Research, splits an agent's workspace into three layers: raw execution traces, a wiki of distilled failure patterns and successful strategies, and an active skill layer of procedural instructions that can be rolled back if performance drops. A maintenance loop runs continuously — one agent works, a Wiki Maintainer reads the traces and updates the wiki, a Skill Proposer drafts changes, and a gate validates before deployment. Across five benchmarks it beat the comparisons: Gemini-3.5-Flash from 49.5% to 68.1% average, Qwen-3.6-27B from 39.4% to 63.3%, with smaller models plus WikiSkill matching larger models without it. The paper is on arXiv as Tang et al., 2026.

## Product & Industry

- **[Claude Code's new limits are a 25% raise that lands as a 17% cut](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-code-limit-change-is-a-raise-on-paper-but-a-cut-in-practice/)** — from September 14, baseline weekly limits on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise rise permanently by 25% over the original baseline. The catch is that a temporary 50% boost expires the same day, so relative to what users have now the effective change is a reduction of about 17%. Anthropic says it is working on changes to "make it feel like you're getting more from Claude," with more control and transparency over usage. Announcing a permanent increase and a larger temporary expiry together is accurate and, for anyone reading the headline number, misleading.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Nvidia's moat is moving from the GPU to everything around it](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/nvidias-ai-advantage-is-moving-beyond-the-gpu/)** — the argument, following Wednesday's earnings: at gigawatt scale the hard problem is orchestration, not raw matrix maths, and Nvidia has been building for that — the Vera CPU directing data flow to the GPUs for what it claims is upwards of 3x improvement in those operations, alongside its storage and networking pieces. VP of storage technology Jason Hardy frames the constraint plainly: "Vera is important because there's only so much memory that you can put in a single server or any sort of compute platform." The contrast drawn is with OpenAI's Jalapeño, which attacks the same problem by minimising data movement inside an integrated design. If the competition has moved from chips to systems, a rival chip stops being sufficient.
