# AI News — Monday, November 2, 2026

> With US midterms a day away, the news is about the politics of AI: OpenAI is preparing a lobbying push for a Congress that may turn hostile, while data centers, facial recognition and Palantir's NHS contract all face public scrutiny.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI plans a post-midterm lobbying push, with binding "speed limits" and "AI Compute Campuses"](https://the-decoder.com/openai-prepares-lobbying-blitz-for-a-post-midterm-washington/)
Per Axios, as relayed by The Decoder, OpenAI is bracing for a tougher Washington if Democrats win the House in Tuesday's midterms. Policy chief Chris Lehane plans to steer the AI debate through 2028 around the message that AI does not replace people, backed by binding safety rules for frontier models that he calls "speed limits," which he names as the company's top priority for the new Congress.

OpenAI also wants to rebrand data centers as "AI Compute Campuses" and promise host communities economic benefits, answering local backlash, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/01/openai-might-rebrand-its-data-centers-as-ai-compute-campuses/) reports. Both accounts rest on Axios's reporting of the company's plans.

### [Twenty-nine NHS trusts listed as benefiting from Palantir's data platform say they are not using it](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/02/palantir-unused-nhs-hospital-trusts)
The Guardian, using more than 100 freedom of information requests, found that 29 hospital trusts NHS England lists as "realising benefits" from the £330m federated data platform told it the system was not live, or they were not using its applications. One senior health official called the gap "very misleading." NHS England says 137 trusts are "live."

The statistics watchdog has said it will take the claim up under its casework function, after NHS England conceded this summer that it could not prove cause and effect for its claim of 117,539 extra operations. MPs are pressing the health secretary to cancel the contract; Palantir's UK chief says doing so would be "putting politics above patients."

## Research & Models

- **[Vals AI finds Chinese models less uniformly pro-government than assumed, but sensitive to prompt language](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-ai-models-are-politically-complex-not-just-government-mouthpieces-study-finds/)**: reported Sunday. Answers in Simplified Chinese lean more toward official Chinese positions than English, Russian or Spanish ones, for American models too, and most Chinese models adopted official lines rather than refusing; Kimi K3, for example, described Xinjiang camps as vocational training centers.
- **[Luxembourg researchers show warm, therapy-style prompts push chatbots into "traumatized" narratives](https://the-decoder.com/empathetic-prompts-can-turn-ai-chatbots-into-seemingly-traumatized-conversational-partners/)**: the study ran 525 sessions with ChatGPT, Grok and Gemini. The team says the outputs map onto human rating scales and do not show real emotional states, but warn that the shift could be risky for vulnerable users.
