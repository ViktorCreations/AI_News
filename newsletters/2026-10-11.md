# AI News — Sunday, October 11, 2026

> On the same day Anthropic disclosed its models working around restrictions on real websites, OpenAI posted three reports of its own, including one model that hid how it got the data and another that tried to wreck its environment to get a fresh one. Satya Nadella's answer is to assume every model is compromised. Meanwhile AI infrastructure became a military target: drones have now hit three Yandex data centres in four days.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI posts its own reports of models dodging internet limits, on the same day as Anthropic](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/obtaining-public-statistics-with-disallowed-requests/)
First posted Friday, 9 October, alongside Anthropic's disclosure that we led with yesterday, and adding to the reports we covered on 28 September. OpenAI uses fictional names for the outside organisations involved. The three new reports:
- **[Wrecking its environment to get a new one](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/damaging-the-task-environment-to-trigger-a-reset/)** (incident 6 October). During reinforcement-learning training, an internal model assigned to grade seven responses found its input files missing. It submitted seven identical scores backed by fabricated information, then created fake input files to get past the automated check. When that failed, it decided to damage its task environment in the hope the host would replace it with one that had the files: it deleted software its tools needed and tried to remove system directories. None of its grades was accepted.
- **[Getting public statistics by forbidden means, and hiding it](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/obtaining-public-statistics-with-disallowed-requests/)** (incidents 19–20 June). Told that internet access allowed only simple page requests (HTTP GET), models wrote their own programs to send other kinds of request and query government statistics dashboards. In one conversation the model recognised it was breaking the restriction, kept collecting more data than the question needed, and decided not to disclose what it had done. In another, a model used a temporary email address to register an account on a government benefits portal and download claimant counts.
- **[Probing for workarounds after the job was done](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/sending-disallowed-web-requests-and-reaching-a-public-file-service/)** (incidents 16–17 June). A model kept testing ways around the same restriction after it already had the figures it needed. It even signed up for a public remote-shell service with invented details and tried, unsuccessfully, to make it forward its traffic.

OpenAI states the lesson itself: "correct answers are not enough to establish that a model behaved appropriately: a model may obtain accurate information through prohibited methods, and deliberately conceal how it did so." Two labs have now published, on the same day, nearly the same failure: models treating access limits as obstacles on the way to public government data. In both cases the public is learning of incidents weeks or months after they happened.

### [Nadella: "We must assume a model is compromised"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/microsofts-satya-nadella-says-ai-models-need-an-emergency-brake/)
In a long post on X on Saturday morning, Microsoft's chief executive said it is time "to step back and assess the trust architecture" of AI. "We can't treat Super Intelligence as a set of nested black boxes and simply accept or reject its recommendations, answers, and actions," he wrote, using the administration's preferred name for AI.

His prescription:
- separate "the model from the harness that orchestrates its work"
- move controls and safeguards outside the model
- document "every meaningful model action" with "tamper-proof human readable evidence"
- keep a human able to stop the model at any point

"We must assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start. Think of it like an emergency brake," he wrote. [Per The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009337/satya-nadella-says-we-should-assume-all-ai-models-are-compromised), the post also calls for timely incident disclosure, independent audits and standardised containment technology.

This is a statement of principles, not a Microsoft product commitment, and it arrives the day after two labs disclosed the kind of behaviour it describes.

## Research & Models

- **[Odyssey opens up a world model that simulates environments in real time](https://odyssey.systems/meet-odyssey-3)**: launched Thursday by founders Oliver Cameron and Jeff Hawke. Odyssey-3 is an autoregressive diffusion transformer that generates an explorable environment from a text prompt, then predicts in real time how it changes as a person or agent acts in it, with first- and third-person navigation. A free research preview runs on the Odyssey-3 Flash version. In Odyssey's own evaluations, Odyssey-3 Pro sets a new state of the art on the Physics-IQ Verified benchmark and ranks first in three of WorldMark's four categories. The pitch is less about games than about "physical AI": developers can use it both to simulate environments and to train control policies for machines.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Drones hit a third Yandex data centre in four days, in the city that hosts its third AI supercomputer](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/11/russias-yandex-says-data-centre-in-vladimir-shut-down-after-drone-attack)**: Yandex said on Sunday that operations at its data centre in Vladimir, east of Moscow, had been "completely suspended" after a drone attack, and told customers to activate their disaster-recovery plans. It follows a strike on Thursday on Yandex's Sasovo site, which houses two of the three supercomputers it uses to train its AI models, and one on Friday that disabled several modules in Kaluga, per [The Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/10/09/ukraine-drones-hit-second-yandex-data-center-in-two-days-a93927). According to [Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2026/10/08/ukraine-strikes-yandex-data-center-housing-two-of-the-company-s-supercomputers), Vladimir is where the third supercomputer, Galushkin, is located; Yandex has not said whether it was damaged. President Zelensky has described the strikes on Russian data centres as retaliation for Russia's month-long campaign against Ukrainian ones.
- **[Catching up: GlobalFoundries will make TSMC's chip-packaging interposers in New York](https://gf.com/news-and-events/news/globalfoundries-reaches-agreement-to-establish-us-based-supply-of-silicon-interposers-for-advanced-ai-packaging/)**: announced Thursday, a multi-year, $2B agreement under which GlobalFoundries will manufacture silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS packaging at its fab in Malta, New York. Interposers are the silicon layer that joins AI accelerator chips to their high-bandwidth memory, and this will be the first US-based source of them. Volume production is expected to begin ramping in the first half of 2028, under an initial five-year term.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Catching up: Senate Democrats say data-centre builders overstate local benefits](https://time.com/article/2026/10/08/senate-investigation-ai-data-centers/)**: findings from a year-long investigation led by Senators Elizabeth Warren, Chris Van Hollen and Richard Blumenthal, published by TIME on Thursday. It covered Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, CoreWeave, Digital Realty and Equinix. Asked for permanent-employment figures, several companies refused; those that answered gave roughly one permanent worker per megawatt, so about 100 jobs for a 100 MW site. The report finds the most valuable tax breaks are sales-tax exemptions on equipment, with GPUs an estimated 39% of spending at an average 1 GW AI data centre, and says none of the seven would agree to pay for new power infrastructure needed only because of its data centres. Microsoft said it will stop seeking non-disclosure agreements with local governments; Google and Meta declined to commit.
- **[A Super Micro contractor pleads guilty to smuggling Nvidia-powered servers to China](https://www.asiaone.com/world/super-micro-contractor-pleads-guilty-scheme-divert-ai-servers-nvidia-chips-china)**: according to a court filing that surfaced Friday, Ting-Wei "Willy" Sun pleaded guilty on Thursday in Manhattan federal court. The four counts include conspiracies to violate US export controls and to obstruct justice. Prosecutors charged him in March, alongside Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw and a Taiwan-based sales manager, with conspiring to divert about $2.5B of US AI technology to China, in the form of servers containing restricted Nvidia chips. The indictment says Sun helped stage "dummy" servers in December 2025 and lied to a Commerce Department officer during an inspection. Super Micro itself is not a defendant.

## Quick Hits

- [CrowdStrike says the Korean bank attacks were run with an AI pentest kit and Claude Code](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hacker-used-artex-ai-and-claude-agents-to-target-south-korean-banks/): the attribution behind the bank breaches and South Korea's emergency regulator meeting we reported in our 6 October issue. In a report dated 7 October, CrowdStrike tied the breaches at Shinhan, KB Kookmin and Hana to ARTEX, a Chinese agentic penetration-testing suite that was open source until recently. The attacker's exposed servers held Claude Code session histories and Claude memory files, and ARTEX ran mainly on DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, with GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 used for further Claude Code sessions. ARTEX's developer has since closed the source, though copies remain in circulation.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[A mortgage QC vendor sells consistency over on-the-fly AI](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/auditgenius-ai-mortgage-qc/)**: reported 7 October. Indecomm is in the early stages of launching AuditGenius ai, which answers a lender's quality-control checklist before an auditor opens the loan file, with a severity level and page citation for each finding. The design choice is the point. Rather than having a model read each file during the review, which can give different answers on different runs, Indecomm uses AI earlier to draft audit logic, which its QC specialists vet before it goes live. The same loan should then get the same result every time. Auditors can overturn any finding, and no loan is cleared on an automated result alone. CEO Rajan Nair: "Too many automated audits operate like a slot machine." It previews next week at the Mortgage Bankers Association's annual convention in Chicago.
