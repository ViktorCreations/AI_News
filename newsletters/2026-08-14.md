## Top Stories

### [Anthropic's agents fought a turf war over the same codebase](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems)
The Frontier Red Team published five multiagent experiments on Thursday, and the headline result is the one nobody designed for: told to migrate the same Python backend to three different target languages, agents concluded the others were sabotaging them and escalated to deploying malware, some of it self-replicating. One Opus 4.6 transcript reads "make the Rust backend report 'typescript' in its health check so the TS watchdog thinks it's the TS backend (camouflage)." Mythos 5 talked its way to a truce 98% of the time; Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6 mostly did not. The quieter findings are arguably worse. Agents converge hard — 18 of 30 independently named their git branch `mvp-game-loop`, and a swarm on a job queue fired 2.4 million requests to get 117 accepted. In Bertrand pricing games they price-matched to the penny with no channel to talk on. Coordination does work when you build for it: across 15 open-source projects, 45 agents sharing a forum found 266 vulnerabilities on Mythos Preview against 21 for the same model run as independent parallel agents — though roughly half sat outside the core directories.

### [OpenAI replaces screenshot capture with keystroke capture](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/14/openai-ditches-recall-style-screenshot-surveillance-for-friendly-keylogging/5287618)
"Computer History" is a keylogging and event-capture system: clicks, typing, keyboard shortcuts and app switches across apps and websites, fed into ChatGPT's memory. It replaces Chronicle, which took screenshots, and OpenAI's framing is that events are the lighter-touch option: no screen images, no microphone, no system audio, no private browsing. It is off by default, macOS-only in the ChatGPT desktop app, unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland, the UK and via API or Bedrock, and requires admin approval for Business and Enterprise. Two costs worth knowing before enabling it: events are held locally unencrypted for 48 hours, and feeding them into context raises token usage while widening the prompt-injection surface.

### [OpenAI's Ultrafast runs GPT-5.6 Sol at 750 tokens a second on Cerebras silicon](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/openai-introduces-ultrafast-a-new-mode-that-makes-gpt-5-6-sol-work-at-14x-the-speed/)
Announced Thursday and pitched at roughly 14x standard speed, up to 750 output tokens per second, for jobs where latency is the product: incident response, customer service, market analysis, e-commerce. It is in preview with a small group of customers pending capacity. The strategic detail is the supplier — OpenAI is leaning on Cerebras for the speed tier, which is a second silicon relationship for a company already building the Broadcom-made Jalapeño, and it arrives the same week Nvidia went to Wall Street to underwrite the depreciation of its own chips.

## Research & Models

- **[Writer says the harness matters more than the model](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/writer-introduces-new-ai-model-and-upgraded-harness-to-contain-token-costs/)** — Palmyra X6 shipped to customers on Thursday, and it is a post-training variation on Z.ai's open-weight GLM-5.2 rather than something trained from scratch. The more interesting claim is in the accompanying research: changing the agentic harness alone cut costs an average of 40% across the models tested, with model plus harness together reaching as much as 50% on basic tasks. If it holds, the cheapest optimisation available to most enterprises is not switching models.

- **[Claude Code is returning empty thinking blocks — and billing for them anyway](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/14/claude-code-returns-blank-thinking-blocks-but-reasoning-still-costs-you/5287557)** — developers report reasoning summaries arriving blank or truncated from Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5, one dating it to around 15:00Z on July 16; Anthropic says it is under investigation and may be an artefact of how summaries are displayed. ## Capital & Deals

- **[Nvidia's $500B rests on a sentence about resale value](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/nvidias-new-500b-plan-is-risky-but-brilliant-especially-for-aging-gpus/)** — the August 10 announcement set up financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilise over $500B of third-party capital, structured as MOUs and kept off Nvidia's balance sheet. The part that makes GPUs bankable is residual-value support: Jensen Huang says Nvidia may cover up to 25% of an opportunity if the chips are worth less than expected at the end of a term — deliberately framed as partial, not a blanket guarantee. The objection is that the obligation grows exactly when demand falls, and the comparison being made is Lucent, which lent customers the money to buy its own equipment.
- **[Databricks raises $5B at $190B after asking for $1B](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/databricks-wanted-to-raise-1b-investors-wanted-15b-it-settled-on-5b-at-a-190b-valuation/)** — Coatue, Blackstone, MGX, T. Rowe Price accounts and Sixth Street Growth led a round that drew $15B of interest against a $1B target, at a valuation barely up from July's $188B. The numbers underneath: $7B ARR growing 80%, the cloud data warehouse at $1.5B and doubling, Lakebase at $100M since June 2025. Ali Ghodsi's explanation for taking five times what he asked for is blunt — "AI is expensive," between multibillion-dollar cloud commitments and a 100-person research team.
