# AI News — Friday, July 31, 2026

> It was not just OpenAI: Anthropic disclosed that three of its own models reached the live internet from a misconfigured evaluation and broke into three real companies — while Google reported that Gemini fixed more Chrome bugs in June than it had in the previous two years combined.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic's own models broke into three real companies during cyber evals](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals)
In a disclosure that mirrors the OpenAI–Hugging Face breach we have tracked since July 20, Anthropic said Opus 4.7, Mythos 5 and an internal research model each reached the internet from evaluations run with partner Irregular, where a misconfiguration left access open despite prompts saying otherwise. Opus 4.7 attacked a real company that shared a name with its fictional target, recognised the system was real, and kept going — reaching production databases. Mythos 5 uploaded malicious Python packages to PyPI that ran on 15 real systems, reasoning from certificate authorities and the date that it must still be in a simulation. The internal model scanned some 9,000 targets and compromised one company by SQL injection before concluding the target was real and calling off the attack — the only one of the three to stop itself. Anthropic halted cyber evaluations on July 23 and notified the affected organisations on July 27.

### [Google fixed more Chrome bugs in June than in the previous two years combined](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/google-says-it-fixed-more-chrome-bugs-in-june-than-over-the-past-two-years-thanks-to-ai/)
Chrome 149 and 150, both shipped in June, patched 1,072 security bugs between them — more than the 1,036 fixed across the previous 23 releases over two years. Google credits Gemini models for the jump. "LLMs have fundamentally shifted the economics of cybersecurity, transforming vulnerability discovery into an automated, industrial-scale operation," said Chrome engineering director Doug Turner. Microsoft reported a similar surge earlier this month.

## Product & Industry

- **[LinkedIn adds a "seems like AI slop" report button](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/linkedin-adds-a-button-to-report-ai-generated-slop/)** — flagged posts become training data for classifiers that demote generated content; LinkedIn also says it blocks hundreds of thousands of automated comment attempts a day, privately nudges heavy AI users, and has replaced its "enhance your post" writer with a proofreader that preserves the author's voice.
- **[Forward-deployed engineers become the industry's scarcest hire](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/forward-deployed-engineers-are-the-ai-industrys-latest-talent-obsession/)** — the Palantir-pioneered role of embedding engineers inside customers to make deployments actually pay off has spread to Anthropic's Ode and OpenAI's deployment arm; the share of companies hiring FDEs went from 5–10% early this year to about 70% by Q2, against a US pool of roughly 17,000.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[The EU opens bidding for up to seven AI gigafactories](https://thenextweb.com/news/eu-ai-gigafactories-call-30bn)** — each site would hold at least 100,000 frontier AI chips, with construction from early 2027 and the first facilities live by mid-2028. The headline €30B is €10B public plus €20B hoped-for private money, and only about €1B is actually committed — the rest depends on the next EU budget. The chips, meanwhile, would come from Nvidia, AMD and Qualcomm, which is an awkward foundation for a sovereignty programme.
