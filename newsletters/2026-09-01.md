# AI News — Tuesday, September 1, 2026

> The Pentagon put custom builds of ChatGPT and Grok in front of three million personnel on Monday, with Anthropic conspicuously missing three days after a court ruled its blacklisting unlawful. The same day, the chair of the Financial Stability Board wrote to G20 finance ministers about AI valuations and leverage.

## Top Stories

### [The Pentagon deploys ChatGPT Mil and Grok for Government — without Anthropic](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/the-pentagon-now-has-its-own-version-of-chatgpt-and-grok/)
Both went live Monday on GenAI.mil, the secure portal launched last year with Google Gemini, which already has 1.7 million unique users out of three million eligible military and civilian personnel. The pitch is containment of a different kind: keep sensitive government work off consumer channels and out of the data collection that comes with them. The absence is the story. Anthropic is not on the portal, having refused unrestricted access while insisting on guardrails against autonomous weapons and mass surveillance of Americans — for which the Trump administration designated it a supply-chain risk, and for which a federal judge ruled last week that the designation was unlawful retaliation. Anthropic won in court and remains off the platform; its competitors accepted the terms and are on it. That is the actual price of a use-case restriction, in a form no policy document captures.

### [The Financial Stability Board chair warns the G20 about AI valuations and leverage](https://the-decoder.com/bank-of-england-chief-warns-that-inflated-ai-valuations-and-rising-leverage-could-trigger-the-next-financial-crisis/)
Andrew Bailey, Bank of England governor and FSB chair, wrote to G20 finance ministers on Monday setting out the transmission mechanism rather than just the worry: inflated AI valuations sitting on rising leverage, leveraged ETFs and trend-following strategies pulling retail money in, and cross-investment between AI companies and their hyperscaler customers concentrating the exposure. "Rising leverage is a feature of a maturing financial cycle," he writes. "While it can reinforce rising markets, it can also intensify declines when sentiment turns." He separately flags that frontier AI could "materially alter the speed, scale and economics of cyber risk." The structural difference from the dot-com era, which NYU's Aswath Damodaran has been making loudly, is that this buildout is debt-financed — we noted on Saturday that more than $400B of AI-related debt has been raised globally this year — so a downturn reaches past shareholders.

### [Instagram will throttle AI profiles that don't say they're AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/)
Announced Monday: accounts built around an AI-generated person will have their reach reduced unless they carry a new "AI-generated profile" label, which carries no penalty. The label covers profiles whose person is substantially AI-generated, not ordinary AI-assisted editing of photos, captions or graphics. Meta's reasoning is unusually candid about the failure mode — "people don't like seeing a profile that seems human, only to find out later that the person was completely AI-generated" — which is an admission that the disclosure is needed because the output is no longer distinguishable. Enforcement mechanics are unstated, including how undisclosed profiles get detected, which is where this will actually be decided. It lands three weeks after Spotify started badging AI persona artists and excluding them from recommendations.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[ChatGPT Search is now regulated as a very large online search engine in the EU](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-faces-stricter-eu-oversight-as-a-very-large-search-engine/)** — the Commission designated it under the Digital Services Act on Monday, having passed the 45-million-monthly-user threshold; the designation covers ChatGPT's search function rather than the assistant as a whole. OpenAI has four months, to the end of December, to comply: systemic risk assessments, transparency reporting, independent audits, and data access for vetted researchers, with the Commission itself as enforcer and a supervisory fee of up to 0.05% of worldwide annual profit. The-decoder describes it as the first standalone AI service brought under the regime. Two of the four obligations — audits and researcher access — are the ones labs have resisted hardest elsewhere.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Nvidia puts $3.5B into MediaTek to keep custom chips inside its rack](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/nvidias-3-5b-mediatek-bet-reveals-its-plan-for-tackling-big-techs-ai-chip-buildout/)** — announced Monday: MediaTek gets access to NVLink Fusion and will design custom silicon for AI companies and hyperscalers that drops into Nvidia-based data centres. The threat it answers is the one running through this whole month — Amazon, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic all building their own accelerators. Rather than fight that, Nvidia is making the custom chip a component of its rack architecture, so customers "standardize on the rack-scale infrastructure across their AI factories" and deploy their own parts alongside its GPUs. It is the same play as the argument we covered on Sunday, that the moat has moved from the GPU to the system around it.
- **[China's CXMT produces its first HBM3E, in small quantities and a generation behind](https://the-decoder.com/chinas-cxmt-makes-its-first-hbm3e-chips-closing-the-ai-memory-gap/)** — a genuine milestone for domestic AI memory, and worth stating with the caveats the report carries: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron are already mass-producing HBM4, yields were estimated around 25% as of June, output is small, and none of the $8.6B CXMT raised in its Shanghai IPO is earmarked for HBM per the prospectus. The assessment is three to five years behind technically. Set against a global memory shortage now raising server prices and squeezing Android phones, a second-tier domestic supplier still matters — but "closing the gap" is doing a lot of work.

## Product & Industry

- **[OpenAI has been charging some large customers only when the model finishes the job](https://the-decoder.com/openai-starts-charging-some-customers-only-when-its-ai-actually-works/)** — per The Information, outcome-based arrangements have been offered to selected large customers over the past few months, billing on completed work such as a handled support request rather than on tokens. OpenAI declined to comment. The hard part is the one nobody has solved: attributing the outcome, since a resolved ticket or a closed sale may owe as much to a marketing push or the season as to the model. Salesforce and Adobe are moving the same way under the same pressure — high operating costs against slower revenue growth. It is also the commercial answer to the reliability problem the research keeps documenting, moving that risk from buyer to vendor.
