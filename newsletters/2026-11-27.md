# AI News — Friday, November 27, 2026

> A quiet holiday-week news day with export controls still rippling: Nvidia reportedly pauses server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, while Microsoft details how state-backed hackers automate attacks with the ARTEX framework and a UK investigation finds undisclosed AI use in more than two dozen court cases.

## Top Stories

### [Nvidia reportedly pauses server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/)
TechCrunch, citing the Wall Street Journal, reports Nvidia told manufacturing partners including Dell and Super Micro to halt server shipments to the four countries for an unspecified period while it assesses whether regional partners meet US export requirements. Chinese companies had been tapping Nvidia chips remotely from data centers there, a loophole Commerce closed earlier this month; the Journal notes the Institute for Progress estimates such remote data centers supply about 24% of China's AI computing power. This is the WSJ's reporting, relayed second-hand, and Nvidia's own statement was not available in the source read. It follows [Monday's Megaspeed blacklist](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cv98xpg5dg5zo) and the new licence rules covering the same region.

### [Microsoft details how state-backed hackers use the ARTEX AI framework to automate attacks](https://the-decoder.com/microsoft-details-how-state-backed-hackers-use-the-artex-ai-framework-to-automate-cyberattacks/)
Per The Decoder's summary of a Microsoft analysis, state-backed and criminal groups are increasingly running fully automated attacks with ARTEX, which distributes attack tasks itself, switches between language models when something fails and operates at machine speed, often leaving defenders minutes to respond. Microsoft recommends deception such as decoy systems and "fake wins" that trick the AI agent into calling off an attack on its own. The FBI [warned about the same tool](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/fbi-says-artex-ai-open-source-hacking-tool-used-to-breach-networks-in-under-three-hours/) earlier this month.

### [Investigation: undisclosed AI use has affected more than two dozen court cases in England and Wales this year](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/26/dozens-of-court-cases-in-england-and-wales-affected-by-undisclosed-ai-use-in-2026)
The Guardian reports judges have complained of AI-generated witness statements, fictitious case law and nonexistent statutes from lawyers, police officers and claimants. Examples include a High Court tax dispute marred by "generative AI hallucinations," a police officer who used AI to write a witness statement meant to maximize an attacker's sentence, and a barrister in a $21 million dispute who relied on an invented ruling, prompting a warning about "the dangers of relying on the product of AI without verification."
