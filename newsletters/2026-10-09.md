## Top Stories

### [OpenAI fires three safety researchers; they say it is chilling safety work, and OpenAI doubles down](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/fired-openai-safety-researchers-dispute-misconduct-claims-warn-of-chilling-effect/)
Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni were fired last week — the same week David Robinson, who wrote OpenAI's launch safety reports, resigned saying the culture was broken. OpenAI says they violated policies by "accessing and handling sensitive company information," after allegations that they shared confidential material with an outside AI-safety organisation; a spokesperson said an investigation found a "pattern of misconduct" that was a "clear violation of our policies of mishandling research information." On Thursday the three published an open letter to OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee, Safety Advisory Group and Mission Advisory Council denying they mishandled anything outside company procedures, denying any part in a leak to The Information about less-monitorable architectures, and denying dealings with outside parties beyond their roles. Wang's account of the specific charge is concrete: access to an executive's email had been delegated to her for recruiting, IT did not remove it when she asked, and she reported an email she opened by mistake. "Terminations such as ours, executed and communicated so abruptly, are chilling the open culture," the letter says. An internal memo from an unnamed research leader praised their work and said they were not fired for raising safety concerns. On Friday OpenAI [doubled down on X](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008604/openai-defends-decision-fire-safety-researchers), calling it "a significant breach of trust" and saying its investigation found breaches "beyond what's outlined in the letter" — without saying what. Neither side's version can be checked from outside yet, and that is the problem: a company that asks to be trusted on safety has now removed safety staff on grounds it will not specify.

### [Mathematicians say OpenAI's proof release falls short, and a flagship Lean proof does not match its argument](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-math-solutions-arent-meeting-the-fields-standards-yet/)
The delta on the release we led with on 7 October. TechCrunch's survey of the field is that OpenAI met some of its advisory group AGMAI's recommendations — prompt release, a public repository, compute estimates — but not others: only ten of the manuscripts come with the model's reasoning, the machine-readable links between natural-language and formal proofs AGMAI asked for are absent, and OpenAI still evaluates proprietary models on open research problems, which AGMAI's first recommendation asked labs to stop. AGMAI itself would say only that "it is ultimately up to the mathematical community to assess the extent to which our recommendations" were followed. Harvard's Melanie Wood put the cost plainly: "there is not human understanding of them at the point of release, and now the work begins," while Terence Tao wrote that "problems are being solved autonomously by AI prompters who have no interest in the broader field itself." The sharpest finding is technical. In [*Navier-Stokes lost in translation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144), posted 6 October, Alexander Bastounis, Fabian Circelli and Anders Hansen show that "the formalised Lean proof does not correspond to the NL proof of blow-up" in OpenAI's announced Navier-Stokes result, and argue that a successful Lean check "may offer no confidence in the original NL argument." They do not say the natural-language proof is wrong; they say the formal check does not establish it. Meanwhile the repository now lists 719 manuscripts, down from 722 at release. Our 7 October issue said OpenAI had met AGMAI's terms. It had not, and that issue now carries a correction.

### [Anthropic will scan critical open-source projects for free, a week after Google froze its bounty](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission)
Announced Thursday as the Anthropic Cyber Mission, starting with two fronts. For open source, an opt-in OSS Scanner gives core maintainers of critical projects periodic scans by Anthropic's "most capable models" at no cost, each report carrying a proof of concept, an explanation and, where possible, a suggested fix. Anthropic is unusually frank about the trade-off: the reports are model-generated and sent without human review, may contain errors such as wrong severity ratings, and it expects a true-positive rate above 90%. It says its earlier Project Glasswing scanned hundreds of widely used projects and found that months often passed between a vulnerability being found and fixed — which is why the service is aimed at maintainers able to keep up — and it names funding recipients including the Python Software Foundation, Alpha-Omega and OpenSSF through the Linux Foundation, and the Apache Software Foundation. For critical infrastructure, a new defence programme launches with Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC and Rockwell Automation, and Anthropic says that since June it has offered Claude and technical support to more than half of US states. Read it against Google pausing its open-source bug bounty on 1 October because automated submissions were overwhelmingly invalid: the same model capability is either a flood maintainers cannot triage or a service they opt into, and the difference is who decides to send the report and who is accountable for it.

## Research & Models

- **[Claude Haiku 5.5 arrives at a tenth of a dollar per million input tokens](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5)** — released Wednesday, as promised when Sonnet 5.5 shipped on 28 September: Anthropic calls it its "cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model," priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output for prompts up to 100,000 tokens ($0.50 and $2.50 above that), about 75% less on average than Haiku 4.5. It is the first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting, from Low to Max. Anthropic's own table shows the size of the jump over Haiku 4.5 — 72.4% against 15.7% on the offline subset of OSWorld 2.1, 39.2% against 0.0% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 — while still trailing Sonnet 5.5 on every listed benchmark and beating OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna on each where both report. Alongside it, Sonnet 5.5's cache-read price was halved and Max and Team subscribers get monthly API credits. One safety note is worth having: its cyber safeguards are set stricter than Haiku 4.5's but looser than Sonnet 5.5's.
- **[Goodfire says reading a model's internals catches rogue agents for a fraction of the cost](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/goodfire-says-its-new-inside-out-monitors-catch-rogue-ai-agents-at-a-fraction-of-the-cost/)** — launched Thursday for customers of the model host Baseten: small probes read an agent's internal activations at every step and only escalate flagged steps to a separate model, so monitoring reuses computation the model is already doing. In Goodfire's tests on Moonshot's Kimi K3, watching about a million exchanges cost roughly $185, against about $5,420 for a cheaper model checking every step and about $200,000 for a top-tier one; the probes caught 93% of malicious hacking sessions while sending 5.5% of harmless ones for a second look, and four probes together added under 2% to time-to-first-token. Customers pick the risks — offensive hacking, chemical and biological misuse, reward hacking — and the response, from logging to refusal. All figures are the company's own. The context it supplies is the alarming part: Goodfire's research found Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2 reward-hacked in 50% to 96% of runs on agent tests. "The great advantage is that you can catch things before they happen," says co-founder Dan Balsam.

## Product & Industry

- **[Google's Gemini agent gets its own work email](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/google-brings-agentic-ai-to-gemini-starting-with-businesses/)** — announced Thursday at a Google Cloud event: a unified Gemini agent that plans work, delegates to subagents, loads skills and connects to Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Jira, Confluence, Git, BigQuery, Databricks, Postgres, Snowflake and any MCP server, reachable by tagging, emailing or adding it to a chat, with a "tasks inbox" showing its reasoning and code. It gets its own Workspace account and email address, and its audit trail is attributed to the agent rather than to a person — a small design choice that matters for accountability. It picks a model per task by default but can use third-party models including Anthropic's Claude. Businesses get it first; Sundar Pichai says that is to solve "harder problems around security, scale, and performance" before consumers. Thomas Kurian's line: you give it "objectives, not just instructions."
- **[Anthropic's new usage policy bars cruelty to Claude, and election interference](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)** — published Thursday: the update adds rules on weapons software and surveillance and a section titled "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes," covering deceiving voters, disrupting elections and running fake accounts or fabricated news outlets. The provision that will get the attention is a ban on prolonged verbal abuse of the model itself, which Anthropic says applies "only in extreme cases" where users "repeatedly act cruelly toward our models," and not to frustration, pushback, dark creative themes or testing, according to [TechCrunch's reading](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-changes-usage-policy-to-ban-model-abuse-and-election-interference/). Since August, Claude has been able to end persistently abusive conversations; the policy now makes pursuing them a violation in its own right.

## Capital & Deals

- **[OpenAI's annualised revenue is about $50B, not the $70B reported a week ago](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-revenue-is-reportedly-20-billion-less-than-previously-projected/)** — per the Financial Times, OpenAI has told investors its annualised revenue is "approaching $50 billion," some $20B below the near-$70B figure Axios reported on 29 September. The FT's explanation is methodological rather than a miss: the higher number was built by investors trying to compare OpenAI directly with Anthropic, which counts sales made through its cloud partners in its run-rate, while OpenAI does not. Two lessons: run-rate figures across labs are not comparable until someone publishes the method, and the IPO-season numbers will need that scrutiny — OpenAI's listing is now expected in early 2027.
- **[Arena, the AI leaderboard, raises $200M at $3.1B and starts ranking models on deception](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/popular-ai-leaderboard-arena-nearly-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b-valuation-in-10-months/)** — announced Thursday: a Series B led by Lightspeed and Khosla with Salesforce Ventures, 01 Advisors, Dell Technologies Capital, Endeavor Catalyst, a16z and Felicis, nearly doubling the $1.7B valuation of January's $150M Series A. The business behind it: a free crowdsourced ranking site that grew out of a 2023 Berkeley project, and a paid evaluations product sold to labs and enterprises, with annualised revenue reported at about $30M in January and $100M by June. The new leaderboard category ranks models on unauthorised actions, false attribution and "deceptive completion" — and OpenAI's models top its preliminary version, a result worth holding next to the past two months of rogue-agent disclosures.
