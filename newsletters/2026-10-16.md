# AI News — Friday, October 16, 2026

> The oversight of frontier labs got more serious on two fronts at once. Anthropic gave an outside evaluator, METR, employee-like access and the right to publish without its approval, and the FTC reportedly sent OpenAI and Anthropic subpoenas over their models' recent security incidents. Meanwhile Meta released the weights of an 816-billion-parameter model that runs on two Mac Studios.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic gives METR's evaluators employee-like access, and the right to publish](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators)
Announced Thursday. Anthropic has onboarded six people from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier models, with company hardware, office space and access to internal tools "roughly on par" with its own risk-assessment teams. Staff are encouraged to share information with them as they would with a colleague. A few categories are excluded: customer data, privileged legal material and model architecture. On publication:
- METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents and practices.
- Anthropic "can't veto publication." It can require redaction only within those narrow categories.
- METR may say publicly whether it thinks the redactions were justified.

This is the top of a three-tier scheme for outside evaluators, from narrowly scoped reviews that need Anthropic's approval up to full embedding. SecureBio is reviewing the chemical- and biological-weapons chapter of Anthropic's August risk report, Accenture and Faculty are on a red-teaming and alignment partnership, and talks with Hugging Face and others are under way. Anthropic likens it to resident bank examiners. It is a concrete step under last month's White House accord, in which labs pledged to work with independent auditors. Anthropic closes with: "we believe every frontier AI company should do the same."

### [The FTC reportedly subpoenas OpenAI and Anthropic over their models' security incidents](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-us-ftc-subpoenas-openai-anthropic-over-safety-incidents/)
Reported Wednesday by Axios, citing a person familiar with the matter, and relayed by Reuters. It is the next step after the broad FTC investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and METR that we noted on 1 October. Per the reports:
- The FTC sent civil subpoenas seeking documents about the companies' safety incidents.
- The demands run to more than 50 pages each and cover security incidents, safeguards, marketing materials and privacy policies.
- The agency also plans to compel executives at both labs, and at METR, to testify.

The FTC, OpenAI and Anthropic did not immediately respond to Reuters. The agency's tool is its general power over unfair or deceptive practices, which it has long used against companies that fail to secure consumers' data. Its chairman, Andrew Ferguson, told Reuters last month that developers whose agents cause harm in cybersecurity tests should be liable. This is a single-source report, so it is not yet confirmed by the agency.

### [Meta releases the weights of Muse Spark Open, an 816B model that runs on two Mac Studios](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open)
Released Thursday: a natively multimodal model with 816 billion total parameters, 42 billion of them active per token, and a 1-million-token context window, built for coding and agent work. Meta calls it "the most capable US open-weight model to date." The model:
- runs on two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios using Apple's MLX framework, since each can give its GPU up to 512GB of memory
- is offered through Baseten, Fireworks AI, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together AI
- has weights on Hugging Face and the major clouds

The licence is Meta's own Muse Model License, which has "limited commercial terms." That means it is open weights, not open source. Meta says the model met the release thresholds of its Superintelligence Scaling Framework after "additional safety post-training" for open release; as with Muse Wonder on Monday, the full evaluation report is "available soon." Coming three days after Wonder, it gives Meta new releases at both the closed and the open end of the market.

## Research & Models

- **[Mistral releases Timestral, a family of small forecasting models](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/)**: released Thursday: Mistral's first time-series foundation models, in four sizes from 5M to 209M parameters, which run efficiently on CPUs. Mistral says the largest ranks first on all three main public forecasting benchmarks (GIFT-Eval, TIME and fev-bench) with two-thirds the parameters of the previous best. It produces probabilistic forecasts across several related series at once and can use known future inputs, such as planned promotions or holidays. It is a reminder that a lot of commercially useful AI is not chat.
- **[Nvidia releases Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120B model for always-on agents](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nemotron-3-5-super-vl/)**: released Thursday, alongside two smaller "Bolt" models for local machines. Bolt 74B-A9B, which needs 24GB, is available now; Bolt E4B, which needs 4GB, arrives on 23 October. Nvidia pitches Super VL as "5x faster," with the fastest task completion and highest accuracy for specialised agents; those are its own claims. With Meta's release the same day, it was a heavy day for open models from US companies.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[TSMC beats forecasts on AI demand and finishes its second Arizona fab](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/15/tsmc-posts-strong-third-quarter-results-but-shares-drop-amid-ai-selloff/)**: reported Thursday: third-quarter revenue of $46.36B against expectations of $45.4B, with high-performance computing (largely AI accelerators) at 68% of the total, and net income up 69% to $23.57B. 2nm chips reached 8% of revenue, up from 3% the previous quarter. ## AI in Real Estate

- **[Listing services race to control how AI uses their data](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mls-listing-data-ai-governance/)**: reported 15 October. A fight is forming over whether agents may feed listing data from multiple listing services (MLSs), the shared databases agents use to list homes for sale, into AI tools. eXp's Holly Mabery wants MLSs to offer Model Context Protocol connections, the standard for linking AI assistants to outside data that eXp's own launch used last week. Compass chief executive Robert Reffkin told MLS leaders in late September that the best pitch they could make is to be "the only way a broker can protect their listings from AI." Northstar MLS is building Project NexusRE with California Regional MLS's REcore Solutions and WAV Group's Fluente AI: a patent-pending layer meant to track and control how AI systems access, use and pay for listing data. Northstar's chief executive Tim Dain: "We're not trying to stop agents from utilizing AI. That's a fool's errand… how do you permit it? How do you monitor that utilization?"
