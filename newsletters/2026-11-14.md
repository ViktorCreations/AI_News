# AI News — Saturday, November 14, 2026

> The Nvidia scandal moves from the boardroom to Washington, where Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is under fire over chip export controls, while Anthropic's IPO is reported six times oversubscribed days before pricing.

## Top Stories

### [Lutnick under pressure over the Nvidia scandal, as Treasury's Bessent says Commerce "has the pen" on blacklisting Megaspeed](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/13/nvidia-scandal-sparks-us-national-security-concerns-china)
The Guardian reports that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC he has "for months" favored putting Megaspeed, the Singapore firm tied to Alice Huang, on the US blacklist, but that Lutnick's Commerce Department "has the pen." The Associated Press reports White House officials are discussing whether to fire Lutnick, and Republican senators including John Cornyn and John Curtis are calling for accountability. Lutnick is due to testify to Congress next month, days after Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia CEO on Thursday and has agreed to appear.

This is the new development since yesterday's issue: the inquiry has widened from Huang to the officials who handled the intelligence about him. Commerce has not been quoted in the sources read here.

### [OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, letting its agents check out on a user's behalf](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)
OpenAI [announced](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet) the feature on Friday. Subscribers add eligible cards, then ask their agent, which OpenAI calls a "dot," to buy something; the user reviews the items, total and card and approves with Face ID or a fingerprint, and a payment token stands in for the real card number. Availability is limited at launch, spending limits and pre-approved purchases are planned, and OpenAI published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers. TechCrunch notes some retailers block agents, Amazon being the largest.

### [A former OpenAI researcher says Chinese models carry the fingerprint of Nvidia Blackwell chips](https://the-decoder.com/former-openai-researcher-says-he-found-nvidia-blackwell-fingerprints-hidden-inside-six-chinese-ai-models/)
Will DePue, who now runs the nonprofit Unemployment Labs, says leftover patterns from the random initialization of model weights show that six Chinese models released since April, from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group, were trained on export-controlled Blackwell GPUs. He says he reproduced the pattern exactly for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3 and published the code. According to The Decoder, DePue says senior researchers at OpenAI and Anthropic confirmed his method; that is his account, and it has not been confirmed by those labs here.

## Research & Models

- **[Runway releases Praxis-1, an open-weights robot-control model](https://the-decoder.com/runways-open-source-praxis-1-turns-video-ai-into-a-robot-controller/)**: weights and code are under Apache 2.0, first shown in late September to select partners. It turns camera images, text instructions and position data into robot movements, adapts to new robots with extra training, and in Runway's own tests still confuses similar-looking objects and cannot reliably plan complex sequences.

## Product & Industry

- **[OpenAI launches two Daybreak security products](https://the-decoder.com/openai-enters-the-cybersecurity-market-with-two-daybreak-products/)**: Daybreak Red Team, in early access, probes web apps for vulnerabilities and tests patches; Daybreak Defense Factory scans code and drafts fixes as pull requests for developer approval. Both run on GPT-6 Cyber, per The Decoder.
- **[Anthropic releases Claude for Manufacturing](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-for-manufacturing-plugs-ai-directly-into-factory-software-to-automate-industrial-workflows/)**: a plugin for Team and Enterprise customers that connects Claude to Autodesk Fusion, Oracle NetSuite, Microsoft 365 and Infor, with 17 skills such as quoting from inventory and comparing suppliers. Reordering from backup suppliers requires human approval, and it does not control physical machines.
