## Top Stories

### [A Yale professor puts Jensen Huang's odds of keeping his job at "about a 50-50" as Nvidia's inquiry continues](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/is-jensen-huangs-job-on-the-line-yales-jeffrey-sonnenfeld-estimates-its-about-a-50-50/)
Following Sunday's announcement that Nvidia's independent directors will investigate Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy, TechCrunch spoke with Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, who called Huang's career salvageable but put the odds at "about a 50-50," described the episode as "remarkably poor judgment," and said he could rebuild trust if he can show he was duped. That is one commentator's view, not a board position: the board has said it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised and that it has confidence in Huang. TechCrunch adds that no one has been charged and that the Chinese Embassy called the allegations "groundless smears and slanders."

The Guardian's [account](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation) and The Verge's [full text of the memo](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029595/full-text-jensen-huang-affair-memo-nvidia) cover the same facts as yesterday's issue; nothing new on the investigation itself has been reported since.

### [UK public bodies say AI-written complaints are now "an onslaught"](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence)
The Guardian reports that offices dealing with constituents' complaints and requests are being swamped by chatbot-generated correspondence. A senior caseworker in a Labour MP's office says it "started as a trickle in 2024 and now it's an onslaught," and that "half the emails we receive are some form of AI," which makes each request harder and slower to assess.

The piece describes how tasks that once required effort and specialist knowledge, such as objecting to a planning application or writing a formal complaint, now take minutes, which raises volumes for the people who have to read them.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[NAR's data scientist tells Realtors: on free AI tools, "you're the bacon"](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-warns-ai-risks/)**: at NAR's NXT conference on Sunday 8 November, Mike Woodward cited NAR's September technology report, which found 23% of agents use AI daily and 25% weekly, with only 12% planning not to use it. He advised paying for services and enabling data-retention opt-outs for anything confidential, and warned that buyers now arrive with AI-generated impressions of a town or property.
