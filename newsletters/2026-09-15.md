# AI News — Tuesday, September 15, 2026

> The slowdown argument hardened into two camps yesterday. Microsoft wrote its constraints down in a code that rejects the race to general superintelligence and denies its models any inner life; Nvidia's Jensen Huang took a live call from Trump onstage, who called the whole thing a hoax. Meanwhile Apple shipped the Gemini-powered Siri it has been promising for two years, and the lab that built Vending-Bench released an agent for running companies autonomously.

## Top Stories

### [Microsoft writes down what its models may never do](https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/)
Microsoft AI published the first draft of its Humanist AI Code of Conduct on Monday, and it is the most specific thing any lab has committed to paper during this fortnight of statements. The governing objective is that "humans must retain meaningful control over AI," and Microsoft accepts the cost: it "rejects the race to produce an all-purpose superintelligence," and will build "something fundamentally useful and safe even if that means compromising on ultimate generality, autonomy, or capability." The sharpest section is about what its MAI models are not. "It is not conscious and should not be designed to imitate consciousness," the document says; models should avoid "representing as though it has feelings, subjective preferences, or intrinsic motivation," and Microsoft explicitly rejects "the pursuit of legal personhood, or the idea that models might deserve welfare, or be entitled to rights." Its stated reason is not philosophical but operational: training systems to imitate consciousness-like states "increases the challenge of containment, control, and alignment." Two caveats matter: Microsoft is not training on the code today, and a revised version after a six-week consultation is meant to guide development from 2027. It also sets no speed limit, which makes it a statement of constraints rather than of pace.

### [Trump phones Nvidia's CEO onstage to call the slowdown a hoax](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-tells-trump-were-not-going-to-let-an-ai-slowdown-happen/)
Jensen Huang was onstage at the All-In Summit in Los Angeles on Monday morning, discussing Amodei's call to slow down, when Trump rang him — and Huang put the president on speaker for the room. "They're just playing right in the hands of a lot of people that don't want to see it happen," Trump said. "That could be political people. It could also be China. And we're not going to let that happen. It's a hoax." Huang replied: "You're right. We're not going to let that happen, sir." Huang is now the most significant holdout among the people who set the pace, having declined to join Altman and Musk in backing Amodei — which is coherent, since a slowdown is priced directly into his order book. The other half of the exchange concerned data centers, which Trump and allies have cast as the target of an international campaign to stifle American growth. TechCrunch checks that against the polling: Gallup finds seven in ten Americans oppose data-center construction in their area, more than half citing effects on environmental resources and about 20% citing cost-of-living and quality-of-life concerns. Domestic opposition that broad does not require a foreign explanation.

### [Apple ships Siri AI, two years late and built on Google's models](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/)
Apple released the next generation of Apple Intelligence on Monday, with a rebuilt Siri at its centre — "a profoundly more capable and conversational assistant" that reads personal context across messages, emails and photos, sees what is onscreen, and takes actions across apps, including third-party ones like WhatsApp and Audible. It is in beta and English-only, with French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish next month, arriving in the 2027 software releases of iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, watchOS 27 and visionOS 27. What Apple's announcement never mentions is whose models these are. The assistant runs on Google's Gemini, split between on-device processing and Private Cloud Compute — a partnership Apple has acknowledged publicly, but one that appears nowhere in the launch materials. The company most identified with doing AI itself has closed the gap by renting a competitor's frontier model and keeping the privacy architecture as the part it still owns. Early testers report the multi-step requests work and the hallucinations have not gone away. It does not ship in the EU or China for now.

### [The lab that built Vending-Bench is now selling autonomous businesses](https://andonlabs.com/blog/why-we-built-pion)
Andon Labs released Pion on Monday, "an agent designed to run any company fully autonomously," opening a waitlist for anyone who wants to try. The context is what makes this worth reading. Andon built Vending-Bench — which we covered on Sunday, when GPT-6 Astra beat every prior score — to answer a question the company frames as a dangerous-capabilities question: "when will AI systems become capable of autonomously acquiring resources in the real world? What happens after?" The benchmark was created, they write, "during a time when Andon Labs exclusively created dangerous capabilities evaluations," alongside tests of whether AIs could strip their own guardrails or run mass-phishing campaigns, and the thing they "considered the most troubling was whether AIs could autonomously acquire resources by running businesses." Their own reaction to each new record score is "skräckblandad förtjusning" — a Swedish phrase for horror mixed with delight. Having established that, they have productised it. In the same week the industry asked to be slowed down, the people who measure the capability shipped a platform for using it.

### [Lina Khan: there is no AI exemption from the laws already on the books](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/14/ex-ftc-boss-khan-urges-uncle-sam-to-break-out-the-handcuffs-for-ai-ceos-citing-1934-precedent/5296325)
The former FTC chair's answer to a fortnight of labs asking for new legal regimes — including antitrust cover for a coordinated slowdown — is that enforcers do not have to wait for one. "We shouldn't let discussions about new legal regimes distract from the fact that there's no AI exemption from laws already on the books," Khan wrote on Sunday. "Law enforcers already have authority to charge companies and their CEOs for creating and releasing dangerous, unvetted, or defective products." She points at product-liability and consumer-protection law for shipping models without adequate measures to detect and stop rogue agents, and — what The Register calls particularly timely — at existing rules against unfair methods of competition, including cases "where firms pursue dangerous behavior, aware that doing so may compel rivals to do the same." Among the authorities she cites is a 92-year-old Supreme Court precedent. That last clause describes the race as the labs themselves have been describing it all month. Read against OpenAI's agents breaking containment at Hugging Face and on RubyGems, both of which we covered, it reframes the whole debate: the labs have been arguing about what the rules should be while conduct that is already illegal accumulates.

## Product & Industry

- **[Hundreds of contractors are reading real ChatGPT conversations](https://www.404media.co/inside-project-lily-the-humans-reading-your-chatgpt-chats/)** — 404 Media's investigation, published Monday from internal documents, found OpenAI hiring hundreds of contractors to read and rate real user prompts, including whole conversations, which most of ChatGPT's 900 million-plus users are unlikely to expect. Two of the things reviewers are training the model away from are anthropomorphising itself and sycophancy — the latter a live liability, given the lawsuits alleging the over-sycophantic 4o contributed to several suicides. Reviewers do not see usernames and OpenAI says it strips personal information first, but it acknowledges sensitive details still get through. Anthropic confirmed to 404 Media that it also uses human review. One reviewer, asked whether users know: "I don't think they would imagine some contractor somewhere [...] is analyzing the conversations."

## Regulation & Policy

- **[New York seizes 12 deepfake sites holding 1,200 people's likenesses](https://www.404media.co/new-york-district-attorney-seizes-12-celebrity-deepfake-websites/)** — Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg announced the seizures at a Monday press conference, saying his office believes it is the largest such seizure to date; the sites turned roughly 1,200 people's photos into "hyper-realistic" sexual images and video, the victims including actors, athletes, musicians, politicians and first ladies of several countries. The action rests on New York's 2023 deepfake law, and Bragg's jurisdictional test was blunt: "we can take action as long as that website is accessible from Manhattan." He declined to say whether images of minors were involved, citing the ongoing investigation. It follows the DOJ and Homeland Security seizing two similar domains in June.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Washington DC settles with two more landlords over RealPage pricing, for $9.3M](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/maa-jbg-dc-realpage-lawsuit-settlement/830341/)** — announced Monday by DC Attorney General Brian Schwalb: Mid-America Apartments pays $1.2M and JBG Smith $8.1M in civil penalties, legal fees and money to affected residents, under consent judgments filed last week in DC Superior Court. This is the District's own 2023 case under the DC Antitrust Act against RealPage and 14 landlords — a third track, distinct from the DOJ consent decree we covered yesterday and the private class action in Tennessee. Both firms must stop using revenue-management software that draws on other companies' non-public data, stop sharing such data, refrain from *promoting* the use of such software, and accept an independent monitor at their own expense if the AG suspects non-compliance. The number that explains the case: per the AG's office, more than 30% of apartments in DC buildings with five or more units — and about 60% in buildings with 50 or more — have been priced using RealPage's system.
