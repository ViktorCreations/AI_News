# AI News — Wednesday, November 11, 2026

> Anthropic opens its IPO roadshow with the same-day launch of a meetings assistant, while governments move from talk to machinery: New York opens its frontier-AI registration portal and eight countries sign a Paris declaration on shutdown mechanisms.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic opens its IPO roadshow, pricing shares at $70 to $78](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-launches-ipo-roadshow-with-64b-fundraising-goal/)
SiliconANGLE reports Anthropic plans to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78, or $64.35 billion at the top of the range, with about 23 million offered by its seven co-founders, who would each receive about $250 million. It logged $17 billion in revenue and a $536 million operating loss last quarter, and says revenue in the first nine months of the year was about seven times all of 2025.

[The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-kicks-off-ipo-roadshow-offering-shares-at-70-to-78-and-targeting-a-trillion-dollar-valuation/) says the company aims to raise up to $90.4 billion including a private placement, at a valuation of about $1.02 trillion. This is the delta on last week's reports of investor pushback: the terms are now set and marketing has begun.

### [Anthropic launches Claude Meetings, Whiteboards and Sheets](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-launches-claude-meetings-to-turn-workplace-discussions-into-plans-and-tasks/)
Claude can join a video call as a visible participant, draft plans, map timelines on a whiteboard and pull figures from connected work apps while people talk; notes and action items land in claude.ai afterwards. Meetings is in beta on Team and Enterprise plans and works with Google Meet at launch. It will not join calls with external guests unless an admin authorizes it, and leaves if one joins. TechCrunch frames the launch as part of the race with OpenAI, Microsoft and Google to build office suites around AI.

### [New York opens the registration portal for its RAISE Act](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/)
Frontier developers with more than $500 million in annual revenue must register by January 1, 2027, publish safety frameworks, report critical incidents within 72 hours and issue transparency reports. A new state office, DIGIT, will oversee them, with penalties of up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million after that. Governor Kathy Hochul also launched a state AI research center at Binghamton University to evaluate advanced systems.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Eight countries and major AI labs sign the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security](https://the-decoder.com/eight-countries-and-top-ai-labs-agree-to-shutdown-mechanisms-in-new-paris-declaration/)**: adopted November 9; signatories include Altman, Amodei, Microsoft's Brad Smith, Mistral's Arthur Mensch and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. A political commitment, not binding: standardized publication of non-sensitive test results, incident sharing, independent audits and shutdown mechanisms for models under developers' control.
- **[Australia may make knowingly selling unsafe AI a crime](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/11/australia-may-make-knowingly-selling-unsafe-ai-a-crime/5308999)**: The Register's follow-up on the AI regulator announced yesterday, noting that the new regulator would require model makers to test and allow inspection.
