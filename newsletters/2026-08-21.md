## Top Stories

### [A third of web pages published since ChatGPT show signs of AI authorship](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/a-third-of-webpages-published-since-chatgpts-launch-show-signs-of-ai-authorship-study-finds/)
Pew pulled nearly half a million English-language pages from Common Crawl covering roughly five years and ran them through Pangram's detector. Across the whole sample about 10% showed significant signs of AI authorship; restricted to pages published after ChatGPT's November 2022 launch, that rises to 35%. The distribution is sharp: `.com` domains come in around ten times the rate of `.edu` and `.gov`, both near 1%, with `.org` at 4.6%. Pew is careful about the tool — detectors misclassify human writing, and the finding is offered as directionally correct at scale rather than a page-by-page verdict. The tells it flags are by now familiar to anyone who reads for a living: em dashes, Oxford commas, "it's not X, it's Y." Put beside this week's other numbers — Amazon debinding pre-2022 books, a data-labelling firm at half a billion — the picture is an industry paying steeply for the shrinking share of the web it did not write.

### [Google gives publishers a button to fight AI-driven traffic loss](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/google-gives-publishers-a-new-way-to-fight-ai-driven-traffic-losses/)
Announced Thursday: an embeddable "Preferred Sources" button that lets a reader mark a site as a favourite, feeding a signal into Search, Discover and Google News so that publisher surfaces more often. The underlying feature launched in May for Google's AI experiences and has had over 345,000 unique sources selected; Google says readers are twice as likely to click through to a preferred source when one is available. The framing is generous and the mechanism is telling — the remedy for AI summaries eating referral traffic is to ask readers to opt back in, one publisher at a time, which puts the burden on the sites losing the traffic rather than on the product taking it.

### [Grok has been returning gibberish, and xAI calls it a rare glitch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/grok-keeps-sending-gibberish-responses-to-users/)
From Wednesday morning users began getting word salad — one asking for a PDF received "match it without and your they and two for planets can practical and often cheese…" for paragraphs; another got strings of links to reinforcement-learning research sites. The Grok account called it "a rare temporary generation glitch," advising fresh chats or regeneration. It appears confined to Grok.com rather than the X integration, and TechCrunch could not reproduce it, so the blast radius looks small. No root cause has been given and xAI declined to comment, which is the part worth noting alongside the company's recent attrition: most of its founding team and 50-plus researchers and engineers have left in recent months.

## Product & Industry

- **[Ramp's spending data shows OpenAI closing on Anthropic in business accounts](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/openai-is-gaining-on-anthropic-with-business-users-new-data-indicates/)** — across more than 70,000 US businesses using Ramp's cards and bill pay, Anthropic went from 41% in May to about 44% in July while OpenAI moved from 39% to about 40%, and 56% of Ramp customers now pay for AI at all, up from 50% in March. Anthropic still leads; the claim is that OpenAI is growing faster this quarter, with a month still to run. Caveats matter here — the panel skews tech, excludes enterprises paying through Amex or similar, Ramp gives percentages rather than dollars, and businesses visibly switch after each model release.
- **[ChatGPT will now write and send your texts](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/chatgpt-can-now-send-texts-for-you-with-new-apple-messages-plugin/)** — an Apple Messages plug-in landed Thursday for ChatGPT, Codex and ChatGPT Work: sort, search, draft, edit, delete and send, running locally without building a full index of the inbox. OpenAI's own advice is the interesting bit — review before sending, and do not turn on persistent approval, because that "removes your final chance to review a message before ChatGPT sends it as you." A sentence that describes the trade in every agent product shipping right now.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Alibaba Cloud says it will keep displacing Western chips with its own](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/08/21/alibaba-cloud-plans-to-use-fewer-western-chips-to-boost-its-already-huge-ai-margins/5290815)** — on the earnings call, CEO Eddie Wu called self-developed silicon "a long-term and important direction," saying its share in Alibaba's data centres "will continue to rise, replacing more commercially purchased chips," and was explicit about why: "Commercial chips already have very high gross margins," so substituting its own lifts Alibaba's. CFO Toby Xu put AI server payback at three years against a five-year life, targeting 2.5 and possibly two. The reality check on adoption: about 650 external customers use Alibaba's own chips, against more than 120,000 for AWS's Graviton.
- **[Waymo has designed its own driving chip](https://www.theregister.com/edge-and-iot/2026/08/20/waymo-has-designed-a-robocar-chip-to-stay-ahead-of-tesla/5290592)** — revealed Thursday in a blog post ahead of a Hot Chips talk at Stanford next week: a TSMC 5nm ML ASIC rated above 1,000 TOPS, built for very low latency on the path from raw sensor data to a driving decision, supporting both CNNs and transformers, with dual-chip redundancy and 200 million-plus autonomous miles informing the design. Waymo says more custom chips are in progress; non-ML functions currently run on parts from AMD, Micron, Samsung, SanDisk and Nvidia. Tesla has been rolling its own silicon for years — the news is that the other side of that argument now is too.
