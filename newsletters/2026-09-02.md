# AI News — Wednesday, September 2, 2026

> Astra, the model OpenAI slowed on August 7 for crossing a critical cyber threshold, is coming — with a perfect score on ExploitBench and two zero-days found and exploited in a modified version of the test. Anthropic shipped Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 the same day, and finally opened the watermark detector it promised on August 14.

## Top Stories

### [Astra is on its way, with a perfect ExploitBench score](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/open-ais-astra-model-is-on-the-way-and-very-good-at-breaking-into-computer-systems/)
OpenAI says it plans to make Astra available "soon," with the most advanced cyber capabilities restricted at first. The numbers explain the caution: a perfect score on ExploitBench — which requires building a working exploit, not just finding the hole — and, on a modified version of the evaluation, two previously unknown vulnerabilities discovered and exploited without human guidance. For scale, when we covered Z.ai's GLM-5.3 on August 15 its ExploitBench figure was 54.4%, with Mythos 5 at 78%. The safeguards listed are abuse and jailbreak detection, restricted responses for accounts assessed as higher risk, additional chain-of-thought monitoring, and specific testing that the model will not break out of its environment — the last one written directly out of the Hugging Face incident. The objection worth carrying is Yona Shavit's, formerly of OpenAI: without third-party verification there is no way to tell compliant behaviour from deceptive behaviour, and every claim here is OpenAI's own.

### [Anthropic ships Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-fable-5-1-promises-better-coding-and-research-at-up-to-45-percent-less/)
Announced Tuesday, both with a million-token context and up to 128,000 tokens of output, plus mid-conversation effort adjustment and content-provenance tagging so downstream systems can check where output came from. On agentic work the jump is large: Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 55.8% against Fable 5's 42.0%, and Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 at 52.6% against 24.7%. Cache reads drop from $1 to $0.25 per million tokens while standard rates hold at $10 in and $50 out, which Anthropic says makes typical tasks about 25% cheaper and complex agentic workflows up to 45% cheaper. Treat the 45% carefully — an update to the reporting notes that at maximum effort Fable 5.1 costs about 20% *more* than Fable 5, because it emits more tokens. Mythos 5.1 is not generally available: US organisations only, through cybersecurity and life-sciences verification programmes, which is now the third frontier model this month behind identity gating. Fable 5.1 can identify software vulnerabilities but, Anthropic says, cannot develop exploits — the line OpenAI's Astra has just crossed.

### [Anthropic opens Claude's watermark detector, 18 days after promising it](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-opens-claude-ai-text-detection-to-regulators-media-fact-checkers-and-others/)
Regulators, law enforcement, media, fact-checkers, independent researchers, educational organisations and EU civil society groups can now request API access to check whether text carries Claude's watermark, along with enterprises verifying their own compliance. This was the missing piece we flagged on August 15: Anthropic had published how the SynthID-derived mark works while the detector remained "soon," which left the company holding the only key. It is no longer the only holder, though it still decides who gets one. The caveats from the original mechanics still apply — sparse marking on factual passages, little on exact code, weak on short samples — and the criticism Gruber made about biasing word choice has not gone away.

## Research & Models

- **[Gemini decides which parts of a video to actually look at, cutting tokens by up to 88%](https://the-decoder.com/google-geminis-new-agent-based-video-analysis-cuts-token-usage-by-up-to-88-percent/)** — live now in the Gemini API at standard rates: instead of sampling frames at a fixed rate, the model runs a decision loop over native video tools, choosing which sections to examine, at what speed, and through which modality — frames, audio or transcript. Google reports up to 88% fewer tokens on 1H-VideoQA and LongVideoBench for 66% lower cost, with accuracy slightly improved rather than traded away. In AI Studio and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform now, with the Gemini app and YouTube's "Ask YouTube" to follow. It is the same lesson as Writer's harness result last month: the scaffolding around the model is where the cost sits.

## Product & Industry

- **[Google's election AI Overviews lean on ten domains and sometimes take sides](https://the-decoder.com/googles-election-ai-overviews-are-opaque-rely-on-few-sources-and-sometimes-take-sides/)** — AlgorithmWatch ran 4,480 queries about three eastern German elections through Google's DSA-mandated researcher API. Nearly half of all links pointed to just ten domains, with YouTube — Google's own — cited most often. Overviews appeared for 39.1% of election queries against 65.3% of non-political ones, and for AfD questions only 24% of the time against 45–58% for other parties. The language is not neutral either: in Saxony-Anhalt the CDU drew positive framing in 81.5% of overviews and the AfD in none. Google would not say what determines when an overview appears, or whether its 2024 election safeguards are still running. Two things to note — this is the same group whose abortion-referral audit we covered on August 24, and the study exists because the DSA forces researcher access, the obligation that arrived for ChatGPT Search on Monday.

## Capital & Deals

- **[AfterQuery hits $3.2B, Y Combinator's fastest unicorn](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/afterquery-reportedly-becomes-y-combinators-fastest-ever-unicorn-now-valued-at-3-2b/)** — eighteen months out of the Winter 2025 batch, founders aged 22 and 23, and a 10x valuation move in under five months from the $30M Series A at $300M in April. It sells the same thing Micro1 and Mercor do: professional expertise as training data, doctors and lawyers encoding how their work is actually done, with $100M annualised revenue as of April and Nvidia among the customers. It is the second expert-data valuation we have reported in two weeks, after Micro1's $500M gross run rate on August 21 — the market pricing the constraint we have been tracking all month — the models are not short of compute, they are short of examples of expert work.
