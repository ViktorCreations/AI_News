# AI News — Monday, September 21, 2026

> The UN's scientific panel on AI published its first thematic brief, and its first assessment of the Hugging Face hack, with an argument aimed squarely at everyone asking for proof before rules: loss of control is precisely the kind of risk the precautionary principle exists for, and governments should not wait for scientific certainty to constrain AI agents. It lands as world leaders gather in New York and the US and China hold AI talks.

## Top Stories

### [Amazon blocks Meta's shopping agent, and the agent-access war gets its clearest test](https://www.theverge.com/tech/998078/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-ai-agent-shopping)
From Sunday, Muse users trying to buy on Amazon met a popup: "continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed." Per GeekWire, Meta never told Amazon that Muse would be shopping its store, and Amazon's objections are specific rather than merely territorial — that Muse does not identify itself while browsing, and appears to capture customer credentials. Amazon's framing is the part worth quoting, because it is the position the whole industry will now argue over: "third-party applications that offer to make purchases on behalf of customers from other businesses should operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate." Meta said at launch that Muse cannot see login details or card information, though reports since suggest it can read the contents of user messages without the relevant permission enabled. This is Amazon's second run at the problem — it sued Perplexity last November over Comet's shopping and lost in August when a judge sided with Perplexity — and it has been quietly stripping item names and images out of its confirmation emails since July to keep that data away from outside models. An agent economy requires someone's storefront, and the storefronts have not agreed to host it.

### [Nobody in world models will say what they are building](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/20/world-model-companies-are-keeping-a-lot-of-secrets/)
A useful piece of reporting on the least legible corner of the field, where the two marquee names — Yann LeCun's AMI Labs and Fei-Fei Li's World Labs — have raised enormous sums and disclosed almost nothing about commercialisation. Pressed on what AMI is working on, co-founder and VP of World Models Michael Rabbat said "we'll talk about it when we're ready to talk about it," clarifying by email that the company is "still in a research and building phase." AMI is under a year old, so reticence is defensible; what makes it notable is that the secrecy extends even to suppliers. Alex de Vigan, CEO of the data supplier Physicl, says he does not know what his own data is being used to build: "I wish they would tell us more. We could build more useful data if we knew what they were working on." The ambiguity is partly intrinsic — a model that helps a Waymo read traffic could equally drive a humanoid or turn a video clip into an explorable space, and AMI has touched manufacturing, biomedicine, robotics and clinical software already. But a field whose products are undefined while its funding is not is worth watching with the same eye we would apply anywhere else.

## Research & Models

- **[Alibaba's 7B open-weight image model claims to beat closed rivals](https://the-decoder.com/alibabas-open-weight-qwen-image-2-1-claims-to-beat-closed-models-in-image-generation-with-just-7-billion-parameters/)** — Qwen-Image-2.1 has a 7-billion-parameter visual generation component and runs on a 3090, yet Qwen says it beats most closed models — on Qwen's own benchmark, with independent evaluation still pending, which is the caveat that matters. The genuinely useful features are practical rather than headline: native RGBA generation and editing, so objects and text can be isolated on transparent layers; up to ten reference images at once for group portraits, virtual try-ons or room design; and circles, masks or painted marks to steer local edits. On Hugging Face, GitHub and ModelScope — but the research licence bars commercial use, so "open-weight" here means inspectable, not free to deploy.
- **[Runway wants video generation to become a stream you steer](https://the-decoder.com/runway-wants-to-turn-ai-video-generation-into-a-live-stream-you-control-in-real-time/)** — research rather than a product: instead of prompt-wait-discard-repeat, minimise time to first frame and then generate continuously while the user directs. It runs on GWM-1, the General World Model Runway introduced in December 2025, which builds on Gen-4.5, generates frame by frame and accepts camera moves, robot commands or audio as control signals. The same stack produced Solaris, which generates user interfaces frame by frame and responds to clicks and voice. Read alongside the item above, this is what world models look like when someone does say what they are for.
- **[Tencent's Gander splits the model into a talker and a thinker](https://the-decoder.com/tencents-gander-aims-to-keep-talking-while-it-works-in-the-background/)** — an architecture aimed at the most irritating failure of voice agents, the silence while something is being worked out. A "cerebellum" holds the conversation second by second across speech, images and text and can be interrupted at any point, while a swappable "brain" grinds through the actual agent task in the background. The design goal is to stop fast response and adequate planning from trading off against each other.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The UN panel invokes the precautionary principle for AI](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/998090/un-ai-panel-hugging-face-hack-precautionary-principle)** — the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, set up last year as the UN's first global scientific body on the subject, used its first thematic brief to assess OpenAI's Hugging Face hack and to make a jurisdictional argument rather than a technical one. Governments, it says, need not wait for scientists to establish exactly how or why such incidents happen before imposing safeguards: loss of control is "one where potential harm may be catastrophic or irreversible, even as its likelihood remains scientifically uncertain" — the textbook case for the precautionary principle, first written into the 1992 Rio Declaration and since embedded in environmental and public health law, particularly in the EU. The brief calls for far more attention and resource on emerging risks plus stronger international coordination on safety and accountability, and notes that incidents have now been documented at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta, including attacks on real targets and swarms of agents taking over message boards. It arrives with the General Assembly convening in New York and US–China AI talks under way, and follows Secretary-General António Guterres warning last week that "the world cannot afford a race to the bottom on AI safety." Set against Trump's weekend claim that the backlash is a manufactured hoax, the disagreement is no longer about facts but about who bears the burden of proof.
- **[Data centers dominated Congressional Black Caucus week](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/20/ai-data-center-alarms-dominate-cbc-week-in-washington.html)** — AI and data-center concerns, alongside redistricting, ran through the caucus's annual Washington gathering, with lobbying interests on both sides of the question turning out. Worth registering precisely because of the weekend's framing from the White House: the opposition to data-center siting is organised, community-level and showing up at political gatherings, which is not what an astroturfed campaign looks like.
