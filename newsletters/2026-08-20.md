## Top Stories

### [Binance opens live trading to AI agents](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/binance-now-lets-ai-agents-trade-but-keeping-them-in-check-is-largely-up-to-users/)
Agent OS launched Thursday: connect ChatGPT, Claude Code or Cursor to Binance through APIs, wallet services and MCP, and let the agent read market data, view accounts and place trades. The guardrails are real but user-configurable — agents run in isolated sub-accounts with withdrawals blocked by default, per-trade approval optional, and caps of $100,000 a day on DeFi transactions, $50,000 on swaps and $20 on payments. The candid part is where responsibility lands. VP Jeff Li says Binance "cannot see the reasoning" behind an agent's decisions, so the sub-account boundary is the primary defence against a compromised agent, and configuring it is the user's job. Read next to this week's other agent stories — an agent recommending a malware package, Copilot explaining its own bypass — the notable step is attaching an agent directly to a live balance at this scale.

### [Pew: 52% of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI, and 9% are more excited](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/19/more-than-half-of-americans-now-view-ai-negatively/5289736)
The excited share has fallen from 18% in 2021 to 9%. On jobs, 71% now expect AI to mean fewer of them over the next two decades, up from 64% two years ago, against 5% who expect growth. The generational detail cuts against the usual assumption: among 18-to-29-year-olds, concern has gone from under a third in 2021 to 55%, with 73% expecting job losses, and they are likelier than older groups to see AI as bad for social connection and creativity. This is the measurement behind the argument Dario Amodei and Gavin Baker had over the weekend about whether safety talk caused the backlash — the young cohort, the one that adopted these tools fastest, is souring fastest too.

### [OpenAI stripped security researchers of cyber-program access, then called it a technical error](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/researchers-complain-that-openai-revoked-their-access-to-limited-cyber-program/)
Five researchers told TechCrunch their Daybreak Blue access under the Trusted Access for Cyber programme vanished on Wednesday — all of them based outside the US and Europe, though it is not clear why, or how many were affected. OpenAI called it "an issue on our end, and not the user experience we want to deliver," blamed a recent technical fault, and asked those affected to reapply and reverify. The programme is ten days old: Daybreak Blue and the higher Daybreak Red tier launched on August 10 alongside GPT-5.6-Cyber, with Red gated behind identity verification, legal declarations and — from September 1 — hardware security keys. A vetting regime that quietly drops a geographic cohort and calls it a glitch is going to be read as something else by the people it dropped.

## Research & Models

- **[Ornith-1.5 ships 397B open weights under MIT, trained on tasks it wrote itself](https://huggingface.co/ornith-ai/Ornith-1.5-397B)** — released Wednesday in 397B and 35B mixture-of-experts and 9B dense variants, with a 262,144-token context extensible to about a million via YaRN. The method is the claim: rather than train on fixed human-curated tasks, the loop "jointly optimis[es] task generation, scaffold construction, and solution rollouts" — the model proposes new problems, builds the harness to attempt them, and feeds its own attempts back as training data. The model card reports 86.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 86 on SWE-bench Verified and 92.8 on GPQA Diamond; the team puts Terminal-Bench slightly ahead of Claude Opus 4.8's 85.0, though it trails on DeepSWE. It builds on Ornith-1.0, itself derived from Qwen3.5 and Gemma4 — an MIT-licensed model at this scale is derived from two other open families, which is what a healthy open ecosystem compounding looks like.

## Product & Industry

- **[OpenAI pitches safety monitoring that keeps none of your data](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/openai-seeks-to-one-up-anthropic-with-new-customer-privacy-protections/)** — "Private Safety Processing," in preview for selected customers from Wednesday, runs automated abuse detection across conversations without retaining them, emitting a "narrowly defined signal" about the activity type rather than the content, with customers free to volunteer context if asked. The target is explicit: Anthropic retains user data for 30 days on covered models like Fable for safety analysis, which has unsettled enterprises with sensitive material. Anthropic's counter is that its human review runs through controlled access paths with tamper-proof logging. Enterprise privacy has become a competitive axis rather than a compliance checkbox.
- **[Google answers OpenAI's teen study mode within a day](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/google-launches-new-study-tools-for-students-across-search-and-gemini/)** — announced Wednesday, a day after ChatGPT for Teens: AI-generated interactive visuals and 3D simulations in Search, custom practice quizzes, study documents built from uploaded PDFs, slides or handwritten notes, Lens-guided problem solving, and in Gemini a dedicated learning hub with flashcards, notebooks and multi-step research you can talk through in Gemini Live. Most of it ships now, with Lens following in the coming weeks.
- **[An agent recommended a malware package and nearly got it installed](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/20/ai-agent-suggested-installing-a-malware-package-engineer-almost-took-its-advice/5289849)** — at consultancy Softjourn, an engineer asked an agent for a library and got a plausible-sounding name back. What stopped it was policy, not judgment: the firm requires verifying AI-recommended packages, and the check showed a repository created days earlier with almost no downloads. Managing director Sergiy Fitsak names the pattern — models invent package names that sound real, and attackers register them, a practice researchers now call slopsquatting. "Skipping that step once is how a team ends up explaining a supply chain compromise instead of shipping a feature on time."

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Marvell hands Google a $12.2B warrant to get inside the TPU ecosystem](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/08/19/google-pits-marvell-against-broadcom-as-it-chases-ai-crown/5289902)** — per an SEC filing this week, Marvell will develop custom silicon attaching to the TPU ecosystem — inference accelerators, storage and network controllers, memory interface controllers, near-memory compute — and has issued Google a warrant for nearly 59 million shares, roughly $12.2B. The filing does not say whether Google will exercise it. Google's TPU work with Broadcom only became public this year, and this does not end it; it gives Google two designers to play off against each other on price and performance. A supplier paying its customer for the privilege of supplying it is a useful indicator of who holds the leverage in AI silicon right now.
- **[TerraPower's pitch for data centres is a vat of molten sodium](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/terrapowers-nuclear-reactor-has-a-secret-weapon-for-powering-ai-data-centers/)** — the 345MW Natrium reactor runs flat out and dumps surplus heat into a molten sodium reservoir, drawing on it for extra steam when demand spikes, which lets very expensive equipment stay at full output instead of throttling. That matters for a load that is both enormous and spiky. Meta agreed in January to buy eight Natrium plants; the first reactor is under construction in Wyoming, and TerraPower says it will name a first data-centre project this year with groundbreaking in 2027.
