## Top Stories

### [Anthropic names Chinese labs in a 200-million-exchange distillation report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/anthropic-details-distillation-campaigns-from-alibaba-moonshot-ai-and-deepseek/)
Published Thursday, covering misuse Anthropic says it detected and disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026 across seven harm areas — cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance, scams and fraud, biological misuse, conventional weapons, and illicit distillation. The distillation numbers are the ones to hold onto. Operators linked to Alibaba ran the largest campaign Anthropic has seen: more than 151 million exchanges between May and July across some 3,500 fraudulent accounts, peaking near 3 million a day, aimed at Claude's agentic and tool-use behaviour, coding and data analysis, and reasoning — with extraction attempts disguised as translation requests to surface internal reasoning traces. A separate campaign Anthropic associates with Moonshot AI appeared to route requests from Chinese military sources, roughly 300,000 over ten days through 5,000 accounts, mostly against Opus, including asking Claude to judge from surveillance footage whether someone was "behaving abnormally." Anthropic says it banned the accounts and tightened safeguards. None of the named companies had commented.

### [OpenAI opens the infrastructure behind Codex and ChatGPT as an API](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents-api/overview)
The Agents API is in public beta, and what it sells is not a model but the plumbing: cloud agents that run for hours, execute code and process files, with automatic context management, parallel tool use and delegation to sub-agents. Developers can run sandboxes on OpenAI or with Cloudflare, Vercel or Oracle, and it speaks MCP alongside custom functions and built-in tools like web search. There is no infrastructure surcharge — billing is tokens only. That pricing choice is the strategic part: OpenAI is giving away the operational layer everyone has been rebuilding by hand, and taking its margin on the inference the layer generates.

### [A Fields Medallist is starting an institute to prove AI safety mathematically](https://the-decoder.com/the-mathematical-ai-safety-institute-wants-to-prove-ai-is-safe-the-way-cryptographers-prove-codes-are-unbreakable/)
When we covered Jacob Tsimerman's Fields Medal on July 27, the notable part was that he had turned his research toward AI safety and was organising events to prepare mathematicians for it. That has now become an institution: the New York Times reported Tuesday that he is founding the Mathematical AI Safety Institute, an independent outfit in the Bay Area opening in January with a planned 10 to 30 mathematicians, and that he is joining OpenAI's safety team alongside it. The premise is the gap between cryptography and AI safety — a cipher can be reasoned about formally, whereas an AI system's safety today can only be probed empirically and declared untested-so-far. MAISI wants theoretical definitions strong enough to support proofs, including zero-knowledge techniques that would let a lab demonstrate a safety property without exposing the model. Worth watching precisely because it might not work: nobody has a formal definition of "this model will not deceive you" to prove anything about yet.

## Product & Industry

- **[A close reading of Astra as a coding model is brutal](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/7/astra-why/)** — Armin Ronacher's argument is that frontier model development has become involution: more effort, more cost, no proportional gain for actual software engineering. His specifics are concrete. Astra writes token-optimised code that is hard to read — Python heredocs doing string replacement instead of using edit tools, unit tests compressed onto single lines for roughly 10% token savings — and those habits leak into code meant to be maintained. His automated run went 35 hours, burned 4 billion tokens for about $1,200, and produced 75,000 net lines across 79 commits without stopping or delivering anything of value. The benchmark scores and the workbench experience have come apart.
- **[Meta's Muse is the No. 2 US iPhone app, and the number underneath is small](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/metas-ai-agent-muse-is-now-the-no-2-app-in-the-us/)** — Sensor Tower puts it at about 83,000 US iOS downloads since Tuesday's launch, up from fourth place on Wednesday. The rank flatters it: Threads did 4.3 million US downloads on day one, ChatGPT cleared 500,000 US installs in its first week, and Muse sits at No. 338 in Google Play's productivity category. A chart position in a quiet week is not adoption.
- **[OpenAI stopped selling Pro subscriptions because Astra demand outran capacity](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/openai-puts-pro-subscriptions-on-hold-due-to-astra-demand/)** — new sign-ups to the $200-a-month tier were paused Thursday, a week after Astra shipped; the API, Plus and Go remain open. Product lead Thibault Sottiaux: "Demand for Astra is really unprecedented. We're pulling all the levers possible to sustain the demand," and the pause was "the smallest step that allows us to continue giving the broadest access possible." No date for resuming.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Google is buying half a Finnish nuclear plant's output to 2049](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/google-signs-up-for-electricity-from-finnish-nuclear-power-plant)** — announced Wednesday alongside a €13B ($15B) Finnish data-centre programme for 2027–28 across Hamina, Muhos, Vaala and Kajaani. The agreement with Fortum takes up to 50% of the Loviisa plant's capacity from 2030 to 2049, starting smaller in 2028, and underwrites a roughly €1B Fortum programme needed to keep the plant running to the end of its licence rather than closing — about €700M of which still awaits investment decisions. It is Google's first nuclear deal outside the US. Finnish opposition politicians have already warned about strain on the national supply.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Multifamily operators are using AI on maintenance, and refusing to cut headcount with it](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/multifamily-operators-ai-apartment-maintenance/828275/)** — reported August 21: Greystar runs a closed enterprise platform that synthesises inspection data so field teams can act on it faster; CAPREIT is looking at inspection camera footage, appliance life-cycle validation and warranty tracking to forecast spend; RPM Living is still evaluating and has implemented nothing. All three say the same two things — the ROI is not yet calculable, and the tool is not a way to shrink the maintenance team. "AI is a great tool, but it's not a replacement for a maintenance team member," as RPM's VP put it. Useful as a corrective to vendor decks: the buyers closest to the work are moving slowly and saying why.
- **[Proptech is now the biggest source of $5–10M seed rounds of any industry](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/08/proptech-seed-rounds-2026-amounts/)** — an August 25 look at the numbers: $4.53B across 231 rounds in the first half, a median round of $6.75M, with MetaProp's data showing seed sizes rising from $3.6M in 2022 to $6.3M now, and CRETI counting 30 deals in the $5–10M band worth $207.3M in H1 alone. Recent seeds include Breezy, Grotto AI and Hint at $10M each. The mechanism investors describe is AI shortening the path from demo to signature — which is also the reason to be careful, since a slick demo is exactly what AI makes cheap.
