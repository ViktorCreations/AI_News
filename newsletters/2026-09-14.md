## Top Stories

### [Altman turns "pacing" into a policy ask, and draws its limits](https://the-decoder.com/sam-altman-calls-for-pacing-ai-development-but-promises-rapid-progress-will-continue/)
Posting today, two days after Dario Amodei's essay and a day after endorsing part of it, Altman set out what he actually means. Pacing is not stopping: progress stays "rapid," just not faster than safety work can absorb. The concrete asks are a uniform, nationwide safety framework for advanced AI — one standard rather than a state patchwork — and US government backing for any international coordination, with the pointed caveat that the industry should not wait for lawmakers before acting. OpenAI, he says, has begun setting explicit safety criteria before major training runs. The line worth keeping is "no amount of American competitive pressure should justify recklessness," because the competitive-pressure argument is exactly what every previous version of this debate has ended on. What none of last week's four statements resolves is the enforcement question OpenAI itself raised with Congress on Friday: voluntary coordination among competitors is the shape antitrust law exists to prohibit.

### [The obvious question about a fortnight of doom warnings](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/13/whats-behind-the-ai-industrys-latest-warnings-of-doom/)
TechCrunch's Equity team put the sceptical case on Sunday, and it deserves a hearing alongside the warnings themselves. Kirsten Korosec's reading is commercial: talking up how dangerous your technology is can be "a weird way of flexing" capability, and it lands with Anthropic's IPO approaching. Anthony Ha's is operational — the labs are saying this because they are "not really in control of these models anymore," which the month's agent incidents support. He is also blunt about the numbers: a stated ">10%" chance of catastrophe is a made-up figure rather than a calculation, and the apocalyptic register crowds out nearer harms like labour displacement and energy use. Both readings can be true at once, which is the uncomfortable part. Our own coverage this month has run the warnings straight; this is the counterweight.

### [Obama tells Democrats to make AI a central agenda](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/13/obama-urges-democrats-to-have-a-clear-plan-for-ai-safeguards/)
Speaking Thursday at a Democratic fundraiser, answering a question from Hakeem Jeffries about how congressional Democrats should approach AI: "This is something that is moving very fast in private hands, and if we don't get on top of it, I think can be dangerous. If we do get on top of it, I do think it's beneficial." His advice was to prepare a framework now for "a very public conversation" on regaining the House. He holds no office and this is not policy, but it is the clearest signal yet that AI safeguards are becoming an electoral position rather than a technical one — and TechCrunch notes he has been a sounding board to both Amodei and Altman, which makes the timing of last week's essays look less coincidental.

## Research & Models

- **[Two open-weight search agents top their class](https://huggingface.co/collections/AllSpark-Research/iris)** — AllSpark released Iris-mini and Iris-pro on Sunday, agents that decide what to search for, read results and judge when they have enough evidence, built on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B respectively. Iris-mini scores 82.2 on BrowseComp, 84.8 on BrowseComp-ZH, 86.9 on DeepSearchQA and 52.3 on Humanity's Last Exam, leading three of the four among comparable open models; Iris-pro reaches 88.6, 85.1, 92.9 and 56.4. Weights are on Hugging Face with code on GitHub. The pattern of the past two months holds: the open-weight frontier for agentic work is being set on Chinese base models.

## Product & Industry

- **[ElevenLabs ships Music v2.5 and leans on its licensing](https://the-decoder.com/elevenlabs-makes-music-v2-5-available-via-app-and-api-with-free-and-pro-tier-options/)** — out Sunday through the ElevenMusic app and the API, with fuller and more natural output; in blind comparison across 47,885 track pairs listeners preferred v2.5, most strongly on R&B, soul, hip-hop, rock and orchestral. Free tier gives five lossless downloads a day with attribution, Pro gives 400 a month, and users keep ownership with commercial use depending on purpose. Downloads imitating a named artist are blocked, as is using someone else's song as a reference. The company says it trained on licensed stems and music — the same ground Suno moved onto last week when it rebuilt on Warner, BMG and Believe catalogue and retired its older models.
