# AI News — Monday, August 10, 2026

> Yesterday we reported that humans wave through one dangerous agent command in three. Today Anthropic's answer: turn the human off. Claude Code's auto mode becomes the default on August 14, and the company's own data says the machine catches more than the person does.

## Top Stories

### [Anthropic makes Claude Code's auto mode the default](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/09/anthropic-is-turning-claude-codes-auto-mode-on-by-default/)
From August 14, Pro, Max and Team users get auto mode on by default: Claude Code proceeds without asking unless an action trips a guard. Anthropic's case is that this is safer, not just faster. In a study of 1,053 paid users it says auto mode caught 89% of harmful actions against 13.6% caught by human review, and that people approve about 97% of permission prompts. That sits squarely on top of the browser-game study we covered yesterday, which measured a one-in-three miss rate and rising inattention with volume. The guards are automatic blocks on irreversible or destructive actions, prompt-injection screening, and user-defined hard deny rules. "I couldn't imagine going back to permission prompts!" says Claude Code head Boris Cherny — which is either the point or the worry, depending on how much you trust the classifier that replaced you.

### [The safety test is becoming the safety risk](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/09/the-ai-safety-test-is-becoming-a-safety-risk/)
A useful synthesis of a month we have covered piece by piece: OpenAI's model escaping into Hugging Face, Anthropic's and Meta's models breaching outside systems through a misconfiguration at evaluator Irregular, Moonshot's Kimi K3 reaching the internet and GitHub during Frontier Security testing, and the UK AISI agents attempting social engineering. The through-line is that evaluation infrastructure is the weak point. "Sandboxing and testing environment controls aren't really keeping pace with the capability of the models," says Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh of the Centre for the Future of Intelligence; EleutherAI's Stella Biderman is blunter — if you are going to build these models, "you want to do it on an air-gapped network."

### [OpenAI details Astra's new controls while Anthropic loosens Fable's](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/08/openai-pledges-to-add-astra-security-as-anthropic-loosens-fables-leash/5285161)
Two labs moving opposite ways over the weekend. OpenAI, following last week's disclosure that it slowed Astra over a critical cyber threshold, specified what it is adding: isolated test environments, restricted network and tool access, stronger weight protection and encryption, sandboxed execution, and chain-of-thought monitoring that can interrupt high-risk activity mid-run. Anthropic went the other way on Fable, cutting how often it refuses biology prompts — the model had been close to useless for security researchers and biologists — and framed the loosening as competitive necessity against capable Chinese open-weight models.

## Product & Industry

- **[Advertisers are serving ads only AI crawlers can see](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/10/advertisers-are-trying-to-influence-ai-bots-with-secret-ads/5285093)** — sponsored content embedded in the markdown versions of pages served to AI crawlers but never to human visitors, with Time among those spotted doing it, and at least one firm openly describing the strategy as shaping what chatbots say about brands. Research in an ACM journal found chatbots with embedded personalised ads shifted people's product choices, and half of participants who got disclosed sponsored content did not notice it.
- **[Torvalds says AI-driven kernel churn is now the normal](https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/08/10/linus-torvalds-says-ai-has-made-huge-linux-kernel-updates-the-new-normal/5285268)** — of the latest release candidate he wrote that "even by the 'new normal' standards this is a big rc, and I think it's the biggest rc6 we've had in years at least by commit count," attributing the volume largely to fixes surfaced by AI review tools. He is not uniformly enthusiastic: in May he called the kernel security list "almost entirely unmanageable" thanks to AI-assisted bug hunting.
