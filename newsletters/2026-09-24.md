## Top Stories

### [A Pentagon review blames AI overreliance for a strike that killed 123 children](https://gizmodo.com/pentagon-investigators-say-overreliance-on-palantir-ai-tech-contributed-to-u-s-strike-that-killed-123-iranian-children-2000814477)
An unreleased internal review, first reported by Bloomberg, finds that a cascade of preventable failures led to two Tomahawk missiles hitting the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, southern Iran, on February 28, the opening day of the Iran war. More than 150 people died, at least 123 of them children. US databases had listed the compound as an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps facility for years; commercial satellite imagery from 2017 and 2018 showed finished walls, separate entrances, a soccer pitch and playground markings. The site was fed into Palantir's Maven Smart System alongside other candidates and came out a recommended day-one target. Investigators found that some Central Command personnel expected Maven itself to flag outdated records and intelligence contradictions, though the review could not establish why they expected that. Palantir told Bloomberg it "is not responsible for the underlying data nor identifying intelligence deficiencies," disputing that its software was at fault. Both things can be true, and together they describe the failure precisely: the tool did what it was built to do, and the people using it believed it was doing something more.

### [Anthropic says Claude found an enzyme system nobody had described](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system)
The wet lab we noted on September 19 has produced its first public result, announced Wednesday: roughly 950 Claude agents spent 21 hours and about 210 million tokens mining DNA sequence databases, pulling more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, narrowing to some 3,500 candidate systems and then to 20 for detailed write-up. One of them is a previously uncharacterised arrangement in bacteriophage DNA that Anthropic calls array-associated reverse transcriptases, or ART: a reverse transcriptase, a partner gene of unknown function, and beside them a long array of evenly spaced DNA repeats — structurally reminiscent of a CRISPR array. The array produces a set of distinct short RNAs, which is the property that makes CRISPR programmable. Anthropic is explicit that it does not know what ART does, and equally explicit that every laboratory step was done by human scientists in its Bay Area lab at BSL-1 and BSL-2. Speaking to TechCrunch, Amodei acknowledged that Stanford researchers had previously described a system similar in some ways. A preprint is out and validation is now the field's job, not the company's — which is the right framing for a result whose significance nobody can yet size, and the reason to treat "AI made a discovery" as a claim with a long tail rather than a finished fact.

### [The first autonomous AI command-and-control implant takes orders from a four-model committee](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/the-closed-quorum-inside-the-first-reported-autonomous-ai-c2-implant/)
Cisco Talos published CLOSEDQUORUM on September 22: a Windows implant that, instead of phoning home to an attacker's server, gathers host information, submits structured requests to as many as four commercial model providers — DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral and Google Gemini — and executes whichever next action wins a plurality vote, with a fixed tie-break order. The model providers are the command-and-control infrastructure. Talos calls it the first publicly documented Windows implant to run tactical C2 this way, and says plainly that it has no confirmation of deployment in the wild; the analysed binary carries no working API credentials and looks like a template. The shift it demonstrates is not capability but effort: the operator is removed from a bounded phase of the attack chain. Talos released its malware-hunting toolkit, CAIRN, as open source alongside the writeup.

## Research & Models

- **[OpenAI publishes MentalHealthBench](https://openai.com/index/introducing-mentalhealthbench)** — announced Wednesday: 1,215 synthetic conversations paired with 5,262 rubric criteria written by a cohort of more than 80 licensed psychologists and psychiatrists across 22 countries, split between everyday non-acute exchanges (53.5%), high-acuity conversations (18.2%) and emergencies (28.3%). Criteria are weighted from −10 to +10, so harmful behaviours subtract. No model does well: GPT-6 Astra leads on 57.3%, ahead of GPT-6 Sol at 53.9%, Claude Opus 5.5 at 52.4% and GPT-6 Luna at 50.2%. The benchmark is released openly.
- **[Alphabet's Intrinsic open-sources the core of its industrial robotics stack](https://www.intrinsic.ai/blog/posts/introducing-intrinsic-core)** — announced September 22 at ROSCon in Toronto under Apache 2.0: a hardware-agnostic real-time control framework, digital twin, motion and grasp planning, pose estimation built on Nvidia FoundationPose, Gazebo-backed simulation, camera calibration and ROS-compatible drivers — the same components Intrinsic says it uses in real manufacturing deployments.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Altman and Amodei ask the UN Security Council for international AI rules](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/23/tech/altman-amodei-ai-safety-un-security-council)** — speaking separately to the Council on Wednesday, the two rivals made the same request. Altman, in person, urged standards for "measuring capabilities, assessing risks, determining whether safeguards are sufficient and preserving meaningful human oversight," plus fast incident reporting so the "world can learn from failures before they become catastrophes" — while saying decisions should rest with governments "accountable to the people they serve," a formulation that would exclude China. Amodei, by video, proposed narrow global agreements such as a ban on using AI for biological weapons, verification systems so states can check each other's commitments, and common testing standards with an incident-notification channel: "I believe that this is the most important global security issue facing the world today." No written agreement was expected and none emerged; an OpenAI official called it a "kernel of an idea."
- **[Trump tells the General Assembly that AI oversight is a "globalist scheme"](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/trump-un-ai-globalist-scheme-altman-amodei-security-council/)** — the president used his UN address on Tuesday to reject international AI governance outright, comparing its advocates to climate advocates and saying "I'm not going to stifle growth of something that will be bigger than the industrial revolution." The two speeches were a day and a few hundred metres apart, and the gap between them is the actual state of AI governance: the companies are asking for rules that the government they are headquartered in had rejected from the same podium the day before.

- **Context: the appraisal data standard behind lender automation becomes mandatory on November 2.** The GSEs' [UAD redesign timeline](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheet/uad-redesign-timeline.pdf) — a joint Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae effort run at FHFA's direction — has allowed either UAD 2.6 or 3.6 since broad production opened on January 26. From November 2 every new submission to the Uniform Collateral Data Portal must be UAD 3.6, with a fatal error returned on anything else; the 2.6 pipeline is cleared by May 3, 2027. Since August 6 the portal has been returning a warning on every 2.6 submission saying so. It is a file-format deadline, which is why it gets no coverage, and it is also the schema that any automated appraisal review a lender builds will have to read. Six weeks out.
