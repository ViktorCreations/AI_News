# AI News — Saturday, November 7, 2026

> An outside assessment of Anthropic's own research finds AI progress there running at roughly twice its historical rate, mostly because of AI-assisted R&D, just as data on spending and investor pushback on valuations suggest the money side of the boom is cooling.

## Top Stories

### [METR finds Anthropic's capability progress is running at about twice its historical rate, with AI doing much of the accelerating](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)
METR, a nonprofit evaluator, was given access from 12 August to 29 October to Anthropic's internal data on benchmarks, training metrics, staff surveys and interviews. Its 5 November report concludes that Anthropic is "likely improving the capabilities of their models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate" of March 2024 to October 2025, with high uncertainty: the true figure could be 1.3 times or 3 times. A majority of the acceleration is likely attributable to Anthropic's extensive use of AI in its own R&D, and METR says it is confident it was not driven by faster scaling of training compute.

METR adds that the lack of observed bottlenecks means further progress could bring faster acceleration. It also says Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, which Anthropic described as aimed at keeping confidential information from accelerating other developers, so the public must rely on METR's judgment more than is ideal. The report lands days after Anthropic's revised IPO filing said it [may "imminently" meet](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-warns-that-its-very-close-to-rsi-capability-aka-self-building-ai/) its own acceleration threshold.

### [Ramp data: AI spending flatlined over late summer while token use kept rising](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/ramp-data-shows-ai-spending-stalling-just-as-anthropic-nears-its-ipo/)
Ramp's report shows token spend grew only 1.5% across August and September, against 42% month-on-month growth in July. Ramp's lead economist Ara Kharazian says almost all AI spending comes from venture-backed, high-growth companies, and that the top 1% of firms, which had reached about $10,000 per employee at their summer peak, stuttered slightly.

Median firms spend $12 to $15 per employee per month and the top 10% spend $600 to $1,000. Kharazian told TechCrunch that "the era of hypergrowth may be over," and that price competition and savvier customers may be capping revenue for OpenAI and Anthropic.

## Research & Models

- **[Thinking Machines introduces Inkling 2 Small, with weights promised next week](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)**: a 594B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 17B active, scoring 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. It is available now through the company's Tinker platform with closed weights; the company says full weights under Apache 2.0 follow "next week" after external safety testing, so it has not yet been released.
- **[Ricursive Intelligence releases Ripple-65B Preview, an Apache 2.0 open model](https://the-decoder.com/ripple-65b-preview-delivers-strong-agentic-coding-results-as-a-fully-apache-2-0-open-us-model/)**: weights are on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ricursiveAI/Ripple-65B-Preview). The company claims 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, the best among open US models, though The Decoder notes it trails Inkling 2 Small on other benchmarks.
- **[Google Research: a smartwatch AI flags high blood pressure without a cuff](https://the-decoder.com/googles-ai-detects-high-blood-pressure-from-a-regular-smartwatch-without-a-cuff/)**: published in Nature, per The Decoder. Trained on nearly 500,000 hours of pulse and motion data, it identified 65.8% of people with hypertension in a 196-person study, against 55.3% for in-office measurement.
