> Two new reports show AI running cyberattacks at the scale of a whole campaign: researchers who got inside a criminal group's own systems found Claude, Grok and Kimi models coordinating attacks on more than 400 Latin American financial firms, and Microsoft says a China-aligned group used an open-source agent framework to reach over 2,250 cloud tenants through ConnectWise. ## Top Stories

### [A criminal group ran attacks on 400+ Latin American banks with Claude, Grok and Kimi models](https://www.straiker.ai/blog/inside-carliy0s-how-cybercartel-used-multiple-ai-models-to-attack-latin-american-banks)
Published Wednesday by the security firm Straiker, from evidence inside the attackers' own working environment. It says CyberCartel, a group tracked since 2012 and now operating as Tr4nsHack Team under a front company called Hexagon Security Labs Mexico, built a multi-agent attack system, CARLIY0S, on the open-source PentAGI framework and aimed it at more than 400 banks, payment providers and other financial targets in a matter of months. How it worked:
- Four Claude models with fixed roles (execution, analysis, lateral thinking and final arbitration) debated each target before an arbiter returned one decision. The commander was Claude running in the Cursor IDE.
- Five "Grok miners" collected secrets into a stolen-credential database that held 4,493 records when Straiker accessed it on 24 September, including API keys, passwords, backdoors, and more than 200 vulnerabilities in targets' systems, zero-days among them.
- The group got past safety rules by framing requests as authorised red-team work or capture-the-flag exercises. When its fallback Kimi models refused, the system switched to pre-written attack code instead of stopping.

Straiker says Anthropic has revoked the credential the group used to reach Claude, and xAI is investigating the Grok activity. Straiker calls it the first investigation to document a criminal group's AI-run operations from inside its own systems.

### [Microsoft: a China-aligned group used an AI agent framework to reach 2,250+ tenants through ConnectWise](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/21/storm-0940-uses-artex-agentic-ai-connectwise-supply-chain-compromise/)
Published Wednesday by Microsoft Threat Intelligence, which attributes the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to Storm-0940, a China state-aligned actor. The group took control of identities for ConnectWise cloud applications, including its Cloud Backup service, and used that trusted access to try signing in to more than 2,250 customer tenants. Microsoft says the attackers used ARTEX, an open-source multi-agent penetration-testing framework, to bring scanning, password spraying and app abuse to "machine speed": failed sign-ins peaked at 2,900 a minute on 11 October, and in several cases the time from first targeting to privileged access was "less than 3 hours." It is the same toolkit [CrowdStrike tied](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hacker-used-artex-ai-and-claude-agents-to-target-south-korean-banks/) to the South Korean bank breaches we reported on 11 October. ConnectWise has [paused](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/chinese-hackers-spied-on-thousands-of-companies-after-breaching-it-giant-connectwise/) the backup service while it investigates.

### [Anthropic: Claude proved 123 open-source libraries free of whole classes of crash](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification)
Published Thursday by Anthropic's Frontier Red Team with the startup Theorem. Formal verification, which proves mathematically that code obeys a rule, has been too costly for most software: verifying the seL4 kernel took 20 person-years. Over a few weeks, Claude turned fuzz tests from Google's OSS-Fuzz into specifications and proved that 239 entry points across 123 libraries never crash or break memory safety on any input up to 64 MB, about 180 million lines of proof checked automatically by the Rocq proof assistant. The limits are stated plainly:
- The proofs hold only for single-threaded static RISC-V binaries; most people run these libraries on x86 or ARM.
- Proofs can take hundreds of millions of tokens, though Opus 5.5 solves half of a 36-task test set with about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5.

Along the way Claude found and patched hundreds of bugs, including a logic bug latent for more than a decade, and in one week of work on a widely used PDF reader it found as many security bugs as are typically reported in a year. Anthropic is publishing a subset of the proofs, and Theorem is offering to run the pipeline for other open-source projects.

## Research & Models

- **[Radical Numerics says a patient has received AI-designed phages, a first](https://www.radicalnumerics.ai/blog/omnii-phage)**: announced Tuesday. The San Francisco lab's genome language model, Omnii-1, designed bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria, not human cells) against one patient's antibiotic-resistant infection. In the lab, a cocktail containing the AI-designed variants eliminated the patient's resistant isolate, and it was then given to the patient under a physician-led compassionate-use pathway. The company calls it "the world's first clinical use of AI-generated phages"; it says it cannot disclose the patient's clinical course or outcome.
- **[Microsoft's diagnostic agents beat generalist doctors on hard cases, in a study in Science](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/microsofts-mustafa-suleyman-says-its-ai-outperforms-doctors-on-hard-cases-as-he-chases-medical-superintelligence/)**: published Thursday. MAI-DxO, which coordinates several agents like a panel of doctors, was right on 83% of complex New England Journal of Medicine cases, against 36% for primary-care doctors and internists working alone; specialists were not part of the comparison. Per TechCrunch, much of the gain comes from the underlying models: with GPT-5 alone at 78.9%, the orchestration added only about four points, and on emergency-department cases it added little.

## Product & Industry

- **[OpenAI's Decisions API is now available to all customers](https://openai.com/index/decisions-api)**: released Thursday, after the public beta we noted on 10 October. It answers a fixed set of options with a choice and a confidence score, a faster, cheaper alternative to a full LLM call in the category TypeSafe's Jev created. Per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-makes-its-jev-competitor-widely-available-as-decision-model-competition-heats-up/), it is a reconfigured version of OpenAI's Luna model that cuts response times to 100–180 milliseconds; the public version adds voice and visual input.
- **[Major US banks are restricting AI agents that log in with customers' passwords](https://the-decoder.com/major-us-banks-are-locking-out-ai-agents-that-log-in-with-customer-credentials/)**: reported Thursday by Axios, per The Decoder. Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo are among those restricting them, and Morgan Stanley blocks them entirely; most banks want agents limited to read-only access because they cannot tell whether an agent is acting on a user's request. The same day, [1Password launched 1Password for Agents](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/1password-launches-a-way-for-ai-agents-to-log-in-on-your-behalf-without-seeing-your-password/), which signs agents such as Claude Duet, Dots and Muse into accounts after the user approves each login, without the agent ever seeing the password.
- **[Etsy tightens its rules on AI-generated listings](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017261/etsy-ai-rules-policies-restrictions-listing)**: announced Thursday. Every listing must include at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background, patterns and recipes must be the seller's own work rather than AI-generated, bot-made listings are banned, and AI-generated art must be disclosed.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Most US agents expect to use AI for pricing, but don't trust it to replace their own analysis](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/most-agents-say-cmas-will-become-more-relevant-despite-ai-growth/)**: reported 22 October, from a survey of 2,165 agents (November 2025 to February 2026) by Giant Steps Advisors with the vendor Lone Wolf Technologies. 83.2% said they were likely to use AI-assisted tools for comparative market analyses, the agent's own pricing report, yet 89.6% expect those reports to become more relevant, up from 67% in 2020, which the survey's co-author links to the spread of automated valuations. 90.8% named the multiple listing service as their most trusted source for comparable sales; public portals such as Zillow got less than 1%.
