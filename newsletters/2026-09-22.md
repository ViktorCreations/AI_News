## Top Stories

### [OpenAI gives mathematicians a voice, and withholds the one thing they asked for](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/openai-forms-math-advisory-group-as-its-ai-resolves-more-than-100-open-problems/)
The Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, announced Monday and hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, is OpenAI's response to the open letter from 25 Fields Medallists we covered on the 12th, which argued that labs racing to one-up each other on famous problems were damaging mathematics as a practice. The group will assess the significance of new results and coordinate their release; members are unpaid, may give unsolicited advice, may go public with their views, and control their own membership — real independence on the axes that cost nothing. What it may not do is stated plainly in OpenAI's own post: "the group will not be responsible for advising us on how to pace our internal progress on mathematics." The IAS was equally clear in its own release: "Although we will give advice, we do not have decision making power at any AI company, and the responsibility for the decisions made by any company will rest with that company." Of the nine initial members, exactly one — the IAS's Camillo De Lellis — signed the Fields Medallists' letter. And buried in the same announcement is the number that will unsettle the field far more than the governance: the same internal model, OpenAI says, has now resolved **more than 100 additional open problems** across most areas of mathematics. The complaint was never that the results were wrong. It was that nobody has time to absorb them.

### [OpenAI proposes global standards for the thing it is worried about](https://openai.com/index/building-standards-next-phase-ai/)
Published Monday, and the framing matters: OpenAI is calling for shared international technical standards covering alignment and recursive self-improvement specifically — coordinated evaluation, incident reporting and governance, rather than each lab marking its own homework. Read across the last week this is no longer an isolated position but a convergence: Anthropic published measurements of how much of its own research Claude now leads and how much compute goes to safety; the UN's scientific panel argued yesterday that governments need not wait for certainty before constraining agents; Microsoft wrote its constraints into a code of conduct. The labs are now proposing, in public, the apparatus that would measure and limit them. The open question is the same one the embedded-evaluator debate keeps running into — whether standards authored by the regulated survive contact with a regulator, and who is funded to enforce them.

### [Meta's Muse is outrunning ChatGPT's mobile launch, on the numbers that flatter it](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-muse-is-outpacing-chatgpts-early-mobile-launch/)
Apptopia estimates Muse took 1.8 million iOS downloads across the US and Canada in its first 12 days against ChatGPT's 1.3 million over the same window, with 2.8 million total installs globally, and it has climbed from No. 2 to No. 1 on the US App Store — above ChatGPT. Daily active users tell the same story: 642,000 in the US against ChatGPT's 231,000 at the equivalent point, and 359,000 on iOS alone. Two caveats belong with it. These are third-party estimates, not company figures. And ChatGPT's mobile app arrived months after the web product had already made it a household name, so "outpacing ChatGPT's launch" measures a very different thing from outpacing ChatGPT. What it does measure is distribution: Meta can put an agent in front of billions of existing users, which is precisely why Amazon blocking it from shopping, as we covered yesterday, is the more consequential story of the two.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Texas halts every data-center permit in the state](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-tceq-to-halt-data-center-permits)** — Governor Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality on Monday to issue no permits sought by data-center projects until ERCOT completes its audit, and instructed that no state agency proceed with data-center approvals until that information exists. "Simply put, Texans must come first," he said. "Data centers must pay their own way, protect our grid and water, and complete the ERCOT and TWDB audits." The conditions attached are specific: projects must cover all electrical infrastructure costs, must *result in lower residential electricity bills*, must not consume water needed by local communities, must report electricity and water use, and must observe setback requirements. TCEQ reports back by October 19, and Abbott says he will work with the legislature next session to eliminate financial incentives for data centers altogether. This is a Republican governor in the largest data-center state in the country, two days after the president called opposition to data centers a hoax manufactured by the left, and it follows New York becoming the first state to halt large-project permits.
