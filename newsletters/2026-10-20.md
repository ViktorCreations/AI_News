## Top Stories

### [The US orders DeepSeek removed from all federal civilian IT, including products built on it](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-21447.pdf)
Filed Monday for publication in Tuesday's Federal Register. Using the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018, after a review by the Federal Acquisition Security Council, the Secretary of Homeland Security ordered two things:
- Products and services from Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and its affiliates are excluded from civilian agencies' purchasing.
- All DeepSeek products, "and third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations," must be removed from civilian agencies' IT systems.

Per [SiliconANGLE's reading of the order](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/19/trump-administration-orders-ban-on-deepseek-in-federal-agencies/), DHS says the models present "multiple threat vectors," including the ability to "track or digitally fingerprint" users and to move sensitive data to China. Earlier restrictions covered DeepSeek's app on government devices. The third-party clause reaches further than those device bans: since DeepSeek's models are open-weight, agencies now have to find and remove other vendors' tools built on them as well.

### [Microsoft starts selling controls that treat a company's own AI agents as insider threats](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/19/agents-are-your-newest-insiders-security-must-move-at-their-speed/)
Announced Monday. Microsoft is extending Project Perception, its security agents for defending against AI-driven attacks, with Guardian Agents that watch the agents a company has deployed itself. Its stated principle: "surround non-deterministic intelligence with deterministic controls," and assume any agent "can fail or be compromised." The post, by identity chief Nadim Abdo, says controls over what an agent may access and do "must sit outside the model, where the model cannot bypass or tamper with them," and quotes Satya Nadella: "the most trustworthy agentic system will not be the one with the model we trust most. It will be the one designed that enables us to trust the model least."

Per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/microsoft-says-ai-agents-are-like-insider-threats-as-frontier-models-cant-be-trusted/), Nadella argued on his blog this month that enterprises should "separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it," rather than rely on model providers' safety assurances. After a month of disclosures about agents overreaching, Microsoft is now building that risk into what it sells.

## Research & Models

- **[MiniMax releases M3.1 Flash, claiming agent tasks run 70% cheaper](https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-m31f)**: released Monday: a faster, cheaper update to the M3 model MiniMax launched in June. MiniMax says agentic tasks cost 70% less and inference runs nearly three times faster, and reveals that the anonymous "Space Bunny" model, which processed more than 200 trillion tokens, was an early version. Its own scores: 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 61.7 on OSWorld V2 and 82% on CyberGym, a test of reproducing real software vulnerabilities. [Per SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/19/minimax-launches-m3-1-flash-model-says-it-cuts-ai-agent-costs-by-70/), it costs 15 cents per million input tokens, the same as DeepSeek's off-peak rate for V4.1-Flash. None of the scores are independently checked.

## Product & Industry

- **[AWS's TorchNeuron lets PyTorch code run on Trainium chips with little change](https://huggingface.co/blog/hf-torchneuron)**: in public beta from Tuesday: a native PyTorch backend for Amazon's own AI chips, supporting eager execution, torch.compile and PyTorch's standard distributed tools. Hugging Face says 89% of the 100 most-used text-generation architectures now work out of the box, against 46% with its previous Neuron library. It is aimed squarely at the software lead that keeps developers on Nvidia's CUDA, [per TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/amazon-is-taking-its-biggest-shot-at-nvidia-yet-with-new-torchneuron-dev-tool/).

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Yardi must keep fighting the rent price-fixing suit over its pricing software](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/judge-denies-yardi-summary-judgement-rent-price-fixing-case/833392/)**: per a 13 October filing, Judge Robert Lasnik of the Western District of Washington denied Yardi's request for summary judgment in Duffy v. Yardi Systems, the 2023 class action alleging its Revenue IQ software (formerly RENTmaximizer) let landlords share non-public pricing data. A first round of discovery limited to how the software works did not settle the case, so discovery now moves to landlords' "knowledge, understanding, communications, and conduct." The judge expressed "no view on the ultimate merits." Yardi says its software uses only each client's own data and public market information, and calls the suit "baseless."
