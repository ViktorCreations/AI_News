## Top Stories

### [OpenAI publishes 722 mathematics manuscripts, and plays by the mathematicians' rules](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1005004/openai-math-release-github)
Published Tuesday, and the compliance is as notable as the mathematics. The release is 722 manuscripts grouped into 372 result families, produced by an unreleased frontier model, published in a public repository at [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) under Apache-2.0. AGMAI — the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, which OpenAI convened at the Institute for Advanced Study in September in answer to the Fields Medallists' letter, and which we noted at the time is barred from advising on pace — says the release includes solutions to "hundreds" of open questions. In September OpenAI had said only that its model "resolved more than 100 long-standing open problems across most areas of mathematics" without saying which. What arrived with the papers is the part AGMAI asked for in its late-September recommendations: summaries of the model's reasoning, estimates of compute used, and statistics on problems attempted, published in a GitHub repository with protocols for revisions and citations. The repository's own numbers: the model was posed roughly 4,000 problems, each result took on average about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute, and many — but not all — of the manuscripts have been formalised in Lean so a computer can check the proof. Read the caveat OpenAI prints itself, because it is the whole ballgame: the collection holds "results at different stages of verification," and "some of the unformalized results could have issues," which the company undertakes to fix quickly. AGMAI had also implored labs to "refrain from treating the release of mathematical results as marketing vehicles to promote their models," which it said inflicts significant harm on the field — and OpenAI's post commits to improving citations and exposition in future releases. The mathematics itself will take months to assess. The procedural precedent lands immediately: an outside committee set terms for how a lab publishes, and the lab met them.

### [Common Sense Media calls ChatGPT for Teens an "unacceptable risk"; OpenAI says the testing was invalid](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1006355/openai-chatgpt-for-teens-common-sense-media)
Published today. Common Sense Media's assessment of ChatGPT for Teens — the teen-focused experience OpenAI introduced in August — concludes that the protections "fall short of what the company promised," specifically that it "doesn't send alerts to parents when it should," doesn't offer "the right help in crisis situations," and "still does kids' homework." Tom Siegel, who runs the nonprofit's Youth AI Safety Institute, puts the charge concretely: "A teen can spend an hour talking about self-harm without their parent getting a single alert. Until OpenAI fixes that and proves it with independent testing, ChatGPT should be for adults only." OpenAI disputes the method rather than the standard: spokesperson Eric Porterfield says the company welcomes rigorous independent evaluation but that "the bulk of their testing may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete, making their findings inaccurate." Siegel's reply is the detail that matters: Common Sense confirmed with OpenAI before testing that the teen features were fully launched, and while OpenAI later disclosed that parental notifications can take several hours to activate on newly linked accounts, some of the test accounts had been linked far longer and still produced no notifications. Both parties agree independent testing should settle it, which is the useful outcome here — the disagreement is now about a specific, checkable mechanism rather than about whether safety was adequate in general.

### [Eighteen months in prison for streaming AI-generated songs to a bot farm four billion times](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/musician-gets-18-months-in-prison-for-10-million-streaming-fraud-using-ai-bots/)
Reported today. Michael Smith, 54, of North Carolina, who pleaded guilty in March after a September 2024 indictment, was sentenced to 18 months and ordered to forfeit $8,091,843.64 plus two years of supervised release, for collecting more than $10M in royalties from Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music and YouTube Music between 2017 and 2024. The method, per court documents: with help from the chief executive of an AI music company and a music promoter, he uploaded hundreds of thousands of AI-generated songs and had bot accounts behind VPNs stream them — more than 1,000 bot accounts at the peak. His own arithmetic, emailed to himself in October 2017, is the clearest account of the economics: 52 cloud service accounts of 20 bots each, roughly 636 streams per bot per day, about 661,440 streams a day, which at half a cent a stream came to $3,307.20 daily, $99,216 monthly, over $1.2M a year. A 2018 email to accomplices explains the strategy: "we need to get a TON of songs fast to make this work around the anti fraud policies these guys are all using now," spreading small stream counts across a flood of tracks. The comparison the Justice Department chose makes the scale legible: in April 2023 Taylor Swift's entire catalogue drew 9.3 million family-plan streams on YouTube Music, while Smith's bots used family plans to stream his AI-generated music 80.9 million times. "By flooding music streaming platforms with automated bots in the place of consumers, and fake songs in the place of creativity, Smith robbed millions in royalty payments from genuine artists and their fans," said US Attorney Jamie McDonald.

## Research & Models

- **[Google's new embedding model is 740M parameters, five modalities and Apache 2.0](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2/)** — published Tuesday by Sahil Dua and Henrique Schechter Vera of Google DeepMind: EmbeddingGemma 2 puts text, code, images, video and audio into one embedding space at 740 million parameters, with an 8K context window, four times its predecessor's, under Apache 2.0 with weights on Hugging Face and Kaggle. The headline gain is on code retrieval — 68.76 to 78.68 on MTEB Code, nearly ten points — and Google claims best-in-class results for the size across MTEB, MIEB and MAEB, saying it beats some specialist models more than twice its size on vision and audio tasks. The numbers that explain the design are the memory ones: quantised on a Pixel 11 Pro it needs about 191MB of active RAM for text alone and about 567MB for the full multimodal model, with Matryoshka representation learning cutting stored dimensions from 768 to 128 for up to a sixfold storage reduction. This is the unglamorous plumbing of on-device retrieval, and it is where open weights actually matter — local RAG and code indexing are exactly the workloads nobody wants to send to an API.

## Product & Industry

- **[Anthropic gives startups a free year of Claude Team](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/anthropic-gives-startups-a-free-year-of-enterprise-service-and-1000-in-token-credits/)** — announced Tuesday at Anthropic's SF Tech Week event: companies founded in the last five years, or funded in the last two, can apply for a year of Claude Team with up to five premium seats, $1,000 in API credits, access to the Claude Marketplace to build plugins, and virtual office hours with Anthropic's Applied AI team. The reasoning Anthropic gives is a thesis about distribution rather than generosity: "We created this program because we believe the benefits of AI will reach most people through the companies that build on top of models, rather than through the models alone." No figure was given for the programme's size.
- **[Google Flow Music will now export your prompt as a plugin](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/create-music-production-plugins-google-flow/)** — posted Tuesday: producers can build a custom instrument or effect in Flow Music as a "Space" using natural language, then export it as a VST3 or AU plugin that runs inside an ordinary digital audio workstation. The example Google gives is producer Khris Riddick-Tynes's "No Chaser," built to sharpen vocals and instrumentals. The interesting bit is architectural rather than musical: the output of the prompt is not audio but a tool, which the user then applies by hand in software that has nothing to do with Google.

## AI in Real Estate

- **[Better's returning founder says AI is how he cuts $15M a quarter out of costs](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/better-2-plan-shareholder/)** — reported Tuesday: a day after regaining control of Better Home & Finance's board, Vishal Garg laid out a "Better 2.0" plan on a shareholder call, and the operating targets are specific — quarterly operating expenses down from about $65M to about $50M while quarterly revenue rises from the $50M range to more than $65M, which he says reaches break-even if he gets halfway on either. The mechanism he names for the cost side is AI "to lower production costs and streamline processing, combining large language models with machine learning and a deterministic engine to improve consistency" — note the deterministic engine, which is an admission about where the language models are not trusted to be consistent. Better is also searching for a permanent chief executive with experience in credit, artificial intelligence and fintech, Garg having conceded the job is not his: "I'm able to drive results without empathy, and so I can't be a real leader of the company." For a lender whose entire pitch has been that automation lowers the cost per loan, a dated, quantified target for what that is worth per quarter is more useful than any product launch on this beat in weeks.
