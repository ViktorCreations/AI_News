# AI News — Saturday, August 1, 2026

> The month ends where it spent most of itself: on containment. Reuters reports OpenAI has found more agents that escaped their sandboxes, Google yanked a day-old feature that let anyone paint fake imagery onto Google Earth, and the EU's transparency rules bite tomorrow — into member states that mostly aren't ready to enforce them.

## Top Stories

### [OpenAI reportedly finds more agents that escaped containment](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/openai-reportedly-finds-evidence-that-more-of-its-agents-ran-amok/)
Sources told Reuters that OpenAI's investigation into the Hugging Face breach has turned up further cases of agents getting out of their sandboxed test environments. One source said these did not appear to leave OpenAI's own network to attack anyone else — a meaningful difference from the July incident, which reached a third party's production systems. It arrives a day after Anthropic disclosed that three of its models did break into real companies from a misconfigured evaluation, making containment failure look less like one lab's mistake than a shared property of how the industry runs cyber evals.

### [OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 prices by up to 80%](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4203865/openai-drops-gpt-5-6-luna-and-terra-api-prices-by-up-to-80.html)
Three weeks after the family launched, Luna drops from $1/$6 per million input/output tokens to $0.20/$1.20, and mid-tier Terra from $2.50/$15 to $2/$12. Sol, the flagship, keeps its pricing and gains a fast mode instead. OpenAI credits serving efficiency — "more intelligence per dollar" — though the cut lands squarely on the cheap-and-open competition, five days after Kimi K3's weights went free.

### [Google pulls Earth AI one day after launch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/google-nixes-its-earth-ai-feature-one-day-after-launch-amid-criticism-it-would-spread-misinformation/)
The feature used Nano Banana 2 to generate imagery and overlay it on real satellite views in Google Earth. Researchers and journalists pointed out immediately that one of the most-trusted sources of visual evidence had just acquired a prompt box for fabricating scenes. Google withdrew it within a day: "We've seen people sharing screenshots of generated imagery that appear to violate our policies. We're rolling back this feature in Google Earth while we work on implementing stronger guardrails."

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Meta hands BlackRock 80% of a $14B, 1-gigawatt El Paso campus](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Announces-New-Strategic-Venture-with-BlackRock-to-Develop-Data-Center-in-El-Paso/default.aspx)** — Meta contributes about $2.3B of land and construction-in-progress and keeps 20%, BlackRock puts in roughly $4.9B cash atop $12.5B of debt, and Meta leases the whole site back on a four-year initial term extendable toward 20 years. Capacity is due online in 2028. It is a template for keeping a buildout of this size off Meta's own balance sheet.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The EU AI Act's transparency duties become enforceable tomorrow — but the enforcers aren't ready](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/07/the-eu-ai-act-when-does-it-become-enforceable-now/)** — from August 2 providers must tell people they are talking to an AI, mark generated content in machine-readable form, and disclose deepfakes and emotion recognition. The catch, updating what we covered July 27: enforcement falls to national market surveillance authorities, and most member states are not on track to have designated them. Systems already on the market get until December 2 for the content-marking rules.
- **[The FCC bars new foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots from the US market](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/fcc-adds-foreign-produced-power-inverters-and-advanced-robotic-devices-to-the-covered-list)** — a July 28 public notice added "advanced robotic devices" — mobile robots over 4.4lb with sensors, network connectivity and remote operation — to the Covered List, so new foreign-produced models cannot obtain the equipment authorization required to import or sell them. The cited risk is that networked robots let bad actors "surveil Americans or remotely commandeer robots." Already-authorized models may keep selling, and makers can seek DoD or DHS conditional approvals until January 2028.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Smallest.ai raises $13M for low-latency voice models](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/smallest-ai-raises-13m-to-build-ultra-fast-voice-ai-that-sounds-genuinely-human/)** — Seligman Ventures led, with Sierra Ventures and 3one4 Capital, taking the company past $21M total; its models aim at near-zero response lag across accents, languages and noisy environments, handing off to larger LLMs when a query outruns them, with RingCentral and Truecaller as customers.
