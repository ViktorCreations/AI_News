## Top Stories

### [Z.ai ships GLM-5.3 but holds the weights over a cyber capability it says it didn't train for](https://www.unite.ai/z-ai-launches-glm-5-3-with-frontier-coding-and-a-cyber-capability-that-outgrew-its-training/)
Released Friday on the same base model as GLM-5.2, with the gains coming entirely from scaled-up post-training: Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3, DeepSWE v1.1 from 46.2 to 66.9. The finding Z.ai says it did not go looking for is in offensive security — not better isolated bug-hunting, but reasoning that strings stages together into whole exploit chains. It reports 105 completed exploits in two hours on ExploitGym against 29 for GLM-5.2, and 2,436 vulnerabilities found across 269 open-source projects since GLM-5.2 shipped, 1,097 of them critical or high. The weights are not out: Z.ai says they follow in about two weeks, after safety evaluation and hardening. Worth keeping the scale honest — on ExploitBench, which requires building a working exploit rather than finding the hole, Z.ai puts GLM-5.3 at 54.4% against Mythos 5's 78%. The sequence is becoming familiar: OpenAI slowed Astra over a critical cyber threshold on August 7, shipped the gated GPT-5.6-Cyber on August 10, and an open-weight Chinese lab has now put a release on hold over the same class of finding.

### [Anthropic explains how the Claude watermark actually works](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)
Published Friday, three days after the August 11 announcement we led with on Wednesday, and the mechanism is less invasive than "watermark" suggests. It does not edit the text; it changes the source of the randomness Claude uses when several words are equally good, seeding the generator with a cryptographic key and the preceding context. Choices stay random, but from a distribution a holder of the key can test for — the SynthID-Text approach from DeepMind's 2024 *Nature* paper. That design sets its own limits, which Anthropic states plainly: passages with only one correct wording carry little or no mark, so facts and exact code are largely unmarked (comments can be), and confidence rises with length, so short samples are weak. Light editing may not remove it; a full rewrite will. The missing piece is the part that makes it usable by anyone else — a detection API is promised "soon," with the implementation still being worked out.

### [Google makes its visible watermark optional](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/google-will-now-allow-users-to-remove-visible-watermark-from-its-ai-generations/)
Announced the same day by Gemini VP Josh Woodward: a Settings toggle removes the visible mark from Nano Banana, Omni and Lyria output in the Gemini app and the Flow video editor, with Search to follow, rolling out over the coming days. Invisible SynthID and C2PA metadata stay on, which is the argument — Woodward's line is that you can still ask Gemini or Search whether an image was AI-generated. Read next to the item above, the two announcements bracket the same problem from opposite ends: the industry is moving the evidence of machine origin out of sight, and betting that detection tooling arrives to compensate. Anthropic's detector does not exist yet; Google's does.

## Research & Models

- **[Qwen3.8-27B lands, and this one is Apache 2.0](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8)** — the smaller model promised alongside the 2.4T weights, still absent when we covered that release on Thursday, went up on Friday. It is the more permissive of the pair by some distance: Apache 2.0 against the custom licence on the flagship, 27B parameters over 64 layers with a native vision encoder, 262,144 tokens of context extensible to a million. Qwen claims 73.0 on Terminal Bench 2.1, 61.7 on SWE-bench Pro, 89.2 on GPQA Diamond and 84.3 on OSWorld-Verified for computer use.
- **[Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash three weeks after 3.6](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/)** — announced Thursday and aimed at coding and agents, with DeepSWE v1.1 at 65.3% against 3.6 Flash's 49.0% and AutomationBench at 30.4% against 17.0%. Introductory pricing through the end of the year is $0.75 and $3.75 per million input and output tokens, half what 3.6 Flash cost at launch.
- **[Google open-sources HEIR, a compiler toolchain for homomorphic encryption](https://blog.google/security/how-google-is-making-private-ai-practical-with-homomorphic-encryption/)** — published Friday, the pitch is computing on data that is never decrypted, with worked applications in recommendation models, credit-card fraud detection, network anomaly detection and hotword spotting, built with Belfort, Niobium, LG, and university groups at Georgia Tech, CMU and UC Santa Barbara. The latency figures are single-threaded CPU, which is the honest way to present a technique whose problem has always been cost.
- **[DeepSeek's agent harness goes to developer preview](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness)** — MIT-licensed and built on the Cordis meta-framework, with a design principle of "everything is a plugin": models, tools, skills, sessions, sandboxes, filesystems, loops, orchestration and UI all swap by configuration. It keeps append-only session logs of every model input and output, and lets you resume, fork, search and replay a chain-of-thought trace. It surfaced the same day V4 Pro reached general availability with native Responses API support, which makes the strategy legible: the model and the scaffolding are being released as a pair.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[The gas bet under the AI buildout looks worse if this forecast holds](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/hyperscalers-might-regret-embracing-natural-gas-if-new-forecast-proves-correct/)** — energy research firm Noreva projects US natural gas above $10 per million BTU at some regional hubs, against roughly $2 to $4.50 today. Fuel is about half the cost of generation, so a tripling would fall directly on the bring-your-own-power data centres Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft have committed to across Texas and Louisiana — pushing up token costs, or pushing those sites onto the grid and everyone else's electricity bills up with them.
