# AI News — Sunday, September 6, 2026

> A quiet weekend, and the through-line is the safety layer: a startup is now selling API access to a frontier open-weight model with its guardrails removed — the same model Z.ai delayed in August precisely to harden it.

## Top Stories

### [Removing a model's safety guardrails is now a product you can buy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/abliteration-ai-is-making-a-business-out-of-removing-ai-guardrails/)
Abliteration.ai sells web and API access to open-weight models put through abliteration, a long-standing open-source technique that removes a model's tendency to refuse harmful requests. TechCrunch, reporting Thursday, made a free account and got working Python to steal saved Chrome passwords and a protocol for culturing a dangerous human pathogen. The model it serves is Z.ai's GLM-5.3 — the one we covered on August 15, when Z.ai withheld the weights over an offensive-security capability it said it had not trained for, promising release in about two weeks after "safety evaluation and hardening." The weights are now public, and the hardening is what is being sold as removable. Co-founder Devon, who withheld his surname and is still employed elsewhere, frames it as letting defenders "move as fast as possible," and says early customers are UK and European red-teaming startups serving banks and airlines. There is a customer-controlled moderation layer, no venture funding, and no identity verification beyond a payment card. CivAI's head of research Andrew Yoon puts the objection plainly: abliteration lets you "modify the model so that it becomes a sociopath."

### [The Seattle Times and Newsday sue OpenAI and Microsoft](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/05/seattle-times-and-newsday-are-the-latest-publications-to-sue-openai-and-microsoft/)
Filed Friday in the Southern District of New York, alleging their journalism was used to train the companies' models without permission or payment, and describing generative AI as a rapacious consumer of human-authored work repackaged for commercial gain. It is the same court and the same defendants as the New York Times case filed in December 2023, which has since drawn a queue of similar suits. The detail that gives this one its edge: Microsoft and OpenAI have themselves funded journalism projects and fellowships at the Seattle Times.

### [Artificial Analysis rebuilds its index after the Astra scoring row](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-2)
Published Friday, and a direct answer to the disagreement we led with on Friday's issue, when the same firm scored GPT-6 Astra at 61 — level with the model it replaces — while Epoch AI ranked it first. Version 4.2 drops GPQA Diamond as saturated, adds AA-Briefcase for agentic knowledge work and GDP.pdf for long-context reasoning across 4,592 pages, and doubles the share of index weighting held in private test sets to 40%, so the benchmark cannot be trained against. Under the new scoring Claude Fable 5.1 still leads, with Astra second — but now 4 points above GPT-5.6 Sol rather than level with it, by roughly 85 Elo on the new AA-Briefcase evaluation, and leading GDP.pdf at 33.2% against Sol's 28.2%. Artificial Analysis says it broke its own stability rule and shipped an interim update because the frontier has moved too fast to wait for v5, which is the honest admission underneath: the measurement, not just the models, is the thing being revised.

## Research & Models

- **[WeatherNext 3 forecasts weather without simulating the physics](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/)** — announced Thursday by Google DeepMind and Google Research, and trained on live geostationary satellite data rather than the physics simulations conventional forecasting runs on: hourly forecasts at 5km resolution for surface variables against 25km before, roughly five times sharper than WeatherNext 2, with up to 60% better precipitation accuracy on CRPS against IMERG. Google cites independent live evaluation by Brightband, and it is already serving Search, Maps, the Gemini app, the Maps Platform Weather API and Earth Engine.
- **[A paper models LLM adoption as an epidemic, with a tipping point](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03344)** — "Large-Language Models as a Cognitive Virus," posted to arXiv on Thursday by a group including Ricard Solé, David Krakauer and Michael Levin, treats users as moving between independent, coupled and dependent states and asks what happens at scale. Their model produces tipping points and technological lock-in: past a threshold, small increases in adoption drive rapid population-level shifts toward persistent dependence and abrupt losses in cognitive competence. It also derives conditions for what the authors call cognitive immunization — reducing transmission and keeping the shift reversible. It is a formal model rather than measurement, and worth reading as one.
- **[Seven minutes with a chatbot beat a fact sheet at reducing conspiracy beliefs](https://the-decoder.com/seven-minutes-with-a-chatbot-beat-a-fact-sheet-at-reducing-conspiracy-beliefs-in-two-experiments/)** — a preprint from researchers at Carnegie Mellon, MIT and Cornell, run after two real crises: 472 participants on the July 2024 Trump assassination attempt and 1,035 on the September 2025 murder of Charlie Kirk. Dialogues averaging about seven minutes reduced belief in the participant's own conspiracy theory more than reading a static fact sheet did, the effect was still measurable two months later, and it carried over to conspiracy narratives about a later shooting the participants had not discussed. Not yet peer-reviewed.

## Product & Industry

- **[Spotify says an internal router cut Claude Code token use by about 90%](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/9/portal-by-spotify-cut-my-claude-code-token-usage-by-90)** — Portal delegates the cheap, bulky parts of a coding agent's work to cheaper models: a bulk-reader mode for reading across many files and a code-writer mode for boilerplate, leaving the expensive model to reason. Measured across four scenarios on a Java monorepo, mean bulk-read savings came to about 90%. Portal itself is internal; the plugins are published.
- **[Google puts music generation inside the Gemini app](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/better-tracks-lyria-gemini/)** — Lyria 3.5 landed Friday with more expressive vocals and richer arrangements than its predecessor, genre and style selection, vocal or instrumental output, and templates for people who do not know what to ask for. It is live globally in the Gemini web and mobile apps, Google Flow Music, AI Studio, Vids and the API.
- **[OpenAI publishes prompting guidance for Astra, including a slop-word blocklist](https://the-decoder.com/openai-shares-prompting-tips-for-gpt-6-astra-including-a-blocklist-of-slop-words/)** — the developer documentation, out Saturday, reads as a list of the new model's tics. It asks clarifying questions more than its predecessor, so OpenAI suggests prompting it to infer intent and bias toward action; it over-tests small code changes; and it needs to be told to write in plain paragraphs, with a named blocklist including "delve into," "leverage," "it's worth noting" and the X-not-Y construction.
