# AI News — Tuesday, August 4, 2026

> Alibaba shipped the 2.4-trillion-parameter model it previewed a fortnight ago, and Palantir's Alex Karp used a record quarter to accuse the AI labs of "capturing the means of production" from the enterprises they sell to.

## Top Stories

### [Alibaba releases Qwen3.8-Max, two weeks after previewing it](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/03/alibaba-debuts-qwen3-8-max-model-2-4t-parameters/)
The preview we covered on July 20 became a product on August 3: 2.4 trillion total parameters with about 95 billion active per query, a 1-million-token context window that Alibaba says covers 200+ pages or roughly 100 hours of footage, and outputs up to 131,000 tokens. It scored 1,668 on Frontend Code Arena, 37 points behind Claude Opus 5's top configuration and ahead of Meta's Muse Spark 1.1, and Alibaba cites a 16-day autonomous coding project and a 500-step chip-design optimisation as evidence of long-horizon competence. It is on Alibaba's cloud now, with open weights and a smaller Qwen3.8-27B promised next week — the part worth watching, given how Kimi K3's weights landed.

### [Palantir's Karp calls the AI industry "Marxist" after a record quarter](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/after-killer-quarter-palantir-ceo-alex-karp-calls-ai-industry-marxist/)
In Monday's shareholder letter and earnings call — alongside $1.9B in revenue, up 93%, and $1.1B in profit — Karp wrote that "there are Marxist overtones and undertones to our business," accusing model labs of trying to "capture the means of production of their purported partners" by absorbing customers' intellectual property and expertise, then competing with them. Palantir's counter-pitch is model-agnostic software that leaves data and outputs with the customer. It is the same argument Satya Nadella made last week about keeping your harness separate from the model — now coming from the company with the quarter to back it.

## Product & Industry

- **[AWS puts vibe coding inside enterprise private clouds via Superblocks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/aws-is-helping-vibe-coding-startup-superblocks-and-the-implications-are-big/)** — under a multiyear joint marketing deal, business users at AWS customers can build apps that never send data to outside model providers: they spin up Aurora databases inside the company's own cloud and run inference through Bedrock, landing under IT's management rather than as shadow IT. The distribution boost is large for a roughly 50-person startup, and the architecture is the point — governance is what has kept vibe coding out of the enterprise.
- **[Apple's Siri finally works, and that may be the problem](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/apple-finally-fixed-siri-so-why-does-it-feel-anticlimactic/)** — the rebuilt assistant in the iOS 27 public beta handles natural conversation, personal context and retrieval (it can find a driver's licence number saved only in a photo), running on Apple's own models trained with Google's Gemini technology, with general release due in September. The assessment is that being a competent assistant is no longer the bar it was when Apple started: agents now complete multistep tasks, and Siri arrives fixed but no longer remarkable.
- **[ChatGPT took about 90% of the House's identifiable AI spending](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/congresss-favorite-ai-tool-chatgpt/)** — House disbursement records for the year to March 31, reported by CNBC, show $100,580 across 798 transactions on ChatGPT against $13,160 on Claude, out of at least $113,740 total; Democratic offices spent $54,165 to Republicans' $15,782. Staff use it to summarise legislation, draft memos and answer constituents — which is to say the body writing AI rules is itself a customer. The count excludes free accounts and AI bundled into other contracts, so real adoption is higher.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Design Arena raises $7.9M to sell human taste to the labs](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/designarena-creators-raise-7-9-million-to-bring-taste-to-ai-models/)** — Index Ventures led the seed, with Conviction, A* and Valkyrie, for a platform that shows people pairs of AI-generated outputs and asks which is better, then sells that preference data to frontier labs building media models. Operated by a company called Intelligence, it claims 5.3 million users and $60M in annual recurring revenue — the thesis being that subjective quality is the one thing you cannot automate your way out of evaluating.
