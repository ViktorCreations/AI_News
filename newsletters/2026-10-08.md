# AI News — Thursday, October 8, 2026

> Two days after OpenAI started watermarking text for the EU and kept the detector private, Google has done the opposite with images, video and audio: SynthID Detector is now open to anyone, worldwide, and it checks content from OpenAI and Nvidia as well as Google. Mistral, meanwhile, previewed a trillion-parameter model and said it will give cyber authorities a version with the guardrails turned down — and researchers found 3,400 exposed AI servers quietly mining cryptocurrency, their command address encoded in a poem.

## Top Stories

### [Google opens its AI-content detector to everyone, and it checks OpenAI's output too](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/synth-id-ai-content/)
Published Wednesday by Pushmeet Kohli, VP of science and strategic initiatives at Google DeepMind. SynthID Detector — which tells you whether an image, video or audio file carries an AI watermark — is now available to anyone at synthid.com, globally, in English, having previously been an early-access tool for media professionals. The scale claims are the striking part: Google says it has watermarked more than 180 billion images and videos and 240,000 years of audio, and that SynthID verification is now built into Search, the Gemini app and Chrome, together handling over a million requests a day. It also covers content from partners — OpenAI, Nvidia and Kakao, with Apple listed as coming soon. Set that beside OpenAI's text watermarking from Monday, where detector access goes only to approved researchers because the mark washes out under paraphrase: the difference is not corporate attitude but modality. Watermarks survive in pixels and waveforms, which have redundancy to spare; they struggle in prose, which does not. One piece of context from our own archive: on 15 August we noted Google had added a toggle letting users strip the *visible* mark from Gemini and Flow output while the invisible SynthID stayed. This is the other half of that bargain — the mark you cannot see is now one anybody can check. Google's post names no limitations, which is the one thing missing from it.

### [Mistral previews a trillion-parameter model, and will hand authorities a version with the guardrails down](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/)
Announced on 6 October. Mistral Large 4 — the company calls it "le Chonk" — is a hybrid instruct-and-reasoning mixture-of-experts model with about a trillion total parameters and 52 billion active, natively multimodal, trained from scratch on 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own European data centres, on data spanning more than 160 languages including every official EU language. The company's own benchmark claims lead on security: 93% of Cybench challenges solved, plus 61.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 59.9% on AutomationBench across 657 business workflows, and 42% on Dense 200 visual grounding against 41% for GPT-6 Astra. A preview API is live on Mistral Studio at $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output; the weights are promised by the end of the month, so this is not yet an open-weight release however it is billed. The provision worth flagging is the access policy: before the weights go out, Mistral says cybersecurity leaders, vetted partners and state authorities will get the model with "reduced moderation and expanded cyber capabilities." A lab deliberately distributing a de-restricted frontier model to governments is a new kind of arrangement, and it arrives five days after Microsoft's own threat report concluded attackers are currently getting more out of AI than defenders.

### [Meta is now judging ads by where they send you, to catch the ones routing to child abuse material](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/meta-rolls-out-new-ai-tools-to-detect-ads-that-secretly-lead-to-child-sexual-abuse-material/)
Reported Wednesday. Meta says it acted against 33.2 million pieces of child sexual exploitation content on Facebook and Instagram in the first half of 2026, more than 97% of it found by its own systems before anyone reported it, with 5.3 million of those actions in India at a detection rate above 98%. The new tooling targets a specific evasion it calls signposting: an advert that looks entirely ordinary and whose purpose is to point people at illegal material hosted elsewhere. A large language model system now evaluates where an ad sends users rather than only what the ad contains, which lets Meta block the destination and act on the account behind it; further AI scans look for exploitation content earlier systems missed, a "red-teaming AI agent" probes Meta's own safeguards for exploitable gaps, and detection of banned users returning under new accounts has been improved. No figures were given for ads removed under the new system, which is the number that will matter. Context for the timing: in August Meta agreed to pay up to $18 billion to settle a suit brought by 29 US states over social-media harms to children.

### [3,400 exposed AI servers were mined for cryptocurrency, with the command address hidden in a poem](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/poellm-malware-infects-exposed-ai-servers-in-cryptomining-attacks/)
Reported Wednesday, from Black Lotus Labs research. PoeLLM has compromised more than 3,400 servers since at least April, with as many as 800 infected systems active on a single day, across the United States and Western Europe. The targets are the giveaway: exposed AI tooling — LiteLLM and Ollama — alongside the Gotenberg PDF converter and Gitea, chosen because such deployments are often misconfigured, reachable from the internet and sitting on GPU clusters that happen to be excellent for mining. The command-and-control mechanism is the detail you will remember: the malware, an ELF file posing as libgcrypt, pulls four words from a poem called "On the Nature of Connection," hosted in a `dash.css` file in a GitHub repository that appears to fork Node.js, then maps those words to numbers through a hard-coded dictionary to produce the C2's IP address. To move the C2, the operator edits the poem — done eleven times so far. Infected machines run XMRig and Iron miners against the Russian service Kryptex and then scan ports 3000 and 4000 to spread, exploiting CVE-2026-42271 in LiteLLM's MCP server test endpoints, which Horizon.ai researchers confirmed can be chained with CVE-2026-48710 for unauthenticated remote code execution. Black Lotus Labs assesses with moderate confidence that the operator is Italian, from comments in the malware and an Italy-hosted admin interface. The lesson is mundane and expensive: the GPU box you exposed to debug your agent is worth more to a miner than to you.

## Product & Industry

- **[ChatGPT answers are about to be full of pictures](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/chatgpt-is-getting-a-lot-more-visual-with-the-launch-of-a-new-interface/)** — announced Wednesday, and confirmed on OpenAI's own feed as "GPT-6 and Intelligent UI for everyone": the new interface fills responses with visuals, many of them interactive — tappable buttons, task-specific calculators, editable charts and graphs, with OpenAI's examples running from a diagram of how a wing generates lift to a multi-day hiking map and a savings calculator. It runs on GPT-6, reached Pro, Plus, Business and Enterprise users globally at launch and free and Go users the following day, and users who want less of it can dial the visuals back. Product manager Aarush Selvan's framing is that ChatGPT "has predominantly been a text-based interface" and that the most helpful answers "aren't just text." Worth holding next to Monday's advertising announcement: the surface that is about to carry product images is also the surface that just became visual.
- **[Microsoft gives Copilot your local files and the run of Windows](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1007113/microsoft-windows-copilot-ai-control-search-hybrid-intelligence)** — shown Wednesday at Microsoft's Windows and Surface event: under a banner it calls "Hybrid Intelligence," mixing local and cloud models, Copilot gains access to files on the PC and the ability to take actions across the operating system. The demo by Jacob Andreou, Microsoft's EVP of Copilot, is the clearest statement of intent — asked to send an accountant what she needed, the Autopilot agent searched folders, renamed files, zipped them and drafted the email with the archive attached. It arrives over the "next couple of months," with a separate Windows search bar that performs quick actions arriving this autumn. Read it against Apple, which announced on 2 October that it is tightening macOS Full Disk Access precisely because agent autonomy makes that level of access riskier: the two platforms have now moved in opposite directions in the same week.

## Capital & Deals

- **[Nous Research confirms $90M at a $1.5B valuation and goes after enterprises](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/nous-research-confirms-it-hit-1-5b-valuation-launches-ai-agents-for-business-users/)** — confirmed Wednesday: a Series B led by Robot Ventures with Nvidia, Union Square Ventures, Menlo Ventures, Samsung and 1789 Capital, taking the three-year-old company to $158M raised. It also launched Hermes for Businesses, letting companies deploy customised agents on multi-step workflows while keeping data private. The numbers behind the valuation are unusual in shape: by the company's own estimate its open-source Hermes Agent has been cloned more than 24 million times and accounts for roughly 2.5% of global AI token usage, and the Wall Street Journal puts annualised revenue at about $36M in mid-September against an expectation of passing $100M by year end. An open-source agent with measurable share of the world's token traffic is a different kind of moat from a model lead.
- **[Sequoia puts $60M into paying people to wear sensors so robots can learn](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/robot-data-startup-mecka-ai-nabs-60m-from-sequoia/)** — announced Wednesday: Mecka AI, founded in 2024, raised a Series B led by Sequoia with Nvidia and Microsoft's M12 among others; TechCrunch's earlier reporting had the company nearing a $500M valuation, which this round does not confirm. The business is deliberately unglamorous — it pays people to record themselves doing ordinary tasks, making coffee or fixing cars, wearing body sensors and using phones, then sells the human-motion data to train humanoid and other robots. The explicit analogy is to what Scale AI, Mercor and Surge did for language models, which is also the warning: that layer of the stack turned out to be both essential and fiercely contested.
