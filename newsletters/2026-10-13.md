# AI News — Tuesday, October 13, 2026

> Meta shipped its first frontier models, and Character.AI, one of the first breakout chatbot companies, filed for bankruptcy with Disney lined up to buy part of what is left. The money mood turned too: the IMF named an AI share crash as one of three big risks to global financial stability, and chip stocks across Asia fell sharply.

## Top Stories

### [Meta releases Muse Wonder, its first frontier models](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/introducing-muse-wonder-models/)
Announced Monday, fourteen months into the overhaul of Meta's AI effort: Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which Meta calls its "most capable models to date" and which it built for personal and professional agent tasks. The pitch centres on two properties Meta has named:
- **Active Memory:** carrying what matters to a user across conversations.
- **Active Focus:** persisting when a task gets hard, recovering from errors and checking in before doing anything important.

Per [TechCrunch's reading of the model card](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/meta-releases-its-first-frontier-model-muse-wonder/), Meta's own tests put both models level with Anthropic's Fable 5.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra on benchmarks, and ahead of them on MemoryBench, a memory test Meta highlighted. Muse Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output through the Meta Model API. Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers and is not offered through the API. TechCrunch also reports both were trained at Meta's Prometheus data centre in Ohio.

One gap to note: Meta says the models are trained, evaluated and released under its Superintelligence Scaling Framework, but "a full report with additional safety evaluations will be available soon." The models are on sale now and the safety report is not yet out, in the same week other labs have been disclosing agents misbehaving on the open web.

### [Character.AI files for bankruptcy, with Disney lined up to buy some of its assets](https://blog.character.ai/an-update-on-character-ai/)
Announced Monday: Character.AI has started a voluntary Chapter 11 process to run a court-supervised sale of all or substantially all of its assets. It cites "significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds" and expects the sale to finish by late November or early December. The service and paid subscriptions continue for now, but the company says plainly that if no suitable bid emerges, "the platform will be wound down."

Per [The Verge's reading of the filing](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009802/character-ai-files-for-bankruptcy-disney):
- Character.AI lists estimated liabilities of $50M to $100M.
- Disney is the lead bidder, setting the floor for rival offers. It would pay $20M for certain undisclosed assets and receive a non-exclusive licence to unspecified intellectual property.
- Disney recently hired Character.AI's former chief executive, Karandeep Anand, as its chief technology officer, and expects some of the technical team to follow.

Disney had previously sent Character.AI cease-and-desist letters over unauthorised chatbots of its characters. Two years after Google paid more than $2 billion to hire away much of its talent, including its co-founders, the companion-chatbot pioneer is being sold off, after a run of lawsuits over the safety of its bots.

## Research & Models

- **[Anthropic: small models may be months from spreading themselves across smart TVs and fridges](https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai)**: published Monday by William Zhou and Daniel Freeman of Anthropic's Frontier Red Team. They ran a 26-billion-parameter open model across 15 networked off-brand Android TV boxes costing about $55 each, slowly, at 0.39 tokens per second, to show that cheap internet-connected devices can host a capable model. In their evaluations, a 9B model got inference running on 8 of the 9 hardware types tested. In simplified end-to-end tests, GLM-5.2 and stronger models could already complete the full replication cycle. If small models keep improving at their historical rate, the authors expect them to reach that level by the end of this year. Such a worm would have no central off-switch, though its doubling time would likely be days.
- **[Researchers show a model can hide a capability that no test can find](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928)**: posted 9 October and accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Andis Draguns, Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Raymond Douglas and Christian Schroeder de Witt build backdoors that hide an encrypted neural network inside a model's weights, which decrypts and runs only on a secret trigger. Under standard cryptographic assumptions the hidden circuit stays "provably hard to elicit and interpret," even with full access to the weights. The practical consequence: pre-deployment capability evaluations assume hidden capabilities can be drawn out, and this shows that assumption fails against a deliberate adversary.

## Product & Industry

- **[Anthropic says it has cut what distillers can harvest from Claude by 90 to 99%](https://www.anthropic.com/research/distillation-defenses)**: published Monday. "Distillation" means using a stronger model's outputs, especially its hidden reasoning, to train a cheaper copy. Anthropic's measures: "preserved thinking," which stops API accounts created on or after 31 August from editing Claude's earlier context, a trick distillers used to coax out hidden reasoning; a two-stage classifier against extraction attempts, which Anthropic says is now about as robust as its bioweapons safeguards, with a false-positive rate under 0.1%; and a block on reading newer models' reasoning through older models with weaker safeguards. It names Zhipu as having distilled US models' cyber capabilities into GLM-5.3, and repeats that requests to Moonshot's Kimi were secretly forwarded to Claude. The trade-offs are stated: Anthropic says it has turned away "meaningful revenue and trillions of tokens' worth of paying traffic," and some legitimate customers have had to change their integrations.

## Compute & Data Centers

- **[Microsoft details Maia 300, its next in-house inference chip](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/deep-dive-into-azure-maia-300-architecture/)**: published Monday. Maia 300's compute is built on TSMC's N2P process, with eight HBM4 stacks giving 288GB of memory and 18TB/s of bandwidth per accelerator. Racks hold 96 accelerators at 167kW, and superpods reach 12,288 accelerators. Microsoft claims up to seven times the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200 and more than 2.5 times the performance per watt. The post gives no deployment date. Each new generation of in-house silicon reduces Microsoft's reliance on Nvidia for serving models.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[Britain keeps early access to frontier models, but critics say its AI safety plan commits to "very little"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cr78pe09y05xo)**: in a speech on Monday, UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan said that in recent weeks the government had worked with the US "to retain access for Britain's AISI to the most powerful AI models before they are released." That access had been in doubt since September reports that the White House asked US labs to hold back models from the UK AI Security Institute. He said it was "likely, if not inevitable" that UK cyber defences would at some point be breached, and pledged to scale up two pilots that have found and fixed over 1,000 vulnerabilities in government systems. His message to labs: "if you cannot adequately control the risks, do not release the system." The Ada Lovelace Institute's Nuala Polo was unimpressed: "There is no new regulatory power, no timetable and no obligation on AI developers."

## Capital & Deals

- **[The IMF names an AI share crash as one of three big threats to financial stability](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/oct/13/ai-share-crash-risk-global-imf-debt-corrowing-cyber-attacks)**: from the IMF's Global Financial Stability Report, launched Tuesday at its annual meetings in Bangkok. It ranks an AI correction alongside rising government borrowing costs and a major cyber-attack as "salient risks." It warns that "weaker or less durable investment and profitability in AI" could trigger a fall "exacerbated by rising leverage, concentrated exposures, and circular financing arrangements," and that "the consequences of an AI correction could be global." The fund also cites "growing evidence" that AI's rollout may not need investment on the scale the hyperscalers are making.
- **[Asian chip stocks fall sharply on a "casino" remark and a Chinese memory advance](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/oct/13/chip-stocks-broadcom-ai-samsung-shares-cxmt)**: on Tuesday, South Korea's Kospi fell 6.25%, with Samsung down 7.4%, SK Hynix down 10%, Tokyo Electron down 7.15% and SoftBank Group down 8.5%. The Guardian ties the sell-off to Broadcom's chief executive likening many AI investors to "casino" gamblers, and to reports that China's CXMT has made progress on a denser DRAM design.

## Quick Hits

- [More than a fifth of the UK Home Office's AI asylum summaries failed expert checks](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/10/13/home-office-asylum-ai-flunks-one-in-five-accuracy-checks/5302649): figures from a [report by the Open Rights Group](https://www.openrightsgroup.org/app/uploads/2026/10/ChatGPT_in_Asylum_Decisions_Errors_inaccuracies_and_incompetence.pdf), obtained by freedom-of-information request. 45 of 203 GPT-4o-generated interview summaries failed initial checks after the April rollout, against 9% in the pilot. The group estimates only 0.3% of summaries were checked, and says applicants are not told when AI has been used in their cases.
