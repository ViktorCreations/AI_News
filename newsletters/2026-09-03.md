> OpenAI has rated Astra "critical" for cyber capability — the first model to hit the top tier of its own Preparedness Framework — and disclosed that the architecture making it good is also making it harder to watch. ## Top Stories

### [Astra is OpenAI's first "critical" cyber model, and its reasoning is going dark](https://the-decoder.com/openai-calls-astra-its-most-dangerous-model-yet-watching-what-it-does-is-only-getting-harder/)
The rating is the highest tier OpenAI has: a model that can find and exploit previously unknown flaws in well-protected systems without a human guiding each step. Against 20 recent V8 vulnerabilities in internal testing it found two unknown zero-days and chained them into a working exploit, on top of the perfect ExploitBench score we noted yesterday. Refusals improved sharply — 91.5% of disallowed cyber requests, against 59% for GPT-5.6 Sol. The disclosure that matters more is architectural. Astra uses recurrent depth, looping text back through its layers before producing output, which raises performance and cuts cost while pushing part of the reasoning into internal numeric representations that no human can read. OpenAI's chief scientist calls chain-of-thought monitoring "fragile" and "unfortunately trending in a negative direction." On August 18 the company made that monitoring the centrepiece of its post-breach safeguards, at roughly 20% of the inference compute being watched. It is now saying the instrument degrades as the models improve.

### [Google ships its third budget model in six weeks while its frontier models stay missing](https://the-decoder.com/gemini-3-8-flash-is-googles-third-budget-model-in-six-weeks-while-frontier-models-remain-mia/)
Gemini 3.8 Flash arrived Wednesday, three weeks after 3.7 Flash, in general and Cyber variants. The numbers are strong for the price: 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, just under Claude Opus 5's 74.0%, a 59 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index level with GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.6, and 86.2% on CyberGym for the Cyber build — at $0.75 and $3.75 per million tokens introductory, against $5/$25 for Opus 5. What is not arriving is the top of the line. Gemini 3.5 Pro, originally targeted for June, and Gemini 4 both remain unreleased with no timeline. Three Flash releases in six weeks is a real cadence; it is also what a company ships when the flagship is not ready.

## Research & Models

- **[World Labs unveils Atlas, one model for generating, reconstructing and simulating 3D scenes](https://the-decoder.com/world-labs-unveils-atlas-a-single-ai-model-that-generates-reconstructs-and-simulates-3d-worlds-from-just-a-few-photos/)** — Fei-Fei Li's company announced it Wednesday as an "omni-model" trained across text, images, video and 3D with spatial context anchoring inputs to positions in space. It emits both video — up to a minute at 1440p under camera control — and native 3D formats including point clouds and Gaussian splats, reconstructs a scene from anywhere between one and a hundred-plus photos, and does real-to-sim for robotics. Human raters preferred it in 75–94% of comparisons against MiniMax H3 and Seedance 2.5, with the lowest reconstruction error at 25.3. It is early access for selected partners, and World Labs is candid that "no single benchmark captures what Atlas can do," which is honest and also unfalsifiable.

## Regulation & Policy

- **[The Justice Department tells a court that training on copyrighted work is fair use](https://the-decoder.com/us-department-of-justice-backs-fair-use-for-ai-training-in-landmark-copyright-case/)** — the DOJ has intervened in the consolidated New York Times case against OpenAI and Microsoft in Manhattan, filing a brief arguing that training does not infringe. Its reasoning: training copies whole works but never publicly distributes them, outputs "often if not always lack substantial similarity" to the originals, and the analogy is a student studying an author's style before publishing their own work. Note how wide the ask is — "it would be problematic—and legally incorrect—to impose broad copyright liability that would generally render training of AI models impermissible." It lands days after Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic, and twelve days after eighteen groups asked the FTC to investigate destructive book scanning. Both of those turn on *acquisition* — piracy, torrenting, cutting up books — which is the ground the Bartz settlement was decided on, and which the DOJ's argument does not reach.
